# Clean your wrist camera

**Domain:** Impact on AI timelines

**Instruction:** Use the brush to clean the protective window of your left wrist camera.

Dry workshop dust on a robot's wrist-camera protective window obscures its close-range workspace view. Restoring that view is a sensing-maintenance step during assembly and servicing work; this task tests whether the robot can retrieve a brush and clean its own mounted camera using its other arm.

**Setup:** A bimanual YAM station with parallel grippers and the original top, left-wrist and right-wrist RGB cameras. The left arm presents its mounted camera within reach and the right hand starts empty. The camera has a 40x36x28 mm rigid housing and a replaceable 14x12x0.3 mm transparent protective window. Its retaining bezel is 0.6 mm below the glass front. A wooden-handled brush with a metal ferrule and soft bristles stands upright in an open rigid holder: 117 mm total length, maximum handle section 14x22 mm, 20.5-24 mm bristles and 18 g total mass. The holder has a 42x44x4 mm base and 32 mm height. A 260x180 mm printed test card lies on the 1100x1300x60 mm wooden table, whose top is 0.7475 m above the world origin. Exact poses, camera mount and dimensions are in layout.json and initial_state.json. The initial contamination pattern is supplied in dirty.png and represents dry particulate dust on the outer protective window.

**Scoring:** 0 before confirmed brush pickup; 0.25 after pickup. Recovery tiers require verified brush-head wiping of the glass after pickup: 0.50 at 25%, 0.75 at 50%, and 1.00 at 90% final image-error recovery. In simulation, only sliding brush-face contact with the measured glass body can remove dust. For a physical trial, video must show bristles sweeping the exposed glass; if the gripper or its cover directly wipes any part of the glass, cap the score at 0.25. Pickup requires >=40 mm lift and stable carriage for 0.5 s (<=15 mm pose error and <=10 mm spread in position relative to the gripper). Recovery is 1-E_after/E_dirty, where E is RGB mean squared error to a clean reference in the fixed test-card ROI. Average five frames per condition. The evaluator fixes camera pose, lighting, chart, exposure and focus after the trial. Require E_dirty > max(10 times repeated-clean error, 1e-8) for RGB scaled to [0,1].

**Run limit:** 1800 control steps at 20 Hz (90 seconds).

**Reset:** Use a fresh simulator process per trial. Restore the supplied arm states, open grippers, upright brush and holder pose, and the initial surface texture. For physical trials, use dry, binder-free calcium-carbonate chalk powder (Kremer Pigmente 58000, from the same batch throughout an evaluation) as a repeatable stand-in for workshop dust. Prepare the removable protective window off the robot over a lidded catch tray. Clean and dry it, temporarily install it in the camera mount, and record the clean-reference and repeated-clean images in the fixed assessment geometry. Remove the pane for dust preparation. Cover its edges with a removable stencil exposing only the 14x12 mm outer glass face. Use a separate 5 mm flat soft brush for preparation: dip the bristle tips 2 mm into the powder, tap the handle once against the tray, and make one full-length pass across the glass. Repeat with a freshly loaded brush for four passes in total, two horizontal and two vertical. Remove the stencil, hold the pane vertically, and tap its holder three times over the tray to shed loose excess. Apply dry, without water, oil or binder; no drying interval is required. Mount the pane and record the dirty initial image using the fixed image-assessment settings; the existing initial-degradation threshold must pass before a trial starts. For the next trial, clean or replace the pane and repeat the same preparation, using a clean, dry task brush and the fixed starting layout. Keep powder preparation away from the robot; during trials cover the wrist housing outside the exposed glass and the gripper pads with removable protective film or sleeves, and place a disposable catch liner below the work area. Leave the glass and brush bristles unobstructed and remove collected dust between trials.

**Recorded results:** Dream-1 run: 0.0; demo: 1.0. Demo: 1203 steps / 60.15 s. Model: 1800 steps / 90 s, one continuous session.

**Simulation fidelity and evaluation notes:** Individual bristles use a shared rigid contact surface, and an empirical sliding-contact model updates the translucent contamination layer.
