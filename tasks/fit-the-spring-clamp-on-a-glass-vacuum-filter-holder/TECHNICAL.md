# Glass filter holder proof lift

## Scene and load path

The empty flask, stopper and fritted base form a 0.72 kg free rigid body. The 0.20 kg funnel and 0.046 kg two-jaw clamp start loose. The model checks the funnel-to-base connection under load and assumes a secure stopper-to-flask connection. Each sprung jaw has ten pairs of upper and lower convex segments. Their V faces capture the opposing bevels. The lower assembly can rise only through physical contact. The membrane, graduations and adjacent height gauge are display geometry. The USD asset is regenerated by running `python tools/build_assets.py` from this folder.

## Controller and functional test

The left gripper closes on the cylindrical funnel body 65 mm above its bottom face, carries it squarely onto the frit and maintains the grip. The right hand squeezes the clamp handles, pushes the open jaws over both flanges, releases them, withdraws laterally and returns home. The left gripper raises the assembly toward 25 mm of measured clearance beneath the lowest flask edge. During load transfer it corrects the wrist using the flask's world tilt, keeps the funnel over the mark and turns the assembly up to 16 degrees about vertical once airborne to keep the elbow in reach. After 28 consecutive ticks at or above 22 mm it lowers the flask onto its original mark and releases. The controller stops lifting if the flanges separate by over 6 mm. The controller writes robot commands. The scorer reads the pose of each independently simulated object.

For bench scoring, align a side camera with the bench surface and the 20 mm gauge beside the flask. Record the grasp on the upper funnel, the free hand and the gap under the lowest flask edge through the entire lift. Count one second of continuous clearance, then verify the flask returns to its mark and the two hands withdraw for half a second. A single frame of clearance or an upper funnel rising away from the flask earns no proof credit. Use the setup dimensions in `task.json` and an empty, dry assembly. The one-body stopper model assumes positive retention of the lower stack; a physical surrogate must retain the equivalent lower load for this test.

In simulation the clearance is the lowest transformed vertex of either ring of the flask foot. Both pads of the lifting gripper must register force on the funnel, with the fingertip centre within 18 mm of the funnel axis and between 26 and 125 mm above its foot in funnel coordinates. Every arm link is checked for contact with the lower body and each clamp link. The other arm must also be clear of the funnel. The noise threshold for normal contact is 0.05 N. A gripper that takes over in midair cannot pass. The hold requires 20 consecutive control intervals. Disassembly invalidates earlier proof. Ten consecutive hands-off intervals after upright set-down end a successful trial. The physical evidence is in `RESULT.json`, `media/grader-trace.jsonl` and `media/side_cam.mp4`.

The kinematic dry run has a stationary holder and no contact-force measurement. It exercises the controller trajectory and cannot certify a lift. Native PhysX trials are required to measure proof performance. The scorer does not test membrane sealing, filtration or glass strength.

## Native proof-lift measurement

The seed-0 reference reached 1.0 at step 1,205 (60.25 seconds of simulated time, 275.65 seconds wall time). The lowest flask edge stayed at least 20 mm above the table for 44 consecutive control steps (2.20 seconds, steps 1,045–1,088), with both left pads on the upper funnel and the other arm clear. The peak clearance was 24.62 mm at step 1,049. At step 1,080 the left pads exerted 19.36 and 19.37 N on the funnel. The left and right jaws respectively registered 7.62 and 7.72 N on the funnel and 7.86 and 6.01 N on the lower assembly. Neither robot arm contacted the flask or clamp, and the right arm registered zero force on the funnel. The independent funnel, clamp and flask poses remained assembled through set-down. The final sample measured a level flask, 1.29 mm funnel offset and 0.66 mm clamp offset after 10 hands-off steps.
