# Glass filter holder proof lift

## Scene and load path

The empty flask, stopper and fritted base form a 0.72 kg free rigid body. The 0.20 kg funnel and 0.046 kg two-jaw clamp start loose. The model checks the funnel-to-base connection under load and assumes a secure stopper-to-flask connection. Each sprung jaw has ten pairs of upper and lower convex segments. Their V faces capture the opposing bevels. The lower assembly can rise only through physical contact. The membrane, graduations and adjacent height gauge are display geometry. The USD asset is regenerated by running `python tools/build_assets.py` from this folder.

## Controller and functional test

The left gripper closes on the cylindrical funnel body 65 mm above its bottom face, carries it squarely onto the frit and maintains the grip. The right hand squeezes the clamp handles, pushes the open jaws over both flanges, releases them, withdraws laterally and returns home. The low-lift controller raises the assembly toward 23 mm of clearance beneath the lowest flask edge, midway between the stripe and the gauge top. During load transfer it corrects the wrist using the flask's world tilt and steers the measured flask foot centre toward the fixture mark. Once airborne it turns the assembly up to 16 degrees about vertical to keep the elbow in reach. The controller holds for 28 consecutive ticks within the inclusive 20–26 mm band before lowering the flask onto its mark and releasing. The grader requires 20 consecutive ticks in the band. The frozen replay used a 6 mm positive flange-gap limit for the scripted stop. The current controller stops upward commands when the measured positive gap exceeds the scorer's 3 mm seated-gap tolerance or the flask foot rises above the gauge. The controller writes robot commands. The scorer reads the pose of each independently simulated object.

For bench scoring, use an empty, dry flask with the base and stopper securely retained. Place the flask in a stable, padded, rimmed tray that covers the lift area and leaves room for both grippers to clear the rim. Both bench credit levels require the recording to show the flask over the tray at setup and throughout any lift. With the flask resting on the pad, set the upper edge of the gauge stripe 20 mm above the resting foot. Align the side camera so the lowest flask edge and the stripe can be compared. Record the grip on the upper funnel, the free hand and the foot through the lift. Count one continuous second with the lowest foot edge at or above the stripe and at or below the 26 mm gauge top. An excursion above the gauge top at any point makes the trial score zero. Verify upright set-down and half a second with both hands clear.

Record the terminal assembly before dismantling it for inspection. With the flask safely supported, steady the funnel, squeeze the clamp handles and remove the clamp. Lift the funnel straight up without sliding it across the membrane. Record a view of the exposed membrane and frit before touching either. Check the whole face for tears or punctures and the edge all around for centring on the frit. Both 0.5 and 1.0 physical credit require this check to pass. A damaged, displaced or unverified membrane earns 0. If glass breaks, stop the trial, leave the fragments in the tray and award 0.

In simulation the clearance is the lowest transformed vertex of either ring of the flask foot. Both pads of the lifting gripper must register force on the funnel, with the fingertip centre within 18 mm of the funnel axis and between 26 and 125 mm above its foot in funnel coordinates. Every arm link is checked for contact with the lower body and each clamp link. The other arm must also be clear of the funnel. The noise threshold for normal contact is 0.05 N. The same gripper must supply sole support from the table-supported start through the hold, which requires 20 consecutive control intervals. At takeoff, set-down and through the ten consecutive hands-off intervals, the centre of the flask foot must be within 4 mm horizontally of its fixed mark. Disassembly or touchdown outside the mark tolerance invalidates earlier proof. The separate 30 mm sideways failure threshold latches zero. A foot clearance above 26 mm at any 20 Hz control sample also latches zero. The simulation evidence is in `RESULT.json`, `media/grader-trace.jsonl` and `media/side_cam.mp4`.

The kinematic dry run has a stationary holder and no contact-force measurement. It exercises the controller trajectory and cannot certify a lift. Native PhysX trials are required to measure proof performance. The scorer does not test membrane sealing, filtration or glass strength.

## Original native proof-lift measurement

The following measurements come from the original 25 mm controller target and the scorer with a 20 mm minimum. The seed-0 reference reached 1.0 at step 1,205 (60.25 seconds of simulated time, 275.65 seconds wall time). The lowest flask edge stayed at least 20 mm above the table for 44 consecutive control steps (2.20 seconds, steps 1,045–1,088), with both left pads on the upper funnel and the other arm clear. The peak clearance was 24.62 mm at step 1,049. At step 1,080 the left pads exerted 19.36 and 19.37 N on the funnel. The left and right jaws respectively registered 7.62 and 7.72 N on the funnel and 7.86 and 6.01 N on the lower assembly. Neither robot arm contacted the flask or clamp, and the right arm registered zero force on the funnel. The independent funnel, clamp and flask poses remained assembled through set-down. The final sample measured a level flask, 1.29 mm funnel offset and 0.66 mm clamp offset after 10 hands-off steps.
