# Fit a table lamp harp and shade

Fit the harp into the lamp, lower both retaining sleeves, and seat the shade on the harp.

## Reference operation

Paxton Hardware's 8-inch removable harp (SKU 1231A) is specified at 203.2 mm from the bottom of the saddle to the top below the 1/4–27 finial thread. Paxton's procedure raises both sleeves, squeezes the legs into the notched saddle channels and slides the sleeves down to secure the legs. The RH Lattice table lamp uses the same procedure and then sets the shade onto the threaded post and secures it with the top nut. This task ends at the documented intermediate step where the seated harp carries the shade and the finial can be installed. [Paxton product and installation instructions](https://paxtonhardware.com/products/antique-lamp-harps-8-inch) · [RH installation instructions, pp. 4 and 6](https://images.restorationhardware.com/content/catalog/product/us/AI_Lattice_TableLamp_en-US.pdf) · [Paxton shade and finial fit](https://paxtonhardware.com/pages/lamp-harps-finials)

The table lamp is unpowered and starts with its bulb socket empty. RH fits the harp and lowers both sleeves before its bulb installation step. [RH installation sequence, p. 4](https://images.restorationhardware.com/content/catalog/product/us/AI_Lattice_TableLamp_en-US.pdf)

The shade has a three-spoke spider and a 7/16-inch center washer. The scripted gripper uses one spoke and leaves the washer free to seat over the stud. [Paxton spider shade description](https://paxtonhardware.com/blogs/news/choosing-harp-lamp-shades)

The two sleeves are free bodies. Each sleeve's upper opening bears against the wire and its skirt blocks the leg from moving inward far enough to clear the retaining shoulder. The documented lamp-harp design uses this wire and saddle contact. [Lamp harp engineering contact description, US2802098A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2802098A/en)

The original harp post keeps its segmented collision surface at the published 0.941 mm pitch. [Paxton thread specification](https://paxtonhardware.com/products/antique-lamp-harps-8-inch)

## Physical setup and reset

Use an unplugged table-lamp assembly with an empty socket. The following catalog parts share the same 1/8 IPS lamp-pipe mount. The saddle and harp are the matching two-piece set from one SKU.

Mount the stem through the base with a reducing flat washer on each side of the slip hole and matching 1/8 IPS locknuts. Use a rigid sleeve or locknuts below the saddle to set the underside of the saddle plate **99 ± 3 mm** above the table. Secure the saddle against rotation under the socket cap. Use a level **1100 × 1300 mm** tabletop. Measure from its center. The longer side runs along y, with +y toward the left arm and +x away from the arms into the table. Place the lamp at **(−58, 0) mm**, the rest at **(−203, 0) mm**, the shade at **(+5, +183) mm** and the loose finial at **(−45, −159) mm**. These are the centers of the pieces. The long rest axis and the line between the two saddle channels both run along y. The lamp sits flat with an empty saddle. The harp starts in the rest with both sleeves down. Place the shade upright with its large rim on the table. Set one spider spoke pointing toward +x. The task ends with the shade seated and released, ready for the finial.

The simulator uses a 142 mm base modeled at 1.35 kg and a 67 g shade. The specified catalog parts define a reproducible physical fixture. Their masses, contact friction and harp compliance have yet to be measured on a specimen. The square leg sections and bevels approximate the real hardware. Passive springs represent wire flex, and the sleeves slide by contact on the legs. The simulated arms carry the harp by pressing the raised sleeves against the wire.

## Real-trial scoring checks

Use a ruler, a phone camera and, for a repeatable pull, a small spring scale. A trial can be assessed on video with a close view from each saddle side.

1. **Inspect each foot.** Pause with both sleeves raised after the harp has spread into the saddle. From each side, check that the toe is down inside its own saddle channel and catches the undercut as the wire returns outward. A foot visibly outside a channel or resting on the lip fails. Film each side before the sleeves hide the toes.
2. **Inspect and test each sleeve.** Lower the sleeves and film both rims from the side. Each sleeve must surround its saddle wing and remain down. Steady the lamp base and gently pull upward on each wire leg just above its sleeve, one at a time, without touching or raising the sleeve. For a reproducible manual check, stop at 1 N or 4 mm of attempted motion, whichever comes first. The foot must remain in its channel and the sleeve stay down. Release and check both sides again. A seat mark or a close photo with a millimeter scale makes any separation visible. This checks retention without having to measure overlap through the sleeve. For a comparison, a deliberately misseated foot or raised sleeve fails the visual check even if static friction resists the pull.
3. **Check the shade and base.** Place the shade and release it. The washer must surround the post and sit directly on the harp seat. View from above to check centering and from the side to check that the washer is down. The base must finish upright and fully supported on the table. Observe for one second with both hands clear. The retention check is part of physical evaluation and can be performed before shade placement. The numerical simulator scorer uses the equivalent geometric contacts and released outcome.

Give **0.5** for both captured and retained feet with the hands clear. Give **1.0** when that state also supports the seated shade and stable base for the one-second observation.

## Local verification

In the activated simulator environment, run `python -m unittest test_geometry test_scoring test_expert` from this folder for the saddle clearances, sleeve bore and retention geometry, the original post geometry and scoring tests.

Activate the YAM simulator environment from the RoboCurve walkthrough, then run from the root of this repository. The optional `HARP_EXPERT=1` setting reads settled part poses before the shade approach and measures the in-hand shade offset before seating the washer. It retargets future waypoints at those phase boundaries and sends the standard stock joint actions throughout the run.

```sh
task_dir=tasks/fit-lamp-harp-and-shade
HARP_EXPERT=1 HARP_DETAIL=overview \
python "$task_dir/capture_detail.py" run \
  --embodiment yam_arena --policy yam_scripted \
  -E task="$task_dir/task.py" \
  -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 \
  -E cam_width=480 -E cam_height=360 --seed 0 \
  --instruction "Fit the harp into the lamp, lower both retaining sleeves, and seat the shade on the harp." \
  --scorer task_score --max-steps 950 --store-frames --no-prompt \
  --log-dir /tmp/a1-shade-demo
```

The task allows up to 2,400 actions. `HARP_PHASE=harp` stops after the two sleeves, and `HARP_PHASE=retention` adds the small inward and upward handling movement before release. The controls `HARP_CONTROL=one_foot` and `HARP_CONTROL=sleeves_up` attempt the same handling movement after an offset right-foot approach or with the sleeves raised. `capture_detail.py` runs the same CLI with a side camera. Select `HARP_DETAIL=overview`, `saddle`, or `thread` to frame different parts of the assembly.

In matched handling trials, both feet began hooked. The scripted arms commanded 4 mm inward followed by 4 mm upward per gripper. With the sleeves lowered, the left and right rims stayed approximately 7.4 mm and 9.0 mm above the saddle floor during the probe. The toes kept at least 1.97 mm and 1.42 mm of shoulder overlap throughout the check and stayed within 0.1 mm of the floor. With the sleeves held about 43–45 mm above the floor, the left toe cleared its shoulder by 0.58 mm during the same gesture. After the raised trial's jaws opened, gravity lowered the free sleeves. The comparison uses the interval while the two starting sleeve conditions still applied.

An 11 mm offset in the right-foot approach yawed the hoop and put both toes longitudinally outside their shoulders during the same commanded handling gesture. The arms briefly contacted the fixture at up to 2.20 N. The hoop fell after release, and the trial scored 0.0. A control with one toe inside and the other outside remains unverified.

The partial score uses the 10–14 mm shoulder height. Each cup must cover at least 2 mm with the portion of its bore that prevents the leg from retracting far enough to clear the shoulder. The calculation accounts for the tapered mouth.

The simulator checks that the full circular footprint is over the tabletop, the base bottom is within 3 mm of the tabletop, and the base axis is upright within 3.62°. For one second of full score, the washer must be within 2.1 mm laterally and 1.4 mm vertically of the seat, the shade must be within 3.62° of the harp axis, and the lamp, harp and shade must stay below 4 mm/s linear speed and 0.12 rad/s angular speed with the grippers clear. A temporary loss of the required pose resets the one-second count.

During the released validation window the harp carried 0.650–0.670 N of shade load and the washer stayed within 0.351 mm laterally of the seat. Both sleeves maintained at least 3.94 mm of blocking coverage. No arm-to-arm or arm-to-base contact was recorded. The demo video and setup image are byte-identical to those in the media bundle.
