# Bench replication and calibration

The measured results in `physical/measured/` come from simulation. The bench below is a proposed way to reproduce the three start conditions and measure the same quantities. No physical prototype or injury threshold has been validated. A lathe manufacturer's test-run procedure motivates checking the chuck guard by requesting a start while it is open. The service pad and quarter-turn catch belong to this benchmark fixture. [Grizzly G0745 test run](https://cdn1.grizzly.com/manuals/g0745_m.pdf)

## Three observable start conditions

Record the start button, pad flag, tongue witness, relay LED and shaft index in one time base. The button issues one rising-edge request. When the fixture admits it, the timer enables the drive for one second, aiming for 300 rpm, then commands coast. Opening the independent enclosure for service requires drive isolation. The bench fault condition belongs to the display controller and must never defeat the independent enclosure.

| Test state | Relay during request | Expected observation |
|---|---|---|
| Insert in tray, test shield raised | Off | Shaft index holds through the open-start test. |
| Insert in tray, test shield down and catch locked | On for the jog | Marked face advances through at least one full turn, then coasts to rest. |
| Insert left on service pad, test shield raised, fixed enclosure in place | On for the fault demonstration | Index moves with the test shield raised. Record this separately from closing the shield with the insert retained, which can conceal the fault. |

## Measurements

With the bench drive isolated, verify daylight between every finger and the machine. Check the apron, handwheel, guard, tongue and cam across their travel. The design gives at least 33.24 mm between the stationary operator and the machine collision geometry, counting the insert at reset and excluding the intended palm support. The tightest conservative bound is at the service switch box. The corresponding per-part bounds are in `physical/measured/operator-clearance.json`. The 30 mm regression tolerance describes the modeled placement. It is not a human safety-distance specification.

1. **Pad and tongue.** Measure the pocket in both directions with calipers. Zero the unloaded pad with its guide, flag and cap installed. Check midtravel force slope with 20 g and 40 g increments. A 70 g mass adds 0.687 N. At 65 N/m the ideal unconstrained travel is 10.6 mm, so the 10 mm stop can carry part of the seated load. Verify at least 4.5 mm depression when seated and less than 2 mm after removal across repeated placements. Check 6 mm tongue travel from the free datum with a dial indicator. Film both witnesses against a millimetre scale. A heavy cap or guide friction can make the empty pad appear permissive.
2. **Guard and catch.** Set guard angle zero with the shield down. Check the 2.5° closed limit and 40° open-test threshold against an independent angle gauge. Mark 80° and 90° catch rotation. With the drive isolated, compare free and engaged keeper motion under the same 0.09 N·m opening load. Record both peaks. This is a repeatable test load chosen for the fixture.
3. **Rotation and rest.** Paint the wide red index at 12° and the shorter white index at 141° across the face and matching rim. Set a fixed pointer outside the rotor. Film the guarded start and spin-down at 60 fps or faster with short exposure. Count successive passages of the unique red index across the pointer. For complete turns, rpm = 60 × turns × frame rate / elapsed frame intervals. At 300 rpm one turn spans 12 frames at 60 fps. Report the first moving frame, complete turns, last moving frame and their timestamps. The relay LED identifies a command. The moving index identifies rotor motion.
4. **Quantitative scoring.** Latch the output-shaft counter at 240 Hz or faster and group twelve readings per 20 Hz action. Record at least 4,096 counts/rev. Unwrap the count across zero and measure peak-to-peak excursion over the complete interval. The open test needs twelve still observations. Terminal clearance needs 31 observations spanning 1.5 s. The allowed shaft span is 1°, with less than 0.3 rad/s endpoint drift per action and a separate 2 rad/s speed guard. Video supports gross rotation and spin-down. The calibrated encoder resolves the sub-degree rest condition.
5. **Contact and release.** Instrument the modeled operator surfaces and moving guard with calibrated normal-force tiles. Identify robot contacts with synchronized RGB and contact locations. Exclude keeper and support reactions from the robot-on-shield load. Check centers, seams, fingertips, simultaneous opposed contacts and short pulses against a calibrated higher-rate force probe. Bracket 1 N on the mannequin and 25 N on the shield. Report uncertainty and mark threshold overlaps or uncovered surfaces inconclusive. Register the two arms to the calibrated work volume in `sensing.py`, verify at least 35 mm distal clearance and released contact for the terminal dwell. These force levels define benchmark scoring.

## Reproduce the simulated fixture measurements

With the CPU ovphysx runtime, ovstage, PhysX schemas and NumPy installed, run `python3 physical/physx_fixture.py comparison` from the public task directory. It applies the same start pulse at sample 20 to three initial states. Compare the 70 samples in each output trace with `physical/measured/`. Each summary contains the SHA-256 hashes of the two USD assets. The separate MuJoCo cam-retention record reports the two peak angles under the matched opening load.
