# Assemble the water-trainer station

Build one disconnected apparatus with a movable water outlet and an inert headform. The operator sets the height of the head support before the robot starts. The water source moves with that support. A separate fixed support carries the mast, rack and guide. The assembly preserves the geometry the robot touches in the submitted scene. The supply tank, plumbing downstream of the air break, and weighing vessel implement the simulated water gauge on the bench.

**Evidence status.** Dimensions below are source dimensions or specified fabrication targets. The reach report contains computed robot kinematics. Each physical measurement sheet is blank until a builder commissions an actual unit.

## Establish the datum

Use [STATION_LAYOUT.svg](STATION_LAYOUT.svg), [BOM.csv](BOM.csv) and the stock two-arm YAM station. The layout uses millimetres. Origin XY lies halfway between the arm base axes. Both base frames face +X, left at +Y, with +Z upward. World z=747.5 is the work surface, z=850 is the survey datum. Subtract **747.5** from a world height to obtain a ruler reading above the work surface. The source floor is at z=−12.5, giving a 760 mm floor-to-worktop height. During physical installation, register to the work surface and surveyed bases first.

## Assemble and survey

1. Lock the standard bench and retain the installed YAM base hardware. Mark the X and Y centerlines using a square and an ordinary rule. Survey center spacing to 610 ±1 mm and frame yaw to 0 ±0.5° against a 200 mm straightedge. Preserve the base-frame height. Shim the apparatus or survey the bench height against its own tabletop if floor levels vary.
2. Fit the removable polypropylene pan to an edge-supported board. Its clear inside spans X 260–910, Y −315–455 and the rim is 20 mm above the pan floor. Any pan wall overhang at the far edge is supported by the floor-bracket assembly. Keep the tray watertight. Place wet fittings, the outlet and the weighing vessel inside this outline. With a dry test assembly separate from robot power, the tray must retain at least 750 mL with the stands in place. Remove the test water.
3. Build the independent floor support. Secure the 160 × 170 foot against rotation and tip using the purchased post's base fasteners and a clamp to the fixed bench chassis. Set the 26 mm post on the foot at (1,010,−140). Use two 25 × 20 × 2 rectangular aluminum tubes with 20 mm depth along Z, joined side by side with cross strips at their ends to span the bracket outline shown. Place the far cross strip on the post with its center at z=780. Locate the bracket using two padded edge clamps to the stationary table side rail at X≈910, separated by at least 80 mm along Y. Use spacers over the tray lip and keep fasteners out of the pan. Secure the near cross strip to the split mast block with two M6 bolts and its mating backing strip. Survey the mast to (410,0) and plumb within 0.5 mm over 180 mm of travel. The mast assembly takes its loads into the fixed frame. Apply the proof loads in [MECHANICAL_CALIBRATION.md](MECHANICAL_CALIBRATION.md) before robot motion.
4. Make the bed platform from sealed plywood or closed-cell laminated stock. Use four threaded feet or spacer stacks under its surveyed foot points. Use two padded hold-downs hooked over the stationary far table rail and a rigid crossbar secured to the rear bed feet. One clamp on each side prevents twisting. Keep the hold-downs outboard of the mannequin plumbing and robot work volume. Fit the heights in [RESET.md](RESET.md). Shim until the platform top varies by at most 1 mm at its four corners. Moving this assembly during setup must leave the fixed mast stationary within 1 mm.
5. Make the inert silhouette to the source dimensions in the table. Fix a 235 × 210 × 28 foam pillow at (543,218,ear−73). Shape the head, neck and torso from closed-cell foam against the `patient` shapes in [raised.usda](../assets/raised.usda), then attach to the platform. Add the ear profile 34 × 21 × 52 centered (483,161,ear+3) with the 11 × 9 × 12 tragus witness centered (478,152,ear). Fit the green level sight to transfer this height to the white centerline at (352,114,ear). Use a 23 mm wide, 20 mm high green band centered on the line, printed in matte ink. A plumb ruler and level must transfer the sight height to within 1 mm. Mount the magenta pointer center at (353,91,zero). The coplanar front faces minimize parallax.
6. Fit the source shelf behind the +Y side of the bed. At X=650 and 750, bolt one 90 × 30 × 3 steel outrigger under the platform using two M5 through bolts with large washers, at Y=280 and 307. Its +Y end projects to Y=365. Bolt one vertical 20 × 20 × 240 aluminum angle upright to each outrigger with a 30 × 30 angle cleat, center Y=355. Its inner face clears the foam torso at all bed heights. Mount the two slotted shelf brackets supplied with the water assembly to these uprights at matching heights and set the 120 × 120 shelf level at (700,385). With the scale and cradle fitted, set the inside tank floor to approximately ear+125. Fine-adjust the vent to the measured effective ear+150 per the water procedure. The nominal public inside floor is z=1185. The 213 mm height from the public bed top to the inside tank floor includes scale, cradle, tank base and shelf stack. Secure the slotted fittings and recheck at each bed height. Restrain the tank entirely on its weighed cradle. Load-test the assembled shelf with a 1.25 kg dummy plus the scale's own mass for 30 s and verify ≤1 mm change in the source reference plane and no bed movement. Lower the centerline by changing all four bed posts together when setting the descending case.
7. Fabricate the moving and fixed mechanism, assemble the stop-controlled tap and set its indexing parts using [MECHANICAL_BUILD.md](MECHANICAL_BUILD.md). Fit the dry assembly to the surveyed mast. Fit the carriage outlet, moving line slack and independent tank per [WATER_CALIBRATION.md](WATER_CALIBRATION.md). Secure the 500 mL maximum charge behind the dummy, attached to its support. Use the printed card, ruler, and references from the submitted setup before filming.
8. Measure dry mass and force, then perform retention, water and reset checks. Validate loaded robot clearance at reduced speed before the timed episode. See [SAFE_WATER_TRIAL.md](SAFE_WATER_TRIAL.md), [RESET.md](RESET.md), and [GRADING.md](GRADING.md). Record instrument IDs, measured values, images and video identifiers with each build.

## Camera registration and reach

Fit the stock gripper and use the task's overview and wrist frames. The overview optical center is (−70,−40,1,310), pointed at (435,53,1,035) with world Z as up. The left and right wrist camera centers in their respective gripper frames are (0,85,70) and approximately (0.23,−88.91,31.03). Each aims at local (0,0,146). Preserve the roll from [cameras.py](../evd/cameras.py). The policy uses 640 × 360 images. Check the order, sights, spring tab, tap and water witness in a recorded dry run. Place separate observer cameras where they resolve the scale, position stops and swept volume throughout the run. Their views are for measurement.

The [kinematic check](check_reach.py) runs the submitted controller's inverse kinematics for its actual YAM joint limits and approach orientations. With the source-compatible YAM URDF, the four plans solved 351 poses. In the public raised plan the left/right base-to-TCP maxima at keyframes were 518.85/531.61 mm with worst residual 0.074 mm. Across the four plans the largest was 548.37 mm and the tightest joint-limit margin was 1.916° on the descending right arm. The last public right-arm keyframe occurs at 24.03 s. These are calculated waypoints. Compliance, hose drag, jaw friction, clearance and flex of a built unit require loaded trials.

From an active YAM Python environment, in the task folder:

```bash
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python physical/check_reach.py
```

## Materials allowance

The 36 BOM entries total approximately US $374. The station and support account for $138, the carriage and tap $94, and the water circuit and its listed metrology $142. These are planning allowances, not supplier quotations. They assume access to the stock robot station, standard hand tools, and the shop equipment listed in the mechanical build. Machining labor, outside fabrication, taxes and freight are additional. The metered plug is the most demanding custom part. Its wet transfer curve must be measured after finishing.
