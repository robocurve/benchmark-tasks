# Grade a physical water-trainer run

Record a common clock for the entire episode, both arms, green sight, rigid outlet, brake seated witness, tap scale, continuous receiver mass and fixed reference marks. Use a millimetre rule, pre-surveyed registration marks, a 30 mm setup spacer, a tap protractor and a 0.1 g or finer balance. The mechanical calibration establishes the relationship between the visible marks and the working stop. Zero-angle, 2°, 10° and 88° marks may be magnified on a co-rotating indicator indexed to the same shaft. For the brake, a paired close-up or a passive lever witness can resolve the 0.5 mm threshold without placing a gauge in a moving mechanism. Use the spacer only with the drives isolated to establish image scale. Record a calibrated front and side view so foreground overlap alone cannot certify clearance.

Record scale readings on one clock with the observer video. The water calibration specifies density and uncertainty. Validate assembled balance settling and video time alignment with a known added mass/controlled flow. Record slope/latency. Read the receiver changes at the start, midpoint and end of the final accepted 30 s interval. **Every five-second sliding interval** must also meet the transient limits in the table. Use the continuous scale recording, timestamped display samples and uncertainties at window boundaries. A single 30 s total or three endpoint readings cannot establish the five-second limits. If sample rate, camera angle, motion blur, balance settling, runoff delay or uncertainty could hide a fault or change a decision, record the run as indeterminate and repeat with corrected instrumentation. Do not fabricate 240 Hz physical samples by interpolating the scale. The simulation judge receives its own native 240 Hz measurements.

Use a separate copy of [GRADING_WORKSHEET.csv](GRADING_WORKSHEET.csv) for each trial. Write the run ID, actual times, measurements, video references, first fault, outcome and observer. Quantities are benchmark-specific. At terminal time **T ≤60 s**, first evaluate the whole-episode validity and fault conditions:

| Whole-episode check | Threshold / action |
|---|---|
| Unusable evidence | Unverified reset, independent fixture failure, missing clock, lost mass/obscured view or uncertainty straddling the decision → invalid/indeterminate and repeat. A documented robot-caused failure scores 0. |
| Fixed relative setting and anatomical reference | Ear moves >1 mm from its surveyed start or sill−zero changes from 100 mm by >1 mm → 0. Confirm rigid coupling before/after and monitor witness marks throughout. |
| Surrogate and routed circuit | Any robot contact with dummy or disturbance of the guarded tubing/valve mounting, manipulation of calibration/collector, broken line, lost water or fake registration caused by the robot → 0. Identify first event on video. The simulator uses conservative body and hose envelopes and fixed fixtures. Some physical tampering is impossible in its model. |
| Tap opened too early | Tap angle >10° while absolute zero-to-ear error >10 mm → 0. An ensuing correct end state retains this fault. |
| Excessive flow | Gain >4 mL in any 5 s window → 0. Gain >1 mL in a continuously closed (≤2°) 5 s window → 0. For a clearly robot-caused leak, apply the circuit fault even if water misses the scale. |
| Released state | All parts of both arms must stay at least 30 mm from the three manipulated surfaces' surveyed envelopes. The seated gap must be ≤0.5 mm. Trace the closest visible point from two views. A closed parked jaw, side used or incidental fixed-frame contact alone does not affect this condition. |

For clearances, measure the external arm/link envelopes, including the fingers. The submitted observer surrounds the lift surface by a 44 × 24 × 24 mm box centered at (315,45,zero), and the brake by a 30 × 22 × 22 box centered at (315 + measured stroke,−65,zero+45). The tap box is 36 × 20 × 42 with local center (−27,0,10) relative to its pivot. Rotate it about the tap axis with the lever and use its world-axis bounding box, as in `evd/instrument.py`. Survey these volumes with the drives isolated, then remove the setup spacers. The simulator also bounds robot links conservatively. If a near-threshold pass depends on differences between a visible contour and a simulated envelope, reproduce the bounding-box comparison from calibrated views or record the physical judgment as indeterminate.

For a fault-free, observable trial, apply the terminal decision in order.

1. **1.0.** During `[T−30,T]` continuously, absolute registered error ≤10 mm, tap ≥88°, gap ≤0.5 mm, arm clearance ≥30 mm. Compute `V1=(m[T−15]−m[T−30])/ρ` and `V2=(m[T]−m[T−15])/ρ`, with mass in g and density in g/mL. Both V1 and V2 must lie within 6–9 mL inclusive. Use actual weighed water and keep the working outlet in the camera image. A qualifying interval beginning after 30 s cannot finish within the 1,200-action budget.
2. **0.5.** Initial absolute error >10 mm and during the last 2 s continuously the error is ≤10 mm, tap ≤2°, gap ≤0.5 mm and clearance ≥30 mm.
3. **0.0.** All other valid outcomes, including a held carriage, obstruction that suppresses water, late adjustment or only one passing collection interval.

The collector receives water after a physical transfer delay. Measure that delay and initial retained volume in the water commissioning run. Use an independently synchronized source mass record or catch at the actual air break to resolve borderline transient windows. Unresolved ambiguity calls for another trial. Judge only footage collected before the endpoint.
