# Spring carriage and working tap

This is a fabrication specification for the disconnected water trainer. The CAD was constructed from `assets/raised.usda`. The three robot contact faces, spring stroke, shelf indexing, shaft axis and outlet elevation follow that source. The source idealizes the mast joint as a perfect slide and represents the backing panel by one solid box. The physical build uses a miniature guide, a clearance lane through the panel and two front bridges that tie its sides together. The rear structure and valve interior have to occupy real space. They are newly designed parts.

All dimensions below are millimetres. CAD coordinates use station X and Y. Moving parts have local Z=0 at the magenta zero. The fixed rack STEP uses world Z. Transfer parts into an assembly using their saved coordinate systems. Add the current world zero to the moving parts only. At the public reset use 940 nominal. Drawings show dimensions at actual grip surfaces. Do not substitute a handle on another face.

## Make the carriage

1. Use `cad/carriage.step` for a 215 wide × 200 high × 9 thick board. For a low-cost build, cut two faces from sealed 9 mm birch plywood and bond/screw the two front bridges using the STEP as a drilling and routing template. Place the inner faces of both bridges at x379.5 and their thicker end pads against the panels. The front of the rack starts at x380. Use countersunk or rear-inserted fixings outside the clearance lane. Chamfer exposed edges 0.3 max, seal ply edges and recheck dimensions after sealing. For a one-piece routed board start with ≥16 mm stock. The original backboard has mass assigned to the entire carriage body. Weigh the finished build.
2. Place a 25 mm square × 410 aluminum mast centered at (410,0), starting z761. For square stock cut a 22.0 mm wide, 2.0 mm deep rebate centered on y0 over at least z875–1125. The rail bed is x399.5, the pocket walls are y±11, and the 20 mm guide truck has 1 mm nominal side clearance on each side. Cut to the outside of those limits if a purchased end cap is wider, leaving ≥0.5 mm per side over all moving parts. The MGN09C underside clearance H1 is 2 mm. A rebate just wide enough for the 9 mm rail leaves the 2 mm tall mast shoulders in the truck path. For Ø25 round stock mill a full chord 2.0 mm deep at the same x399.5 datum (the chord width is 13.56 mm). The round surface continues away from the truck outside that chord. Check the actual truck and end-cap clearance along the full stroke. See [Guide seat cross-section](GUIDE_SEAT.svg). Make a z880–1120 rail from MGN09R stock. Select a 240 mm cut with 20 mm hole pitch, or transfer-mark its actual factory holes before tapping M3 at least 6 deep. Center the rail on y0, head screws below the bearing path. Use rail screws per the supplier. Use the one-block MGN09C with 10 mm installed height, 20 mm width, body length 28.9 mm, 15 × 10 mm hole centers, four M3 threads with 3 mm nominal depth. Shim/face the rail seat if the measured installed height differs, keeping the truck face x389.5. Fasten the flat back of the board to the block, selecting M3 screws and shims for 2.5–2.8 mm actual thread reach into the block. No screw may bottom or contact the recirculating guide. Reference [HIWIN miniature guideway dimensional table](https://hiwin.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/Linear-Guideways.pdf).
3. Fit two fixed end-stop blocks bearing against the *metal body/rigid stop pad* of the truck. Use 22 × 12 × 6 aluminum pads secured to the mast on both sides of the guide with M3 bolts and 0.5 mm fiber shims, keeping the bearing track itself clear. With a 28.9 mm truck whose ends take the pad, the opposing contact planes are z895.55 and 1104.45; if end caps project farther add two rigid carriage lugs and set stop faces against them using a height gauge. The measured pointer limits must be 910 and 1090. Transfer the captive block from its shipping keeper directly onto the installed rail; fit the two stops before moving the block away from the supported transfer position. Move it by hand and verify that each stop limits the board while the truck is still fully on the rail.
4. Mill or waterjet `rack.step` from 6 mm 6061-T6 plate. File the tooth top faces level, remove burrs, and keep root radii ≤0.4. Inspect the first and last tooth against a common datum. Coordinates of the 18 teeth are x383, y−65, z933.75+12n, 0≤n≤17. Their dimensions are 6 × 18 × 3. Attach the spine to the mast with two rear ties at z927.75 and 1143.75. Per tie, cut one 6061 crossbar 95 × 12 × 6 (along Y, Z, X). Its attachment centers are y−79 and y0. Set front face x428, rear x434, ends y−86.5 to +8.5. Drill Ø3.2 through X at both centers. Fit a 42 mm long OD8 spacer between x386 and x428 at y−79. Use an M3 × 60 countersunk screw through rack, spacer and crossbar, a washer and prevailing nut. Seat rack screw flush. Shim between the mast rear and crossbar, then screw/tap the solid mast rear for M3 ≥7 mm engagement. Square the rack and torque securely; make the rack parallel to the guide within 0.3 over its working stroke. The CAD provides 0.5 mm between the front rack face at x380.0 and each spanning bridge at x379.5. With the wet carriage supported, the pin retracted and hoses in trial position, a 0.20 mm feeler must pass freely at each bridge/rack overlap during a slow manual sweep through z910–1090. Check again after removing the retention proof weight. If it catches, remove burrs or finish only the rear face of the affected spanning bridge to recover clearance; keep ≥4.0 mm finished bridge thickness and leave its end pads, rack, mast and robot contact datums in place. If warping exceeds that allowance, replace the bridge. Record the smallest measured clearance. Use a directly through-bolted sleeve with a crush spacer if using hollow mast stock.
5. Mount `lift_neck.step` from the rear through the (y45,z0) hole with M3 screw and a keyed adhesive/bedded interface. Jig the teal contact faces at x300–330, y34–56, z±9. The integrated 12×12 stem carries the load to the board. Add the zero witness (353,91,0) and outlet lip/witness (372,50,+100). Fix the pointer through a rigid board bracket. Set the outlet lip to the measured pointer offset with a depth gauge. The open spill chute passes through the 31 × 29 window, y33–64, z+57–86, with M3 mount holes at (25,68), (72,68), (32,104), (69,104). Route the water circuit as shown in the hydraulic drawing. Provide a strain-relieved loose loop over the complete 180 mm of travel.

## Assemble and set the brake

The steel tongue bears on the top of a rack tooth. The tab, U-shaped yoke and tongue translate together. The robot approaches from smaller X and presses the existing tab **toward +X**. At 8 mm positive stroke the tongue has passed the rear of the tooth. A force pushing the tab only in Z can lift a supported assembly but cannot clear a shelf.

1. Laser or waterjet the single flat 3.00 mm 304 stainless `locking_yoke.step` on its XY outline. Finish the sliding strip to 6.00±0.05 wide and 3.00±0.04 thick, stone the load-bearing tongue flat, and tap both tap-drill pilot holes M2. Cut the single-piece slotted PETG or acetal tab from `brake_tab.step`. Slide its open edge over the tab stem and fasten with two M2 countersunk/low-head screws of appropriate length. Head/body must remain inside the drawn 28 × 20 × 20 grip envelope. Machine or print the `pin_housing` and `pin_housing_cap` in acetal/solid PETG, finish the slot with a 3.2 mm shim/file, tap indicated holes M2 and M3. Use solid perimeters in screw regions; tapping a hole into sparse infill is unsuitable.
2. Bolt the housing to the rear-accessible board holes at (−46,+32) and (−29,+32) using M3. Lay the yoke in the slot so its right-return leg is behind the comb teeth. Fit the cap with two M2 low heads into the two vertical holes. The slot is 6.24 wide × 3.24 high; the lip retains the flat strip. Return and full-press stops bracket the yoke shoe. Thin shim foil at the stop faces may correct process error, while preserving the 8 mm stroke and registered tab.
3. Epoxy a 0.80 mm thick stainless Ø8 spring seat washer, bore ≤3.0, flat against the moving shoe at x338. Position its axis at y−28, z+45. Press an OD3 smooth guide pin into the fixed seat so that its supported tip projects 9 mm from x356.8 toward −X. The movable washer clears the guide at full stroke. Fit a closed-end compression spring, target 20.0 free length, OD ≤8.0, ID ≥3.5, solid height ≤9.0 and rate 0.55 N/mm (±10% acceptance). Specify by working forces or measure stock to select. Example winding estimate: ~0.62 mm music wire, mean diameter ~6.5, ~9.7 active turns; the measured working curve controls selection. Use stainless spring stock if repeatedly exposed to splashes. Fixed and moving seats must be parallel. At seated/full stroke the seat separation is 18.0/10.0. The 2 mm preload gives 1.10 N seated and 5.50 N fully pressed before friction. Fit a witness line across the fixed bracket and yoke at complete return, and a second line at +8. A 0.5 mm feeler against the stop distinguishes marginal engagement.
4. Place the supported board slightly above a shelf, release the tab and lower slowly onto the tongue. With the tongue seated x380.5–387.5, it overlaps the rack in X by 5.5 and in Y by 15. At full press its front face is x388.5 with 2.5 clearance. Fully supported, lower to a tooth by hand; an unsupported board can drop. The pointer rests at 891.75+12n. Public stale/target rests are 939.75 and 1059.75. Shifted stale/target rests are 963.75 and 1083.75. Each is 0.25 mm below the nominal label.

## Build the rotary water tap

The task calls for a working external quarter-turn tap. The trainer uses a washable, nonclinical shop-made rotary plug. It has two hose tails, a silicone liner, a crossed acetal plug and a dry detent. Its external control center and exposed surfaces come from the USD. The short portion of the source's square shaft hidden inside the housing becomes round to provide a water seal. Full- and part-open hydraulic behavior requires wet commissioning.

1. Machine the cartridge body from acetal or PETG with the two clamp flanges and dry stop quadrant as in `tap_body.step`. It can be cut in subpieces, dowelled and bonded using the STEP as an assembly jig, or printed and finish-reamed. Finish the sleeve bore to Ø12.00±0.03. Install a smooth 18 mm piece of silicone, Shore A 45–55, Ø12 OD × 8 ID. Punch the opposing Ø3.0 water windows while supported on a disposable mandrel, centered at x368. Dry assemble and index the sleeve using the two matching body ports. Capture it with a small dab of neutral-cure silicone on its **outer** surface at each end; wet-test after cure. Use straight nonmedical 5 mm OD metal/polymer tube tails with ≥3 mm bore, inserted to the outside of the liner and sealed with two-part water-resistant adhesive compatible with the body. Acetal does not bond reliably without treatment; use mechanically captured tube tails or water-compatible 5 mm compression fittings with matching manufacturer-specified threads if selecting acetal. The STEP models plain Ø5 sockets. The directly buildable bonded version uses the PETG body with prepared bonding surfaces. Expose 10–12 mm outside the body for 4×6 silicone hose; bead the ends and secure with small spring clips. Keep adhesive clear of the bores.
2. Machine the wet plug from smooth Ø9 or larger acetal stock. Finish the wet region x357–378 to Ø8.15±0.03; polish to smooth slip in the wetted liner without spiral tool marks. Drill the Ø3.20 main diametral port at x368 along Z in the **closed** position. Index the blank at 45° and drill an initial Ø0.50 diametral pilot in the +Y,+Z direction. With a simple angular indexing jig or CNC, dress the two tapered surface grooves around opposite OD lands, between angular stations 34° and 72°, measured from ±Y toward ±Z in the closed plug. The CAD cutter envelope lies 0.055 mm outside the 4.075 mm plug radius. At the material surface the V-groove width therefore increases from approximately 0.12 to 0.34 mm, while actual radial depth increases from 0.08 to 0.18 mm. For an indexed manual setup cradle the plug parallel to a drill-press table in a V-block, fit a marked disk to its dry end, and index it every 2°; the 9 mm dry square keys the 0° datum. Make the two diametral holes using sharp Ø0.50 and Ø3.20 drills. For each surface channel, fixture a fine V-engraver with a 70° included angle, touch off on a spare blank, and set radial penetration to 0.08 at the 34° start, increasing uniformly to 0.18 at 72°. Broaden the upper flanks with small symmetric axial finishing passes to reach the specified mouth widths. Blend the stations into one continuous polished groove on each side. Inspect the axial mouth width at ×10 or higher against a calibrated 0.1 mm reticle or USB-microscope scale. Wet commissioning controls acceptance of the finished part. Grooves join the pilot and main windows; retain the uncut near-closed land. Deburr under magnification. Record the final measured pilot diameter and wet calibration. A 0.50 pilot is deliberately undersized; it is a machining start size. If a calibrated part is measured too open, replace the plug.
3. The dry handle may be fabricated separately from the wet plug. Key the x325–358 portion on a 9 mm square, or use two 2 mm cross-dowels joining a matching rebated dry extension, keeping all joints clear of the liner. Bond/pin grip parts in a jig to `tap_rotor.step`. Set the loose cam radially between the stationary quadrant and front plunger ring, slide the plug/shaft from the front through the cam and liner, then cross-pin the dry cam `tap_crank.step` to the square. Its sliding Ø2 dowel projects 3.4 mm rearward from x352 into the quarter-circle slot. Bond the dowel in the hub without sticking it to the stationary quadrant. Install a standard adjustable M5 steel spring-ball plunger coaxial with X, center (y−85,z−39.5) in the stationary front annulus. Tap the 4.2 mm bore M5×0.8. Use a long-nose version or threaded spacer to give 1.6 mm minimum ball travel and reach the cam face; lock nut after setting. The ball climbs the ramp towards 45° and rolls down to either endpoint. Commission breakaway angle, torque and return in both directions. The simulated detent uses a 0.035 Nm capped drive, switches beyond 60°/below 30°, and has model friction. The physical plunger is a quasistatic analogue with measurable end states.
4. Bolt the valve via the board's (−99,−60) and (−99,−39) holes. At x350 use the 1.6 mm cross-hole and Ø1.6 × 25 roll pin through crank and shaft, flush within the rotation envelope. On the dry rear quadrant face, slip an OD18 × ID13 thin thrust shim over the shaft during insertion. Insert a Ø2 × 17 transverse dowel in the shaft at x356.95; retain it with shaft adhesive. It bears through that thrust shim on the rear face x355.6. Shim between crank and quadrant with OD17, ID13 washers (within radius 8.5, inside the stop slot) before inserting the crank pin. Select ~0.35–0.45 mm front and ~0.20–0.30 mm rear shim thickness to trim axial play. The dowel and shim remain forward of the wet cartridge. Check body-housed shaft end float ≤0.4. At closed, the knob center is (315,−85,−30). At open it is (315,−65,−50). The 20 mm offset lever rotates −90° about station X; verify its hard stops against a square and trim with thin shim tape if needed. Paint closed/open witnesses. Test full stroke, supported valve and brake access, then calibrate on water.

## Mass and cost

The simulator gives the carriage body 400 g, the brake 45 g, and the tap 25 g separately. The raised assembly's nominal total is **470 g**. A 400 g finished-total interpretation understates the lifted load. Weigh the carriage body with the wet moving tubing, guide block and fixed valve housing. Weigh the removable moving brake and rotor separately; tare slack tubing at midtravel. A nominal CAD volume check gives the board about 322 cm³ after hydraulic service cutouts, about 193 g in 0.60 g/cm³ birch. A solid PETG board at the same volume would exceed 400 g alone. The lift, pin housing/cap and valve housing add roughly 24, 22 and 21 g at 1.27 g/cm³. Allow about 16 g for a guide truck and measure actual hoses, water, fasteners and brackets. Use bolted rear ballast washers away from the rack to approach the target without changing the grips. The 304 yoke at 2.93 cm³ is about 23 g, and the solid PETG tab about 14 g. Acetal rotor at 8.36 cm³ is about 12 g. Trim the brake and rotor with captive washers; align the rotating balance weight on the shaft to avoid creating a gravity-return lever. These estimates are design calculations, not bench measurements.

A shop with a printer, drill press, taps, hand tools and a small lathe (or access to a makerspace) can use the drawings and interchange solids. Flat blanks can be subcontracted to a waterjet service. The allowance includes the two water scales and fluid metrology items under W10–W11. Shared survey, mechanical inspection and shop equipment under S10/M12, the robots, CNC time and finishing labor are excluded. Access to the indexed fixture, micro-drills, fine engraver and magnified inspection is part of the shop assumption. The made parts, price allowances and force targets do not imply that a physical specimen has been built.
