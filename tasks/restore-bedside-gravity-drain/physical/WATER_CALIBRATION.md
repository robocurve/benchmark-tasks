# Build and commission the water circuit

These are fabrication dimensions and commissioning targets for an isolated tap-water trainer. No as-built measurements have been supplied. Use ordinary water fittings and an inert mannequin. No patient or clinical equipment connects to the rig.

## Working points from the submitted task

Use world heights in mm. The effective source is 150 mm above the mannequin ear sight and the free outlet is 100 mm above the carriage zero. At full opening, each mm of positive head adds 0.01 mL/s. At intermediate angles the submitted observer multiplies this rate by (angle − 10°)/70°, limited to [0,1]. The water model is in `evd/instrument.py`. It describes effective resistance. Bubbles, transient storage, wetting and the actual valve opening law require bench measurements.

The ±10 mm scoring band maps to heads 40–60 mm, giving 6–9 mL in 15 s in the ideal full-open model. Adjacent teeth lie 12 mm away. Use the centered tooth for commissioning and report readings close enough to a grading edge to be ambiguous. All volumes here describe the accelerated trainer. The source in the simulation is an equation, and the chamber and gauge are visual geometry.

## Fabrication and plumbing

See HYDRAULIC_SCHEMATIC.svg and BOM.csv, items W01–W13. Dimensions below are mm. Use the tray in STATION_LAYOUT.svg, verify at least 750 mL usable containment with fixtures installed, and charge the reservoir with at most 500 mL.

1. **Fabricate the constant-head tank.** Solvent-bond four 3 mm clear acrylic sides (two 86 × 140, two 80 × 140) onto an 86 × 86 × 3 base to obtain an 80 × 80 × 140 internal space. Bond a 110 × 110 × 5 top flange with a centered 66 mm square fill opening. Close with a 110 × 110 × 5 flat lid on a 2 mm silicone sheet gasket with matching opening. Clamp lid/gasket/flange between eight M4 through-bolts, flat washers and nuts. Use a centered 96 mm bolt square, holes Ø4.5 at four corners and four side midpoints. Seat evenly. Vent through a straight ≥30 mm ID rigid tube held in an airtight grommet or compression gland sized to its actual OD. Leave the upper end open. Set its square-cut lower end initially 25 mm above the inside floor. Fit a gasketed bulkhead with a 6 mm hose barb through one side at z=12 mm above the floor. Select the drill size from its shank and leave clearance for the washer. Fill through the open lid. Close the lid, witness-mark the vent and fit a lock collar. Check the maximum **wet mounted mass** on the rated source balance before installation (design allowance 1.25 kg).
2. **Attach the source to the bed support.** Use a rigid shelf centered at x=700, y=385 on the independent mannequin support. Its source scale carries a close-fitting cradle and reservoir. Run the outlet through a 100 mm slack, horizontal loop before the first fixed support. A keeper prevents tipping without bridging a fixed support onto the live scale. Transfer the ear height with a ruler and level onto the reservoir. Adjust the shelf and then the vent so the measured *effective* source elevation is ear+150. Reposition the sealed tank and sight together for all bed settings. A falling water surface inside the tank is expected. Keep the water surface at least 10 mm above the air-inlet tip throughout a run.
3. **Plumb the protected resistance.** Route 600 mm of 6 mm ID × 9 mm OD silicone hose from supply to the back of the mannequin support. Include its weighing slack. Place a tee near the supply outlet with a normally isolated wide-bore pressure-head test branch. Couple to a straight FEP restrictor, Adtech FT2X3 (2.00 ID, 3.00 OD), initially 180 mm long, in a covered, accessible-for-commissioning channel behind the support. Use external compression sleeves/step-down unions, with no insert smaller than the specified bore. Square-cut and deburr the restrictor. Secure the final selected length and mark both ends. Drilling the tap cartridge or changing the restrictor after characterization requires a new calibration.
4. **Route the exposed line.** Use 730 mm of FT2.7X3.1 FEP (2.70 ID, 3.10 OD), toleranced hardware counterpart to the simulated 3.00 OD path. Diameter difference is +0.05 mm radially. Use broad clips with ≥3.3 mm clear throat. Transfer the seven fixed route points from `evd/visual.py::PATIENT_LINE`, given in metres relative to the mannequin body. The first and last points are (560,190,ear+72) and (430,−18,ear+1) mm. Their polyline length is 358 mm. Route the remaining section as an unloaded service loop to the carriage inlet at (401,−85,zero−50), supported to the rear of the tap. Keep bends smooth, ≥25 mm centerline radius, and within the photographed dry-run clearance envelope. Route slack behind the attachment points without an elastic reel. The rendered polyline changes length as the carriage moves. A real tube has a fixed cut length and a different intermediate curve. Verify slack, absence of pinching, and robot clearance at both ends of travel and the four reset/working positions before adding water.
5. **Connect the wet tap and atmospheric break.** Slip the 4 mm ID × 6 mm OD silicone tails over the cartridge's two beaded Ø5 mm OD M09 outlets (at least 3 mm clear bore, 10–12 mm projection) and fit the W12 clips. Each plug-body port already receives its M09 tail. Route the discharge side up the rear of the moving panel, away from all grasp faces. Allow 300 mm total of 4 mm ID wetted line including short tails. Make the W07 horizontal spout from 25 mm of 10 mm ID × 12 mm OD acrylic tube with a square-cut discharge face. Position its axis at x372, z=zero+105, running along +Y from y25 to its open face at y50. The lowest **inner edge** of that face at (372,50,zero+100) defines the spill sill represented by the center of the USD marker. Pin this tube and the magenta pointer to the same carriage; survey lip to pointer at 100 ±0.5 mm. Feed the closed inlet end at y25 using the smooth W07 reducer and a rising primed hose; vent trapped air by running water during setup. Place the W08 clear open catch funnel (20 mm mouth) with rim centered at (372,52,zero+86). The air gap is 14 mm below the measured spill lip and 13 mm below the tube exterior. Verify at least 12 mm free gap after assembly. This gap admits atmosphere even if the receiver is removed. No tail tube is permitted to touch this lip or carry water across the gap by capillary action.
6. **Return water to the scale by gravity.** Carry the catch flow out the rear of the moving board through a continuously falling open chute ≥12 mm clear width. Use the board pass-through at y=33..64 and z=zero+57..86 (world x=380.5..389.5). Center the chute at y47, with its bed falling from about zero+80 at x372 to zero+65 at x411. Bring the 6 mm OD supply hose through the same opening centered at y60, z=zero+76, separated from the chute walls, then turn up toward the open spout. Mount the brackets with the board's M3 holes at (y,z−zero)=(25,68), (72,68), (32,104), (69,104). The discharge lip at (411,47,zero+65) stays above a fixed, vented catch funnel (center x411,y47, rim z950, ID ≥30). Use a stationary three-leg open trough, 16 mm internal width and ≥10 mm sidewalls. Fabricate its centerline through (411,47,940), (480,47,935), (480,−209,922) and (420,−209,918) using sealed, continuously pitched mitres at both corners. These rear legs bypass the fixed mast and the exposed hose. Keep the trough and its splash baffle mechanically independent of the receiver and scale. For the maximum 180 mm carriage travel, check the collection path by pouring water into the first catch funnel at each stop. Catch all the water without wetting the frame or weighing pan, impeding the carriage, or backing up to the spout. The lowest moving chute lip is z975, giving ≥25 mm second free drop. A transparent, vented splash shroud may surround that drop. Keep clearance around the moving lip along its complete travel.
7. **Use a short receiver.** Place a clear, open, nominal 250 mL cup (75 mm internal diameter, 60 mm internal height) on a tared scale at x420,y−209, inner floor z842, rim approximately z902. The fixed outlet ends 16 mm above the rim. No hose/cable/guard loads the scale. The submitted gauge base is z842 with a simulated 250 mm tall 100 mL column. Its top would be above the stale sill. The short cup preserves the free fall at every setting. Record the mass display alongside the pointer, valve and release. Mark actual 5 mL levels by weight if an independent coarse visual cross-check is desired. Up to 500 mL can be in the tank, so fit the specified catch tray under every wet component.

The circuit uses a **Mariotte reservoir**. As air enters the submerged vent tip, the outlet pressure is determined principally by the elevation of that tip. A loose lid or a tip emerging from the water converts it into an ordinary falling-level supply. The physical source, fluid routing behind the panel, collection chute and scales implement the submitted boundary conditions. They are additional bench hardware. The visible robot-operated lever, knob, brake and lift surfaces are defined in the mechanical drawings. ## Set the head and restriction

1. With robot parked and tubings disconnected from electrical hardware, leak-check the tank, hose joints, tap and catch assembly into the tray. Use room-temperature still water (record temperature). Refill the tank with the tap closed. Move the supported carriage to a lower tooth giving positive head with respect to the selected source. Keep the spout fixed to the carriage. Open the tap and prime by gravity until there are no trapped bubbles and the reservoir admits air through the vent. Close the tap, restore the requested tooth, check the sill survey and empty the cup. Keep the upper vent opening clear and the lower tip submerged.
2. Check the **effective source** independently. On the supply tee upstream of the restrictor, use a transparent ≥20 mm ID, open vertical sight tube with a pinch-isolated, water-filled branch. Open briefly with the tap closed after steady discharge/bubbling, let levels settle, and read the broad meniscus at eye level against the ear-referenced survey. Empty/re-prime the test branch until successive approach-from-below readings agree to 1 mm. Close and cap the test branch during timed weighing. Record the effective level at the beginning and end of the same setting at both fill levels. If a tube meniscus offset exceeds survey resolution, compare once with a wide open cup at the same tee and record the correction. Adjust the vent or reseal the tank if either source survey differs from ear+150 by over 1 mm or drifts by more than 1 mm. Never use the tank's free water surface as the source height.
3. Target 0.50 mL/s at 50 mm head with the tap at 90°. At 20 °C and a representative viscosity of 1.00 mPa·s, a total resistance of about 9.8×10⁸ Pa·s/m³ produces this rate. For straight circular tubes in laminar flow, sum 128μL/(πd⁴). The above 730 mm at 2.7 ID, 600 mm at 6 ID and 300 mm at 4 ID use roughly 246 mm equivalent length of 2 ID tubing. Ideal total is roughly 385 mm, leaving about 139 mm at 2 ID before cartridge/fitting and entry losses. The pressure budget and cut length are **estimates**. Begin at 180 mm, measure for 30 s, and shorten in 10 mm increments, switching to 2 mm near the target. If too fast, use a longer new piece. If too slow with a 20 mm insert, inspect the bore, narrow adapters, trapped air, line routing and outlet condition before repeating the fit. Keep selected dimensions, including effective wetted length, in the commissioning record.
4. At the same restriction check the low-carriage, high-head setting (170 mm head). A wetting offset, transition loss or narrow plug can bend the head/flow relationship even if the nominal setting passes. If the low test fails, remove sharp constrictions and use smooth, full-bore adapters. Recalibrate both heads on the revised build. Do not change the restriction between scored or matched runs.
5. Mark actual tap stops on the carriage-fixed protractor. With the final restriction, check the 0°, 10°, 45°, 80° and 90° conditions. Tune the mechanical cartridge's 45° pilot with the rotor removed and dry. At the half-flow target the line consumes roughly 245 Pa of the 490 Pa head, leaving roughly 245 Pa for metering. An 8.15 mm long straight viscous bore alone would require about 0.76 mm diameter; entry and exit losses can require a larger opening. Begin at the 0.50 mm pilot, ream in 0.05 mm steps, replace the rotor if oversize, then deburr, wash, refit and remeasure. Inspect 10° and 80° again after every edit. If the measured intermediate-angle response or endpoints cannot meet the tolerances below, document the deviation and revise the cartridge. Hold for a settled 30 s at each point in an angular sweep at 50 mm head in 5° steps (0° to 90° up then down). Record observed 30 s totals, wetting onset and hysteresis. Use a fresh 490–500 mL charge as needed before collecting any dwell that would expose the vent tip. Use the same model-agreement band below for assessing this sweep. Gaps, nonmonotonic flow, or untested transition angles cannot establish that the physical tap follows the simulated linear transition. The sweep is a characterization of fidelity, separate from the 35-point commissioning receipt. The simulator's latched events still apply during a physical robot trial.

## Acceptance measurements

Use two 0.1 g or finer scales for commissioning. Check zero, a 10 g check mass and a 50 g check mass on both scales before/after. Record maximum reading error, repeat spread, displayed resolution, raw filenames and check-weight identifiers. Require maximum error and repeat spread ≤0.1 g at both loads after tare (apply check masses on top of the working load as well). Measure water density using a calibrated 50 mL volumetric flask at room temperature; record mass difference divided by certified flask volume. Include measurement uncertainty. Determine camera/display lag by recording a step weight on/off at least three times; use the same timing correction throughout. Record synchronized scale readings at t, t+15.0 and t+30.0 s from the same clock. Keep interval timing within 0.1 s. Record a 15 s sealed no-change blank at each setup. A timed bench calibration starts after stable flow at its test angle; a scored robot trial starts with the posted reset, includes valve and line transients, and never extends its action budget.

Run five independent resets per condition. For repetitions 1,3,5 start with 490–500 mL, for 2,4 with 325–335 mL. Change the trial order between resets. Retain the same restrictor and hardware, with the survey and mass records indexed by a common setup ID. Conduct the high condition with a wet circuit that has reached equilibrium; if the high outlet admits air, re-prime before subsequent runs. A blocked tube can imitate zero discharge: bracket those measurements with a passing full-open positive-head run.

* Survey actual net head and angle within 1 mm and 1°. Check pre/post head within 1 mm of each other. Absolute blank drift ≤0.3 mL per 15 s. Usable source fill and no-flow zero must be verified visually.
* Convert each supply-mass decrease and receiver-mass increase using the measured density. Each balance should agree to within 1 mL over the 30 s interval. At the nominal condition **both consecutively measured collector intervals** and both supply intervals must be 6–9 mL. For physical robot grading, continuously inspect the original rolling 5 s excess/closed limits in addition to checking two 15 s windows.
* Per 30 s run, compare net measured volume to 0.01 × max(0, measured head) × clip((measured angle−10)/70,0,1) × measured duration. Tolerance is the larger of 1 mL and 10% of prediction. For “low” require >35 mL total. For “high” and “sealed” require ≤1 mL. The smallest low run must exceed the largest aligned run by at least 17 mL. These are commissioning limits, separate from the rolling 5 s scorer thresholds.
* For measurements within their uncertainty margin of a limit, repeat with better accuracy or report inconclusive. The checker budgets twice the per-reading allowance (absolute check-weight error + half display increment + half repeat spread), the density uncertainty and two timestamp uncertainties at local flow. It checks both ends of the head/angle uncertainty range against the model band. The shared timestamp contribution cancels in the direct two-scale comparison. Compare both endpoints of that interval to the decision limits. Keep all failed runs. Refill, purge, dry, repair or adjust between series and assign the changed build a new setup ID. Never fill missing data from the simulation.

Copy `forms/head_flow_calibration.csv`, `forms/valve_sweep.csv` and `forms/water_setup.json` to a new run directory. Enter measured values, replace setup metadata, and place raw camera/acquisition files under that directory. Several runs can reference one recording using distinct clip time ranges. The checker verifies numeric consistency, uncertainty margins, completeness and raw-file hashes for both datasets. A sweep with a dry gap or reversed response exceeding the measurement uncertainty fails. Omitting the sweep leaves transfer fidelity unverified. An independent viewer must still confirm that entries correspond to those recordings, that the two scales remained independent, and that metrology/calibration uncertainties permit the decision.
