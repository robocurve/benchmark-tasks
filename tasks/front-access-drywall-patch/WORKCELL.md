# Build, reset and grade the repair

This work order starts after a damaged area between studs has been cut square. It ends with a mechanically attached substrate for mesh reinforcement and joint compound. The front is the only access during the episode. Another person can remove the rear inspection cover after the robot and driver have stopped. The backing method follows the manufacturer's large-hole repair sequence. [ADFORS instructions](https://www.adfors.com/sites/hps-mac3-adfors-us/files/2024-05/fibatape_large%20hole_instructions.pdf)

## Driver and load model

Contact with the face compresses a spring-loaded nose. With the shaft normal within about 11.5° and the nose within 1.2 mm of the local board surface, sustained rotation under feed cuts into that point. For a successful installation the tip travels about 31 mm by rotating, the strip lies against the board, at least six of ten 15–30 mm depth samples lie in the timber and the driver has done at least 1 J of rotational work in that timber. A missed bite consumes a screw. A braked spindle cannot complete the lead. Compliance at the installed site transmits loads to the original rigid bodies and can fail under overload.

The spring is 180 N/m with 34 mm of travel. Lee Spring LCM063DF 05 provides a build reference for the custom return sleeve, with 0.18 N/mm nominal rate, 80.5 mm free length and 12.915 mm solid height. A guided 34 mm compression from its free length gives 6.12 N by the nominal linear rate. This specifies the return spring only. [Lee Spring metric catalogue](https://www.leespring.in/sites/default/files/2019-09/compression-springs-lee-spring-in.pdf) The numerical spindle has a 0.65 N·m drive limit. As the screw enters wood, cutting resistance rises from 0.025 toward 0.325 N·m; it opposes native shaft rotation and puts the opposite torque into the wood. Comparable plywood coupon studies measured seating torques of 0.31–0.69 N·m under different pilot and head conditions. This is an engineering approximation for the pine/gypsum pair. The pressure, friction, material torque and failure numbers are not measured properties of the specified fastener in this stock. Robot actions must produce the motion and react the load. The seated joint gives finite in-plane torsional resistance (12 N·m/rad) to the head and shank under the next screw’s reaction. Its axial stiffness is 9 N/mm. Once the head seats, 12 N of nominal screw tension draws the two real contact surfaces together. The tension acts along the wall normal on the timber and facing with opposite forces; a failed connection loses it. These are fixture model parameters. Paper rupture, individual thread flanks and strip indexing are reduced in the simulation. [Coupon measurements](https://bioresources.cnr.ncsu.edu/resources/effects-of-pilot-hole-diameter-and-depth-on-screw-driving-torques-in-plywood/) · [Commercial pressure-start operation](https://documents.milwaukeetool.com/58-14-0071d1.pdf)

For physical commissioning, drive a screw through an offcut into spare timber set against its rear. Confirm the nose depth leaves the head at the paper face, the paper is intact, the screw actually threads into wood, and the worked piece can be released. Repeat off the edge of the strip and confirm that a visually seated head does not create a connection. On the shared rig, prepare and cut stock away from the station, catch dust below the wall and de-energize the driver during restocking.

## End-of-episode score

Inspect the retained work at the end of the episode. Mark horizontal reference lines 25 mm above and below the centre on the grader's template. For each row, both jamb fastening points and its qualifying patch screws must be on the same side of that band. Either strip may form either row. Only sound fastenings into the actual timber count. A failed joint or movement of 2 mm or more between its original attachment points loses that fixing. Use a witness mark and a 2 mm gauge when inspecting through the rear cover. Broken or missed connections provide no support. Examine actual penetration via the inspection cover after the push test. Score the highest completed row of the table.

| Score | Test |
|---|---|
| 0 | No timber row fixed at both the left and right old-wall jambs. |
| 0.25 | At least one timber row fixed to both jambs. |
| 0.50 | Two distinct strips fixed to both jambs, one above and one below the centre band. |
| 0.75 | Both rows present, patch fastened to each with at least one screw. Carrying tool removed, no arm or tool supports any workpiece, and the patch fits for one second. |
| 1.00 | As above, with at least one panel fixing on each side of each strip, at least 25 mm left or right of centre. The released patch passes the centre push. |

For fit, bridge the joint at each corner with a straightedge referenced to the old wall. Check the face step is below 2 mm in either direction, the widest edge gap is at most 3.5 mm and the panel sits in the opening (allow 0.5 mm edge-reading tolerance). For the push, zero the travel reference at the released face, wait 0.5 s, push straight inward at the centre with 2 N for 1 s, then unload and wait 1 s. Maximum travel of any corner relative to its settled reference is 1 mm. A fixed bridge with 1 mm go/no-go stops or gauges on the corners lets the grader observe movement during the push. A tool may be close to the panel while free of it. Check the handle is detached. The last step certifies this defined light-load test, with no structural load rating implied.

## Reset

Unpower the tool. Remove the rear commissioning cover to inspect the rails and photograph the eight fastening sites; use a fresh set of stock for the next trial. Replace the old front panel or its pierced jamb sections, both timber strips and the facing patch. Cut the board and slots using a master template before the trials and stage spare sets. Clean the table, reload twelve screws, return tools and stock to the listed rests, close and fasten the rear cover and reset the arms. Use the original setup photograph to check all loose parts.
