# Pre-simulation design review — 2026-11-06

Instruction: Use the left arm to hold the slingshot frame and the right arm to
pull back and release its loaded ball. Knock down both towers with the ball.
Keep both arms behind the launch line. One ball, one shot, 1,200 controls at 20 Hz.

Starting state: two YAM arms, open grippers; a free-standing fork and guided
pouch; one red 50 mm diameter ball already in the pouch; two three-block towers
in a bounded catch tray. The fixed numerical layout, masses and dimensions are
generated by build.py into geometry.json and pilot.json. No randomization.

Physical impediment: the right arm must stretch a passive spring while the
left resists its reaction force on the free frame. Releasing finger contact
lets the spring accelerate the pouch and ball; a travel stop retains the pouch.
The ball then flies and collides with free tower blocks under native PhysX.
Observable hazards: unbraced launcher translation or tilt, premature launch,
ball escape, direct robot contact with towers, and unstable initial stacks.

Fidelity decision: this is a tabletop toy slingshot with a horizontal pouch
guide, not a calibrated flexible-rubber model. One native prismatic spring
represents the resultant of two bands; its target never changes. Rendered band
curves follow measured endpoints and have no collision or force effect. The
guide constrains the pouch orientation and lateral motion. Rigid, non-fracturing
toy blocks and ball approximate foam props. No arbitrary launch impulse,
velocity assignment, object teleport, grasp weld, or scheduled tower collapse.

Distinct contribution: simultaneous counterholding, elastic energy storage,
release timing and ballistic interaction with distant targets. This differs
from the local spring-clamp, drawer-counterhold, tray-transfer and pin tasks.
An Angry Birds theme alone establishes neither benchmark novelty nor societal
importance. This is an ordinary bimanual game task, not yet a RESIST acceptance.

Alternatives: a slower draw, different grasp or aim, or a ball-triggered cascade
are legitimate. Pushing towers directly, throwing the launcher, scoring stacks
that fall at reset, throwing the ball without drawing the pouch, and hand-guided
flight are not. The scorer requires a supported draw, a released forward
crossing, stable launcher pose, hands behind x=0.55 m, and settled tower collapse.
Grasp checks use actual finger positions and TCP proximity, not force closure;
launch provenance is sampled at 20 Hz. Reject if these proxies admit cheating.

Overlap checks: no local slingshot/Angry Birds implementation found. The live
Robocurve board was inaccessible through web access on 2026-11-06; an indexed
slingshot query returned no matching task, which is not evidence of absence.
Detailed LIBERO, RoboCasa, SimplerEnv, BEHAVIOR, DataCenterBench and RoboDojo
overlap remains open. No definitive novelty claim was established by that initial check.

Rejection criteria: unstable reset; infeasible arm path; absent continuous
contact during draw; ball escapes without release; frame needs artificial
anchoring; idle/unbraced controls earn credit; native full score cannot be shown
within the limit; rendered evidence does not match the scorer. Verify the simulation host, GPU, source revision/fingerprint, native joint controllers and all three camera
outputs. Unit checks cannot establish physical feasibility. Record actual
iteration results separately and retain failed runs.

## Camera and controller revision — 2026-11-07

The updated AGENTS.md walkthrough was read. Its stock YAM task-file interface
supports primitive objects and library assets but has no spring/compound-joint
declaration, so this apparatus continues to use the repository's native Isaac
extension. It is not claimed to run through `yam_scripted` or Inspect Robots 1.0.
The overview is now above and behind the arms at (-0.12,0,2.40), looking toward
(0.46,0,0.75), 19.4 degrees from vertical. The camera stand remains behind the
arm bases instead of standing through the workspace. Native views must verify
both arms, draw, release and tower impact remain visible.

Weak/glancing shots and perched blocks prevented full score in earlier native
trials. The next controller requests 195 mm of hand travel (pouch travel is
physically limited to 180 mm), storing more elastic energy than the earlier
150 mm command. Actual travel differs because the free frame and joint
controllers deflect under load. Spring properties, masses, run limit and scorer
thresholds remain unchanged. Both towers are again aligned at y=-0.075.

The longer draw brought the releasing wrist into the pouch path and is rejected.
Return to the demonstrated 150 mm hand draw. Narrow each toy block along the
shot direction from 54 to 30 mm: the former broad stacks tended to slide or
bridge rather than tip; the new 150 mm tall towers have a 30 mm tipping footprint.
Keep their 70 mm width, 18 g mass and three levels. Fresh idle validation must
reject spontaneous collapse. Score and safety thresholds remain unchanged.
The camera rises to 2.55 m and targets (0.50,0,0.75) to include the catch tray.

Native v7 exposed a sampling defect: a shot can cross the launch line, strike a
tower and reverse velocity within one 50 ms observation. The forward-crossing
gate now accepts forward speed above 0.5 m/s in either adjacent sample, while
retaining the braced draw, open fingers and release-distance requirements.
A specific rubric regression check covers this case; actual finger opening and
ball forward speed are now included in native diagnostics.

The slender first tower toppled safely in v8, but its falling blocks did not
reach the second. Version 9 uses centers x=0.61 and 0.67, leaving a 30 mm clear
gap between the towers so the intended ball-triggered cascade can transfer
momentum. There is no connection between the stacks. The camera targets x=0.55
to include the upper catch-wall edge. An idle trial must still score zero.

## Submission overlap review — 2026-11-11

The authenticated task board returned 1,602 tasks, including accepted and pending
entries. Searches across title, instruction, keywords and context found no
slingshot, catapult or Angry Birds match. The nearest projectile task was
TK-TEJ5WU, “Catch a ball flying out of a chute in a basket before it touches the

this task requires robot-loaded elastic energy, counterholding and release to
strike two targets. This is a meaningful manipulation difference, not a colour
or layout variant. Broad projectile keyword matches concerned MRI screening,
