**Ordinary closure method.** The liner closes the way drawstring liners are meant to: one drawstring runs in the mouth hem and is exposed as a loop at a cut-out on each side; pulling the two loops slides string out of the hem and gathers the mouth, and the bag is then lifted and carried by the strings. Glad's instructions for its small drawstring bags describe the same everyday method: "Lift the trash bag from the container by pulling the drawstrings on either side of the bag" and "Carry the bag by the drawstring and toss into garbage" (Target, Glad Small Drawstring 4 gal: https://www.target.com/p/glad-small-drawstring-trash-bags-beachside-breeze-4-gallon/-/A-83771022). There is **no knot, cord lock, clip or added handle**. The mouth stays gathered because the bag hangs from the strings while carried and, once set down, because the gathered film and string do not spring back open by themselves.

### Liner/closure preflight (once per liner batch, before robot trials)

Not yet performed by the authors. It takes three fresh liners from one batch, each put through steps 1-3 below; record the results in the build log.

1. Confirm the liner has one drawstring exposed at two cut-outs on opposite faces of the hem. Record the cut-out width and the length of loop that can be hooked out of a fresh liner without gathering the mouth.
2. Fit the liner as in **Reset** below. By hand, pinch the far end of each loop and pull them up: the folded surplus must slide up over the rim and the mouth gather to an opening of at most 80 mm.
3. Lift the bag clear by the two loops only, hold 5 s, set it upright on the table and let go. Measure the opening after 2 s. Accept the liner batch only if it is at most 80 mm and all three blocks are inside. Repeat with three liners.
4. If a batch fails, do not substitute a lock, clip or knot; report the batch as incompatible.

## Reset (start state)

1. Put a fresh liner in the pot, bottom flat on the pot floor, and fold the surplus (the liner is about 25 cm taller than the pot) down over the rim; the hem then hangs around the outside of the pot about 2 cm above the mat.
2. Turn the liner so the two hem cut-outs face the two arms (+y and -y). Hook each exposed drawstring loop (the flat film tape, about 20 mm wide) out of its cut-out and lay it on the mat as a narrow U, strands about 16 mm apart, about 85 mm from the cut-out to the end of the loop, pointing outward and turned 30 degrees toward the front of the table (+x), with the tape on edge as it leaves the hem channel (its width vertical). A loop that falls flat is left as it lies. Do not pull further: the mouth must stay open over the rim.
3. Place the three blocks inside, long side along y: two standing on a 45 x 100 mm face side by side on the liner bottom, centred at x = 0.1965 and 0.2435, y = 0; the third lying flat (70 x 100 mm face down) on top of them, centred at x = 0.22, y = 0.
4. Arms at the standard ready pose; clear the tabletop beside the pot (x < 0.10). Start the 4,000-step (200 s) budget from this state.

## Scoring (ruler, overhead/side video, block count)

Use the fixed overhead camera plus a side camera with a ruler or grid in view, frame timestamps, and a block count after the trial. **Opening** is the largest straight-line distance across the bag mouth (the hem), read from two views if the mouth is tilted. Failures latch: once a zero condition happens the trial scores 0 regardless of later recovery.

| Score | Condition (highest satisfied level) |
|---|---|
| 0 | Opening never reaches 80 mm or less while the loops are held and pulled (see 0.25); **or** any block leaves the bag at any time; **or** the bag is torn/cut to bypass the closure; **or** (simulation) invalid geometry. |
| 0.25 | **Cinched by the drawstrings:** the opening reached 80 mm or less with all three blocks inside, **while each gripper holds one drawstring loop between its fingers, the string has been drawn out of the two cut-outs by at least 100 mm in total and is taut, and no film has been pinched between the fingers at any earlier time.** (A block's smallest face is 70 x 45 mm, diagonal 83 mm, so no block fits through an 80 mm opening.) |
| 0.5 | **Carried by the drawstrings:** after cinching, the whole bag and contents were at least 20 mm above the pot rim at the same time, with the opening at most 80 mm, **while both loops were held and the string taut, neither gripper touched the film, and no film had been pinched between the fingers**: the load hangs from the loops. |
| 0.75 | **Set down:** at the end the bag rests on the tabletop, upright (gathered mouth at least 150 mm above the table top), bottom within 25 mm of the table top, every part at least 20 mm clear of the pot's outer wall and at least 20 mm inside the table edges, opening at most 80 mm, all blocks inside. |
| 1.0 | **Released:** the set-down state holds for the final 2 s with both grippers at least 60 mm from the film and the drawstring loops, and the bag's visible extents moving no more than 10 mm. |

**Reading the grasp conditions on video.** Mark the tape 100 mm from each cut-out with a pen line on both strands before the trial; "drawn out by 100 mm in total" means the four marks together have moved at least 100 mm away from their cut-outs (read against the ruler), and "taut" means the strands between hand and cut-out are straight, not sagging. A loop is held when the tape is visibly clamped between the two fingers; film is pinched or touched when any part of the liner is between or against the fingers in the side or wrist view. Lifting, pinching or squeezing the liner film itself earns no cinch or carry credit, whatever the opening.

Regrasps, pauses and corrective pulls are allowed. Any landing spot on the table that satisfies the margins is valid.

## Simulation correspondence and limits

| Real setup | Simulation |
|---|---|
| Code A liner: 58 cm mouth, 40 cm tall, film gauge not published | PhysX surface-deformable film authored in the fitted state: 152 mm body diameter inside the pot, an arc over the rim and the surplus folded down outside to a hem of 58.4 cm circumference 23 mm above the mat; 377 mm profile length (the real liner's last ~2 cm, which would rest on the mat, is omitted); 0.10 mm film. The fold is part of the film's rest shape, so the unfolded film keeps a slight crease there. Material constants are not calibrated. |
| Drawstring and its two exposed loops (flat film tape, about 20 mm wide) | **Each exposed loop is a flexible strap the gripper must pinch:** a chain of 15 thin rigid links (10 mm long, 20 mm wide, 2 mm numerical thickness, 4 g per loop in total) joined by spring-loaded joints, laid out as in Reset as a U of two strands and a bight. The strap bends freely about its width (the soft direction of a flat tape), twists against a spring (up to 60 degrees per joint) and bends only a little within its own plane (25 degrees per joint), like a flat tape. The robot pinches it with its ordinary contact grasp, closing across the doubled strap; there is no attachment. The twist spring is stiffer than a real film tape, so a simulated loop stays on edge where a real one may fall flat; the 2 mm link thickness and the 4 g mass are numerical (a real tape is far thinner and lighter). The string inside the hem and the newly drawn-out string between each strap end and its cut-out are a routed-length spring through 20 hem markers and the four strap end links; the tension pulls on the strap ends and reaches the hand only through the strap's joints. Pulling the loops slides string out of the hem and gathers the mouth. The hidden and drawn-out string is force-only and drawn as a line, not a collider. No lock or one-way force: with the loops released the string force is zero. Numerical safeguards: the elastic string force is capped at 0.7 N (about twice the 0.4 N each strand carries when the bag hangs from it), so under a hard pull or a fast lift the force-only drawn-out string lengthens (by up to about 1 m in the demo runs) rather than pulling harder; a viscous term (0.5 N s/m along the routed length, itself limited to 0.7 N), standing for the string's sliding friction in the hem, damps the bounce of the hanging bag; the solve uses an effective inertia of 1.5 g per hem point and 2 g per strap end; the 20 hem points have no colliders. |
| NYPON pot, 17.1 / 15.9 cm diameter, 15.2 cm high | Static ring of 40 wall segments, 159 mm inside / 171 mm outside diameter, rim at z = 0.902 (152 mm above the mat), 6 mm floor; a straight cylinder (the pot's taper is not modelled). |
| Three 50 g blocks | Three 50 g rigid boxes, 70 x 100 x 45 mm, placed as in Reset. |

The primary simulation scorer (`drawstring_score.py`) applies the same thresholds to the recorded film mesh, block poses, strap links, hem points, gripper poses and finger openings at every control step. A loop counts as held when the fingers are closed to 15 mm or less with a strap link between the pads (pads modelled as 24 mm wide, from 30 mm behind to 13 mm beyond the tool point, with 3 mm contact margin); film counts as pinched when a film node is between the pads with the fingers closed to 30 mm or less, and as touched when a film node is within 5 mm of a finger's outline (10 mm thick fingers). Drawn-out string is the summed distance from the four strap ends to their cut-out hem points minus its start value; the string is taut when the routed string length is no more than 10 mm below its start value. Containment uses the non-convex film mesh with two force-free virtual caps (across the hem, and across the fold over the rim while the surplus is folded down), signed winding numbers so the gap between liner and folded-back surplus counts as outside, checked on every block's centre and six face centres with an 8 mm tolerance (film contact offset and film pressed in under the carried load) and on its eight corners with a 20 mm tolerance. The film mesh has 9 mm median and up to 20 mm triangle edges, and a sharp box corner can protrude through a film triangle between its nodes (by up to about 15 mm in these runs) while the film still encloses the block; 20 mm is the largest triangle edge and less than half the blocks' smallest edge (45 mm). A block whose centre or any face centre leaves the film fails the trial. A film edge stretch above 1.5 marks the simulation trial invalid; it is not a tear measurement.
