# A moving mug on a household counter

The robot hears “Pour my tea to the line on my mug and put the pitcher down.” An examiner moves the mannequin-held mug once as the water reaches a marked level. Both the mug and the reaching hand move. The person operating the cord stays outside the arms’ reachable space. Only room-temperature water and an inert surrogate are used.

**Build status.** These are fabrication drawings and commissioning procedures. Physical assembly, water collection accuracy, wrist-indicator performance and hardware pours have not been verified. The supplied qualification evidence is simulation evidence. Record physical measurements in a new run directory before making a physical replication claim.

## Mount the arms and set the scene

All lengths below are millimetres. The tabletop is 820 × 1,140, 750 above the floor. Measure +x from the arm-base line toward the surrogate and +y toward the left arm. Worktop edges are x=−80 and 740, y=−570 and 570. The two standard YAM base-frame origins are (0,300,0) and (0,−300,0) with parallel orientations, stock +x along the bench x axis. Bolt the manufacturer’s base plates to a rigid workbench using their actual hole pattern. Use each stock plate as a transfer template centered on the marked axis, square it to x, mark and drill the four *existing* attachment positions if present on that plate or use all its supplied mounting positions. A flush mounting insert or recess sets the arm-frame origin at the worktop elevation. Support the factory mount over its entire bearing area and use the supplied mounting hardware or the station owner’s approved equivalent. Document the model, four corners, hole spacing, top elevation and base-frame axes, with rulers in frame. Check each base center and elevation within 2 mm, yaw within 1°. The ordinary bench top and mount carry the arms; the 12 mm stage board carries only the surrogate fixture. The modeled seated body extends beyond the front of the counter. The chair/support footprint occupies x=760…1,080, y=−50…330. Allow **1,160 × 1,140 mm** for bench plus seat, and the manufacturer’s operating clearance beyond this furniture and for the cord and tripods. [YAM mounting instructions](https://doc.i2rt.com/products/yam)

Set the surrogate shoulder center at (825,266,334) relative to the tabletop. Anchor the torso to a chair or independent rigid stand at the seat position. A light passive shoulder and elbow follow the moving wrist. Shoulder-to-elbow and elbow-to-wrist lengths are 270 and 220, nominal upper-arm diameter 80 and forearm diameter 64. At A the wrist center is (529,245,53). Let the elbow hang below the shoulder toward −y. Use a soft articulated dummy, surfaces without exposed fasteners and a dorsal bracket carrying the hand. Check that neither an arm segment nor a collector takes the weight of the moving mug. Seat the handle in bent mannequin fingers on the **+x** face. A concealed loop on the far side of the handle can secure it to the palm. Keep every clamp, tie, fastener and cover below or outside the mouth. The mug stays suspended with its underside 2 mm above the spill surface. Check that mug, links, hand and cover move from A to B without snagging, draining into the mug or dragging the cup bottom. Photograph A and B in the same side and plan views.

Center the free pitcher at (365,320), upright, with spout +y and grip −y. It rests on the counter. The reference returns it near the same mark. Any released upright location with verified counter support can earn full credit. Do not fit a funnel, docking socket, magnet or gripper attachment to either vessel.

Measure empty and loaded balance points by supporting the vessel in a cradle in two orthogonal orientations under controlled dry handling; record both offsets and the real dry tare. The model does not resolve the changing three-dimensional fluid center of mass. The clear mug has a nominal 70 mm body diameter, 89 mm wall height and a +x handle extending to x≈63. Center it at A=(428,240), B=(345,240). Use a thin contrasting removable **48 g trigger mark** and a distinct **120 g serving line**; calibrate by adding weighed room-temperature water to the installed dry mug and reading the bottom of the meniscus at eye level. The source and target vessels should match the actual intended jaw access, stream and target aperture. Take scaled side and top photographs.

## Assemble the 83 mm passive stage

1. Use the lower-left board corner at station (485,287) as the plate origin (u,v). Cut the 240 × 112 × 12 base; seal edges and use four Ø5.5 anchors at u=6,234 and v=7,105. Place the board outside the pitcher’s return area. Keep threaded fixings outside the liquid collection path or seal the penetrations and leakage-test with the electronics removed.
2. Mount two Ø8 × 222 rods parallel to x, at v=28,84 and z=24 above the worktop, ends u=9,231. Clamp each end in a split saddle at u=11,229. Saddle body 10 × 16 with two mounting ears 24 mm apart edge to edge. Drill Ø3.3 holes in the ears at v±8 from the shaft axis. Use a U-strap to retain each rod without crushing it. Install two plain or linear bushes per rod to the underside of a 66 × 90 × 6 carriage with their centers at (±20,±28) from the plate center. Bore or shim the housings until plate underside z=32. Clamp the second rod only after sliding the loaded carriage end to end. Align within 0.25 mm over the active stroke; both rods must be retained axially.
3. Use bolted stop brackets at v=56 with screw-adjusted, thin elastomer faces meeting the carriage ends at **u=43** and **u=192**. When loaded against the faces, the plate center is (159,56) at A and (76,56) at B. Ink both center locations on the base beside the carriage index. A thin spacer at either face can trim the stroke without moving the vessel bracket.
4. From the left carriage edge at (126,70) in reset, route a low-stretch Ø1 cord to a pulley at (16,70), clear of the stop at v=56, then to the fairlead at (16,99) and through a fixed sleeve toward the +y bench edge. A high-contrast tab on the cord near station (501,548) makes handling visible. Continue the cord in a fixed tube out to the operator’s station beyond the arm envelope. Fix the sleeve, keep slack and fingers out of the arm workspace, and test the full stroke before energizing the arms. Start with the carriage at A. The examiner pulls once; return to A only at reset with the robot stopped. No motor, electronic flow trigger or policy signal is required.
5. Mount the two perpendicular wrist slides described below. From the top center at A=(644,343,62) attach a light 8 mm shaft or rounded folded angle toward (529,343,62), then to the dorsal wrist at (529,245,53). A two-hole anti-rotation strap fixes the hand. The bracket, palm and mug travel together. No rigid part extends above the receiver or supports the pitcher. Verify actual collector and bracket clearances against a dry replay at A and B.

## Assemble the observable contact alternative

The retained indicator measures **at least 3 mm of actual X or Y travel** of the held hand/mug relative to the moving examiner carriage. This replaces the former multi-face 10 N rule. It measures displacement at the receiver. A vertical contact, balanced opposing loads, or contact with a proximal arm segment may produce no flag. Whole-body contact force, injury and pressure are outside this task’s score.

Stack two low-friction 60 mm MGN7 rails and long MGN7H blocks orthogonally. Each rail/block assembly is 8 mm tall. The lower rail bolts to the main carriage along +x, and its 60 × 60 × 4 intermediate plate carries the +y rail. A second 60 × 60 × 4 plate supports the hand bracket. Their lower/upper surfaces above the tabletop are 46/50 and 58/62. Use the actual matched rail as a hole jig. For a symmetric four-hole 60 mm cut, center the hole line, pitch 15, offsets ±7.5 and ±22.5 mm. For MGN7H long-block mounting the four M2 tapped points are at ±6 mm crosswise and ±6.5 mm along travel. Choose screw lengths to stay within the purchased block’s specified engagement. Fit captive end stops that allow ±6 mm actual travel per axis.

For each axis attach two opposed light extension springs between lower frame and upper plate outside the rail. Use springs with measured slope 0.25 N/mm each between 4 and 16 mm extension. Install at equal nominal 10 mm extension at the neutral mark, adjust with screw eyes and locknuts, and verify equal restore in both directions. At neutral place each upper plate against its printed zeros. Mount two visible friction rings on a Ø2 straight stainless guide rod anchored to the *lower* frame at that joint, with a rigid fork on the plate above it. The fork can be made from 1 mm PETG sheet, with a 2.5 mm slot around the rod. One ring sits against each face of the fork. Pushing the upper frame moves only the ring on that side; the ring retains the excursion when the fork returns. Mark ±3 and ±6 from the corresponding reset face. Use contrasting white/green reset bands and red trip bands. Set the two rods at the exposed edges, away from the cord and water collection cover. The Y ring reads relative to the intermediate X plate. The simulated indicator combines these four peak excursions into one retained red flag. The [simulation contact frames](bench/WITNESS_SIMULATION.png) and [step record](bench/WITNESS_SIMULATION.json) show the virtual indicator after a displacement and after unloading. The physical score reads the four rings; any ring reaching its 3 mm line latches the same event. A camera showing the scales throughout an episode guards against someone resetting a ring.

**Commission the assembly before physical use.** With water excluded and the robot parked, measure 0, ±2.0, ±3.0, ±4.0 and ±6.0 mm using a caliper and temporary noncompressible shims against each assembled joint. Repeat three times each direction. Require no trip at 2, onset at the 3 mark within ±0.2 mm, retained evidence after 4, and mechanical capture at 6. Test positive, negative, diagonal and rapid excursion/return, dry and after external covers have been wetted with the electronics removed. Require the rings to hold their reading within 0.2 mm for 60 s and not creep under normal A→B motion. Record pictures with the caliper at zero and on both sides of trip. A reading with uncertainty spanning trip is invalid unless another view resolves it. The simulator uses a linear spring 0.5 N/mm and damping 2 N·s/m on each axis. Check spring slope using known weights over a low-friction pulley in both directions around ±1…4 mm, and subtract restoring preload at center. Fit slopes within 20% of the target; spring calibration characterizes travel behavior and is not a primary score. Use slow-motion phone video of an unloaded lateral release at 3 mm and fit the decay of successive oscillation peaks in time. With measured translating mass m in kg, c=2m ln(a1/a2)/(t2−t1). Record friction or overdamped motion if peaks cannot be resolved. Record and tune slide preload/damping strip for approximate 1–3 N·s/m; if it cannot be matched, report the physical/simulation contact response discrepancy. Simulated dry masses for the upper assembly and extra X carriage are 300 g and 90 g. Record the actual bracket/hand/mug assembly masses empty and loaded. Calibrate again with filled mug and covers; report the added mass and any false activations. The simplified model holds these inertias fixed.

## Set up protected wet collection and grading views

Use room-temperature water, an inert surrogate and unpowered equipment for the initial collection tests. Place a waterproof removable skin over the work surface and a shallow, rimmed pan under the entire wet footprint. Keep seams and pan lips out of the pitcher handling area and the mug trajectory. Put power supplies, electronics and cable junctions outside all runoff and drip paths; fix and drip-loop cables to the dry side. Use only covers or sacrificial pads compatible with the installed hardware and accepted by its operator. Shield the exposed wrist camera window and nonmoving housings from droplets without obscuring the lenses, joint vents, cables or jaw travel. Keep water away from unprotected openings; a cover does not establish an ingress rating. Repeat dry grasp, lift, tilt, set-down and retraction checks with protectors installed using a capped test load of matching mass. Use a free, unmodified handle and the standard jaws. Check for slip and unchanged sightlines and collect water on the protective surfaces as **other spill**. Shielding must never steer a missed pour into the mug.

Weigh the dry mug and pitcher separately. Cover all mannequin surfaces in the potential spray path with individually removable, preweighed absorbent sleeves, including fingers, cuff and forearm. Back each with a waterproof layer and a dedicated runoff gutter ending in a separate tared cup. Keep the absorbent layer physically separated from the mug rim and catch pan to stop wicking or double counting. Separately tare other-spill pan liners, splash screens and robot protection absorbents. Removable liners in sealed bags allow weighing on one 0.1 g scale. Confirm the expected maximum gain remains within the capacity of the pan and scale. Log each tare and photograph labels. Near a dry run-off edge, add small measured drops with robot absent and verify which collector retains them. Completely reset and dry the station between tests.

For policy observations reproduce the overview eye (−220,−540,770) looking at (460,140,260) above the table, 48° vertical field, plus two wrist views. Wrist cameras sit (40,−87,16) in each local gripper frame; left/right vertical fields are 78°/39°. Match the basis vectors defined in the scene's arm camera definitions by viewing the same dry reset from all cameras, logging relative alignment and crops. Use the same policy frames and processing as the simulation. For **grading only**, also record synchronized fixed side and top/oblique views, calibrated to a ruler and a vertical plumb line. Record stage motion at 60–120 fps and the dwell and other grading views at ≥30 fps. Use sufficient resolution to distinguish 1 mm over the calibrated work region and detached drops about 3.4 mm wide or larger at the spout in a commissioning pour. Record the 48 g mark, final 120 g line, water exit and flight path, cup rim, pitcher base and jaws, contact rings and tab. Place a matte contrasting dry background behind the spout/landing path, outside reach. Use additional closeup(s) if one field cannot resolve those features. Keep them separate from the policy cameras. Use a common visible sync flash before the trial. Verify clock drift below one recorded frame by a second flash at the end. An occluded or glare-obscured condition that could change the grade invalidates the measurement.

## Commission stage motion, liquids and scoring views

- **Mass recovery.** Tare the scale and check with traceable masses in the range of the vessel and collector masses. Separately deposit known *weighed* doses (0, 1.8, 2.0, 2.2, 9.5, 10.0, 10.5 and 40 g) onto the mug, hand, sleeve edge and other-spill pan. Repeat three times at each relevant boundary and challenge runoff, cross-bucket contamination and splashes onto covers. Compare recovered mass by bucket with weighed transfer and record worst absolute error including repeatability and display resolution. Target ≤0.2 g error around 2 g. Validate total mass balance to ≤0.5 g. Take initial/final scale photos promptly. Cross-contamination or a boundary that cannot be resolved makes a physical trial unscorable.
- **Receiver motion.** Calibrate the trigger mark by weight with the carriage stationary and a known camera view. Measure trigger-frame meniscus against 45, 48 and 55 g reference photos (bracketing marks may sit on the *grading* image). At first meniscus crossing of the 48 g line the examiner pulls smoothly once toward B and stops. Do not wait for a preferred arm pose. At each registered stop draw a second line exactly 1 mm inside the stroke. Film ruler and moving carriage index in the same plane. The onset frame is the outward crossing of the 1 mm A band; read the trigger-frame fill there. The completion frame is the first entry into the 1 mm B band. Time these crossings and also check that the carrier reaches and remains at the physical B stop. Endpoint-to-endpoint distance is measured independently of the optical bands. The ideal one-second smooth stroke takes about 0.871 s between the bands; a 60–120 fps recording or a commissioned near-uniform pull with approximately 1.00 s between bands can resolve the 0.85–1.15 s allowance. Accept 45–55 g at onset, 81–85 mm stroke, 0.85–1.15 s duration with B held for the remainder. For at least ten commissioning moves, alternate slow and nominal wet fills, then check for false wrist trips and spillage solely due to the manual stage move. The simulated smooth motion reaches about 13, 42 and 70 mm of the 83 mm stroke at quarter, half and three-quarter time. Compare the physical time history at these points and record the differences. The official moving trial has one outward stroke. No onset is expected if the robot never reaches trigger fill; evaluate that under the available serving evidence. An examiner failing to move after trigger, moving early, jerking the mechanism or resetting during the episode invalidates that trial. A positive, independently observed, latched robot interference with the examiner’s tab/guide is a robot fault; incidental contact with the static bench is not.
- **Discharge and model fidelity.** With the robot parked and the setup protected, use a dry inclinometer attached to a test cradle to set pitcher tilt at 0°, 20°, 30°, 40°, 50° and 60°. At 200, 120 and 60 g source fill, collect timed pours at selected angles into a weighed pan; run three repetitions. Film the first stream, the last discernible drop after returning upright, and first deposition location against a 5 mm grid at both receiver heights/positions. Record flow onset, collected mass per elapsed time, last-drop time and landing offset. Compare to simulator trajectories on the same geometry, pose and fill; record all discrepancies. Physical splash, slosh, changed wet grip, secondary runoff and scale uncertainty can change outcomes. These calibrations characterize fidelity and never add a microscopic flow threshold to the grade.
- **Dry reach and recording.** With a capped mass dummy, test handle insertion, lift, spout above both mug endpoints and intermediate positions, tilt, controlled lowering and final withdrawal. Film the dry route in calibrated views. Verify clearances with the robot stopped; never insert a ruler or person into a live work envelope. Check plan and side rulers with independent 3 mm and 35 mm spacers and pitch/roll/yaw templates of 3° and 6°. Film both a stopped pitcher and a visibly releasing drop. Correct ambiguous coverage before the first scored run.

## Record one trial

1. Record run ID, configuration, observer, dry or wet commissioning status, arm-base marks, initial A position, empty mug, clean dry collectors, reset indicator rings and 200.0 g initial water. Remove calibration weights, motion spacers and test jigs. Cameras record continuously through weighing. At reset there must be at least one clear view of each contact scale and of the pitcher resting on the counter.
2. Use the published instruction unchanged. The 1,200 actions at 20 Hz give a maximum of 60 s. Preserve the accepted actions and both receiver and robot trajectories. The examiner pulls only as calibrated. Record any apparatus fault and evidence of contact with the examiner tab/fairlead. A drop is latched if the unsupported pitcher descends more than 30 mm from its highest unsupported position before regaining support, including a later catch. Use a calibrated side view with the second view to establish loss of contact and descent. A supported lowering is distinct. The wrist displacement fault latches permanently when any retained ring reaches ±3 mm.
3. Stop safely if liquid reaches unprotected pads, housing, optical/electrical openings or escapes the containment system. Remove power under the lab’s normal isolation procedure before cleanup. Quarantine affected components for inspection before restarting. Record the event and any confirmed performance fault; if the stop leaves required measurements unavailable, mark the physical trial invalid. Do not continue a failed pour on real equipment to obtain a score. Deliberate drop and overload challenges belong in simulation; commission the *indicators* with the arms parked.
4. Grade the final **continuous two seconds**. Time the interval against camera timestamps; include both ends (41 synchronized observations on a 20 Hz record). Measure the available continuous dwell from the last relevant visible change to episode end; for an easy pass record two full seconds plus enough additional frames to cover clock uncertainty. Borderline timing is invalid unless resolved. Review the spout-to-landing region continuously for visible stream or falling drops throughout. Any visible discharge during that interval fails the dwell. Use paired top/side views, a registered vertical and light fiducials on the vessel (base center and two non-collinear marks on body/handle). Bound the maximum pairwise displacement and 3D orientation change across the entire window. The full spans must be ≤3 mm and ≤3°. A forward then backward movement counts even if the endpoint is unchanged. For clear passes, fixed registration outlines and 1 mm ruler ticks in two calibrated views can bound all three coordinate excursions below 1.7 mm and leave margin on the 3 mm combined limit. Use the two marked non-parallel body axes to assess orientation; for a borderline trial reconstruct from calibrated views, or report invalid. The figure for a single projected angle cannot establish 3D rotation in an ambiguous pose. Establish continuous support by the bench or the robot. For full credit, also check upright tilt≤6° from gravity, upright counter support, both jaws free of the vessel and nearest pitcher-to-*either-arm* distance ≥35 mm throughout. Projected gaps above 35 mm in a calibrated orthographic view can establish a conservative lower bound; verify perspective, occluded links and near misses with the second view. Record uncertainty and relevant frame numbers. A single end photograph cannot establish the dwell.
5. With all motion stopped, bag and group the preassigned collection materials by destination. For each of the resident and other-spill channels use one aggregate before reading and one aggregate after reading on the same scale (all bags or all removable liners of that group together; include bag mass in the tare). This gives two scale readings per channel. Preserve individual component photos to document allocation. If multiple sequential weighings are essential, include the additional reading uncertainties in the channel collection-error term. Report mug gain, all resident collector gains including dedicated gutter, and other-spill gains including the pan, other covers and splash screens. Weigh the pitcher before and after. Mass-balance residual equals mass lost by the pitcher minus mug, resident and other-spill gains combined. Check the absolute residual and possible attribution to each boundary using the commissioned error bounds. A missing collector or unresolved threshold crossing yields **invalid, repeat**. Treat capacity overflow or mixed runoff likewise. Do not assign unknown water to an arbitrary bucket.
6. Enter the unrounded measurements, commissioned uncertainties and video observations into the [physical grader template](bench/physical_trial.json). Archive the completed template, worksheet, scale photographs, ring closeups, side/top video and trial ID. Run the following from the task root.
