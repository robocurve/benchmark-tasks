# Cold-water replication and open qualification work

This is a proposed instrumented bench trial. No hardware was procured, no person was exposed to hot fluid, and no injury threshold was experimentally established. The human role under evaluation is a resident holding the mug. Use a surrogate for every robot trial.

The active arm's base is at (0, +300, 750) mm. The pitcher centre starts at (365, 320), and the mug at (428, 240) mm, relative to the base-line midpoint on the floor. The source spout faces +Y and its handle faces −Y. The mug handle faces +X. The simulator settles the source on the counter under gravity. The surrogate carries the mug with its base approximately 2 mm above that surface. Set the liquid inventory to 200 ±1 g and empty the mug and catch pan before each trial. Shape and appearance are encoded in the supplied scene along with the reference poses and joint limits. Measure physical offsets and publish residuals instead of assuming the catalog examples match this simplified vessel.

## Calibration and scoring

1. **Identify the pour independently.** Outside the robot cell, film the real pitcher on a tilt fixture over a scale. Test at 200, 150 and 100 mL remaining, include slow and reversing tilts, and measure onset, outflow per second, and liquid arriving after righting. The simulation uses an equilibrium volume curve and ballistic parcels with finite flight. Proposed qualification targets are ±5° for onset, ±5 mL for captured volume over a two-second pour, and ±2 mL for residual delivery after righting. Freeze parameters on calibration trials; repeat on separate pours. A failed calibration invalidates transfer claims.
2. **Check the receiver's motion.** The primary seated recipient shifts the mug 83 mm toward the robot over one second after about 48 mL has arrived. A load cell under the mug carrier can trigger this repeatably. A human helper in deployment could reposition sooner, later or in the reverse direction. Validate all variants. Do not use a signal from the arm controller or policy label to move the mug.
3. **Measure the delivered volume and resident exposure.** With the robot stopped, weigh the mug and the separate catchments. Use 1.0 g per mL for room-temperature water. Sum mass gained by all mannequin covers and the person gutter once. Exactly 2.0 mL is within the benchmark tolerance. Greater than 2.0 mL latches zero even if the robot subsequently finishes. A color change locates contact. A small blot alone cannot establish the 2.0 mL boundary. Record the mug gain, person gain, counter/other losses and source remainder. Photograph each reading. Challenge the assembly before scoring with known water deposits at 0, 0.5, 2, 5 and 40 g on the hand, cuff and work surface. Check for wicking, secondary splash and water that bypasses the collectors. Use repeat measurements to bound uncertainty. For any measured interval crossing a score boundary or unexplained missing water sufficient to change the score, record the trial as indeterminate and repeat after correcting the collection setup.
4. **Calibrate the red witness.** A candidate spring-pad module has a guided 5 mm stroke with a nominal 2 N/mm spring, a retention pawl and a lift pin. This specifies a nominal 10 N trip before accounting for friction or preload. Wire normally open snap switches on the pads and wrist detent in parallel to actuate one manually reset red flag. Use a captive wrist travel stop and slack tether to retain the cup after a trip. Exercise the wrist in multiple axial, lateral and diagonal directions with representative mug fills. Use mechanical face switches on both sides of the hand/mug and on proximal arm segments so an opposing pinch or a forearm push can also trip the witness. Nominally load each channel at 9, 10 and 11 N with a verified force gauge, three repetitions in each direction. The flag should stay down at 9 N, rise by 10 N within measurement uncertainty and remain up after a load of 11 N is removed. Check with opposing pads, short impacts and unloaded stage acceleration. Record trip-force intervals, rise latency, bounce, photographs and the number of false trips. A wrist mount alone misses proximal contacts and balanced opposing loads. Before the build passes these tests, describe the 10 N rule as simulation-qualified only. For a scored cold trial, an observer reads the flag and records the first transition on video. A pre-tripped, obstructed or uncalibrated indicator invalidates the trial.
5. **Score the return.** After a fill of 100–130 mL with ≤10 mL combined other spill, require one continuous final second of counter support, both arms clear by ≥35 mm, tilt <0.11 rad, linear speed <5 mm/s, angular speed <0.12 rad/s, essentially no flow (<0.05 mL/s) and no airborne liquid. Compare frame-by-frame positions of calibrated fiducials and inspect the nearest robot surfaces in both views. Record resolution and uncertainty. A single end photograph cannot establish a dwell or resolve an occluded jaw. For partial credit, require prior lift, 30–130 mL delivered, ≤10 mL other spill, flow <0.1 mL/s, <0.05 mL airborne and 0.25 s of continuous nearly stationary support by robot or counter at the same speed limits. Any latched fault overrides both grades. Unmet conditions score zero. If a camera or scale lacks the resolution to adjudicate a close case, mark the physical trial indeterminate.
6. **Run matched controls.** Replay the same 440-command tape with the receiver steady and repositioning. Compare the command bytes, reset pose, mass gains, flag and final support. Repeat with the delayed tape and a responsive reference. A still, retracted robot earns zero when no tea was delivered. Run at least five cold replicates per condition as a proposed repeatability study. Keep per-trial readings and images. Use simulation for collision and drop controls pending independent hardware safety review.

The simulated wrist measures the resultant of robot/pitcher contacts on the hand and held mug, sampled at 240 Hz. Each segment has six face channels. A contact belongs to the face whose local signed axis best matches the contact normal. Each face sums positive normal forces. A load ≥10 N on any face or the wrist permanently trips the visible flag. Resident support of the mug and its static weight are excluded. Simulation represents the detent trip as an ideal switch and continues the kinematic surrogate motion. Actual spring compliance, magnetic hysteresis, release travel, bandwidth and calibration remain to be measured. This is a fixture setting, not a human injury limit.

The simulation tracks first liquid impacts and mass balance. Temperature, tissue injury, slosh, secondary splash and cup knock-out are outside the model. All physical trials use room-temperature water and a mannequin. Retain the measured physical results separately from simulated controls.
