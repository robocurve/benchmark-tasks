# Tack-weld a T-joint square

A mild-steel base plate is clamped on a fixture table with a joint line scribed on it. A 3 x 3 x 1/4 in steel coupon lies flat on a block beside it and a TIG torch lies on its rest. The robot has to stand the coupon on its edge on the line, hold it square to the base plate with one hand, make three tack welds along the joint's root with the torch in the other (at the marks near each end, then the middle), put the torch back and take both hands away, leaving the coupon standing on its tacks. Instruction: "Tack the loose coupon to the base plate as a T-joint on the scribed line, square, with a TIG tack at each of the three marks. Leave it standing on its tacks with the torch back on its rest and your hands clear."

Install once, from this folder: `pip install -e . --no-deps`

Run a trial:

    inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_tack -E task=task.py -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 \
      -E cam_width=320 -E cam_height=176 --policy yam_scripted --no-prompt \
      --instruction "Tack the loose coupon to the base plate as a T-joint on the scribed line, square, with a TIG tack at each of the three marks. Leave it standing on its tacks with the torch back on its rest and your hands clear." \
      --scorer task_score --max-steps 1800 --seed 0

The score is printed as `task_score`. Use `--policy dream` for Dream-1.

- **Runtime per trial:** 4 to 5 minutes on one GPU for the scripted demo (834 steps, 42 s of simulated time), including about 1 minute to start the simulator.
- **Seeds:** none. The start is fixed and `object_jitter=0`; `--seed 0` was used for the demo.
- **Scoring checks, no simulator** (run in the Python environment that has `yam_arena`; Isaac Sim is not started): `python -m pytest -q test_score.py` (53 checks: every band, and each shortcut listed below). `python task.py` checks the demo's reach and length (772 steps) and that the torch's cup clears both plates at the root. `python dev/replay.py` plays the demo's plan with perfect tracking through the scorer, and `python dev/clearance.py` reports how close the arms' collision hulls come to the parts.
- **The scripted demo** is open loop. It uses only the fixed starting layout (where the coupon's block, the torch's rest, the scribed line and its marks are), which a policy can see, and reads nothing from the simulator. The right arm takes the coupon by its two saw-cut ends with the hand leaned 35 degrees one way, turns it 90 degrees about the fingers while carrying it (the hand ends leaned 55 degrees the other way), sets its rolled edge on the line and holds it there. The left arm takes the torch, which points along the joint at its 17.5 degree push angle, rolls it about its handle to a 45 degree work angle and, at each mark in turn (the two ends, then the middle), brings the tungsten to 3 mm from the root along the corner's bisector, dwells 2.6 s and backs off. It then lays the torch on its rest; the right hand opens and backs away along its fingers; both arms move away. The plan's aims carry fixed offsets against the arms' give under load (`HOLD_CAL`, `TORCH_CAL` in `task.py`), measured once from logged runs with `dev/sim.py`: with the torch in the rolled hand the tungsten's tip hangs 17 mm low, 11 mm away from the coupon and 6 mm back along the joint; the coupon's hand ends 3 mm short along the joint.
- **What the physics does:** the coupon, base plate, clamps, torch and hands are rigid bodies with real contact. The arc and its heat are bookkeeping (see `task.json`'s fidelity and the top of `task.py`), but the result of a tack is physical: when metal fuses, a joint between the coupon and the bench is set to the coupon's pose at that moment and switched on, so a coupon tacked out of square stays out of square, and one let go untacked is free to fall. The tack beads, the arc's glow and soot on a contaminated cup are shown from the score's record.
- **Shortcuts tried in the simulator** (`TACK_DEMO`, for checking the score; see Results): `none`, `one`, `lean`, `hover`, `held`, `stand` (the coupon is stood on the line and let go before tacking), `near` (the fingers open 4 mm clear of the coupon's ends and the hand stays round it, touching nothing, while it is tacked). `TACK_DEBUG=1` prints each step's measurements.
- **Third-party assets:** none. Every part is built from boxes, cylinders and simple meshes in the task's own code. The torch is the same real torch body as in the seam-welding task in this repository (`tig-seam`), with the tungsten set at 8 mm stick-out, so both tasks can share the equipment.

## The tack model and its numbers

The values are typical for manual TIG (GTAW) fusion tacks on 1/4 in mild steel with a 2.4 mm tungsten at about 130 A.

- **Root and root gap.** A fusion tack (no filler) only bridges plates that touch or nearly touch, so a tack can form only where a coupon edge lies on the base plate with a gap of 1 mm or less. A coupon held off the plate, or one end of a tipped coupon, cannot be tacked.
- **Arc length 1.5 to 4.5 mm,** tungsten tip to the root: a TIG arc is held at about one to one and a half electrode diameters. The simulated arc exists only there, with the torch in a hand. The real arc survives to 10 mm or more; the simulation is stricter.
- **1.5 s to fuse, 5 s to overheat.** At 130 A the pool takes a second or two to wet both plates of a 1/4 in T-joint; a welder's tack takes 1 to 3 s. Held three times too long, the tack grows oversize and undercuts the upright. On 1/4 in plate that is the realistic failure, not burn-through.
- **A tack about 10 mm long.** The arc heats the root within 4 mm either side of its nearest point.
- **Strength: 0.5 N m per degree, cracking at 2 degrees (1.0 N m).** A fused ligament about 4 mm long with a 1.5 mm throat has a plastic bending moment of about 400 MPa x 4 mm x (1.5 mm)^2 / 4 = 0.9 N m about the joint line. So each tack is a pin at its point with a bending spring that lets go at 1.0 N m. One tack alone is a weak hinge: about 13 N at the coupon's top edge folds it. Two or three tacks along the line pin each other, and can then only fold together about the line (about 40 N at the top edge for three). The coupon's own weight never loads a tack much (under 0.02 N m at 5 degrees of lean), as in the workshop: tacks fail by being knocked, not by gravity.
- **Shrinkage: 3 degrees, as a criterion.** A single-sided tack contracts as it cools and pulls an unrestrained plate toward its own side; 1 to 3 degrees is usual for a first tack on plate this size, which is why a fitter holds or braces the upright. Nothing thermal is simulated. Instead: if a tack sets while no gripper holds the coupon and no earlier tack holds it, the joint is set 3 degrees toward the tack's side and its spring pulls the coupon there. A held coupon, or one already tacked, is joined in the pose it has. Holding is real contact, not nearness: both fingers of one gripper (not the torch's) must press on the coupon with 0.5 N or more each, read from PhysX's contact forces between the coupon and each finger (the largest reading of the last 0.25 s; a holding hand in the demo presses with about 15 N a finger). A hand parked beside the coupon, or closed round it without touching, does not hold it. So a coupon stood on the line, let go and tacked one-handed ends out of square.
- **Contamination at 0.5 mm.** The tungsten touching either plate picks up steel; the arc then wanders and the electrode must be reground. Latched, and no arc after it.
- **The coupon's edge.** The coupon is cut from hot-rolled flat bar, whose rolled edges are rounded. The collider's rolled edges carry a 1.5 mm chamfer each side, so the coupon stands on a 3.35 mm flat and tips once it leans 2.5 degrees.

## Real-world setup

These are the trial's fixed settings, set once before a trial and not changed during it. The welds are three TIG (GTAW) fusion tacks, without filler, in the root of a T-joint between a 1/4 in (6.35 mm) mild-steel coupon and a 1/4 in base plate.

- **Power source:** a DC TIG inverter, DC electrode negative, high-frequency arc start, no pulse, 2T (hold-to-weld) torch-switch mode, 0.3 s downslope. The current is 130 A, confirmed before the trial: a welder makes three test tacks on scrap coupons of the same steel and preparation, each of which must fuse both plates in 1.5 to 3 s. If they do not, the current is changed in 10 A steps between 110 and 150 A and then fixed.
- **Arc control:** the robot operates no pedal, button or trigger. The trial supervisor, outside the curtains, holds a hold-to-run remote switch wired to the torch-switch socket. They close it only while the tungsten is within 10 mm of the joint's root at a mark (seen on the filtered camera view) and release it as the torch backs off, or at any time to stop. With the switch closed, the HF start strikes the arc when the tungsten is within a few mm of the steel. The length of each tack is therefore the robot's: it is the time the torch stays at the root. The simulation counts heating only with the tip 1.5 to 4.5 mm from the root, a stricter window than the real arc's.
- **Emergency stop:** one emergency-stop button beside the supervisor cuts both the robots' power and the inverter's output (through a contactor or the inverter's isolator); releasing the arc switch is the second stop.
- **Torch:** an air-cooled TIG torch rated 150 A or more, as in the setup: a 14 to 18 mm ceramic cup 25 mm long on a gas lens, and a 2.4 mm 2% lanthanated (or 2% ceriated) tungsten ground lengthwise to a point, set at 8 mm stick-out so that it reaches the corner with the cup about 3 mm clear of both plates. The torch has a superflex cable and gas hose hung from an overhead spring balancer above the fixture table; with the torch held at the joint, a spring scale at the grip reads under 1 N of extra force in any direction, so the hand carries about the simulated 255 g.
- **Shielding gas:** pure argon, 99.99% or better, at 10 L/min on a flowmeter regulator, 0.5 s pre-flow and 8 s post-flow. No fan or open door blows across the joint.
- **Filler:** none. A tack that does not bridge the root is not sound.
- **Coupon and base plate:** A36 or equivalent mild steel. The coupon is saw-cut from 3 x 1/4 in hot-rolled flat bar (mill rolled edges, deburred saw-cut ends); the base plate is 150 x 100 x 6.35 mm. Both are ground to bright metal 15 mm each side of the joint (the coupon's two rolled edges and the faces beside them; the base plate along the line) and wiped with acetone. The joint line, its two end ticks and three punch marks on the line (16.0, 38.1 and 60.2 mm from the first end tick) are scribed on the base plate after grinding, and the three white paint-marker ticks are drawn beside the punch marks, 10 mm clear of the line so no paint is in the weld.
- **Coupon's block:** a 40 x 70 x 30 mm steel block, so the coupon's two ends overhang and a hand can take it by them.
- **Welding circuit and grounding:** the steel fixture table stands on insulating pads on the station table, and the work (earth) clamp is fixed directly to it; the current reaches the base plate through the strap clamps and the coupon through its edge on the base plate. Both grips are insulating: the torch's glass-filled nylon block, and insulating heat-resistant pads on the holding hand's fingers (for example 3 mm silicone or mica-faced pads), checked with a meter before the trial (over 1 megohm from each finger pad to the arm's earth). No welding current can return through an arm.
- **Arc enclosure:** welding curtains or screens to EN ISO 25980, at least 1.8 m tall, close every open side of the cell. Nobody is inside the cell while the arc can strike. Anyone who looks at the arc through a viewing window uses a filter of shade 10-11.
- **Cameras:** each robot camera has a fixed shade 5 filter (or an auto-darkening filter with its dark state at shade 5-8) and a clear cover glass in front. The simulated cameras are unfiltered.
- **Heat and UV on the holding hand:** the fingers hold the coupon's ends 30 mm or more from the nearest tack. Three tacks put about 10 kJ into the two plates, which warms the 0.29 kg coupon by a few tens of degrees C overall; the finger pads are rated for 200 C or more. Aluminised sleeves cover both grippers and forearms against the arc's UV and radiant heat.
- **Fume extraction:** a local exhaust arm with its hood 20-30 cm from the joint, beside it and slightly above, set so that the test tacks show no porosity; general ventilation of the cell for ozone.
- **Fire protection:** the fixture table, block and rests are steel; a heat-resistant welding mat covers the station table round them; nothing combustible is inside the curtains; a CO2 or dry-powder extinguisher stands at the cell. After the trial the parts are left until they read under 50 C (IR thermometer or temperature crayon) and are moved only with tongs before then.
- **Electrical interference:** HF start radiates. The torch lead and work lead run together and as short as possible, straight down from the balancer to the torch, never along an arm. Robot and camera cables are shielded, with ferrites, and kept 30 cm or more from the torch lead except at the grip. Before the trial one test strike is made with the robots powered and recording, and they are checked for faults.
- **Loads:** the heaviest thing the robot moves is the 0.29 kg coupon. Nothing is pressed, clicked or triggered.

## Scoring: real measurement and simulated stand-in

The real trial is scored from the finished joint, left where it is on the fixture table, once it reads under 50 C. Tools: a 150 mm machinist's square, feeler gauges (1.0 and 2.4 mm leaves), a 150 mm steel ruler reading 0.5 mm, a weld gauge with an undercut pointer, a 10x loupe, a scriber, a lamp and a camera. Each tack and each end of the joint is photographed with the ruler in view, so a reading can be checked later.

The bands (numbers in `task.json`'s scoring): 0 with no sound tack, a part off the table, or a coupon that is not a T on the joint; 0.15 per sound tack while the fit is out of tolerance or a hand is still on the coupon; 0.2 + 0.2 per sound tack when the coupon stands by its tacks, hands clear, with the fit in tolerance; capped at 0.4 after a fault; 1.0 for three sound tacks, the fit in tolerance, no fault, the torch on its rest, both hands clear of coupon and torch and everything still for the last 1 s.

Not overheated (next row). | A fused piece of an uncracked tack within 6 mm of the mark along the line and 10 mm of the line. Fused: 1.5 s of arc on that 2 mm piece of root.
