# Reproduce the occupied receiving fixture

All dimensions are in mm. This is an inert mannequin fixture for comparing docking decisions. The published trajectories come from simulation. The following are proposed construction checks and acceptance targets. No physical build or repeatability result is claimed.

## Fixture datums and parts

Use the bench point under the receiver center as (0,0,0). Positive x is the withdrawal direction. A 220 × 390 × 3.4 steel web carries two 34 × 34 bosses, 10 high, with Ø18 through bores and a 3 × 45° underside lead-in. Locate the bosses at (40, ±61). Match the supplied plate and receiver DXFs. Use two rated, through-bolted handles at y=±135, grip center 41 above the underside, bolt holes Ø6.6 on 76 centers. Match the real hardware drawings and weigh the finished plate with backing plates to 3.00 ±0.03 kg.

Staging is centered at x=−220, top z=92. The receiver is centered at x=0, top z=69. Each bay has two 98 × 26 × 12 faces at y=±119. The receiver uses a round Ø12 pin and a relieved pin with flats at ±2.7, both with 9 of straight shank and a 6 high tapered nose. Tips are at z=84. Fully seated, the straight shank overlaps the straight bore by about 6. Mark the 4 mm engagement criterion with removable inspection marks or two side mirrors, one per bore. Use a known 2 mm shim under both seating faces to establish the 4 mm insertion image, then remove it and record the fully seated image. Demonstrate a one-pin perch in the calibration video. Pins visible through the holes plus both face gaps closed distinguish it from a seated plate.

The surrogate has a fixed shoulder at (600,0,450), hinged upper arm and forearm of 310 and 290 joint spacings (220 and 170 g), a 620 g wrist carriage at (150,0,77) at home, and two independent 45 g finger pairs. Each pair has 12 of vertical travel, nominal stiffness 2.50 N/mm, and carries two 72 × 17 × 17 coated bars. The 127 × 11 × 2 gauge leaf at home reaches x=40 near the round pin. Mark both finger groups, wrist carrier, elbow, cuff, working sleeve edges and gauge tip.

## Replace the powered stage with a cord and rail

| Part | Installation |
|---|---|
| One pair of commodity side-mount ball-bearing slides, nominal 300 mm | Mount vertically on parallel sidewalls as for a drawer, with **horizontal travel**. Join their moving members with a light crosshead. Fix positive stops 200 mm apart and reference both endpoints to the base. One example is the Everbilt 12 in full-extension set D80612C-ZP-W. [Slide dimensions and product](https://www.homedepot.com/p/302857902) |
| Nonstretch braided line, smooth fairlead, two stop blocks, ruler, tape, light shuttle and removable stop pin | Tie the line to the crosshead, route it parallel to travel then through the fairlead to an operator outside the work area. Mark the free end against a fixed ruler with the crosshead against each stop. Keep the line taut before starting. The operator supplies the pull. At the end, place the line in a cleat or clamp outside the work area. Lock home for the continuing profile. |
| Compliant wrist mounting | Fit a lightweight wrist shuttle to the crosshead on a short longitudinal slot with ±20 mm end stops. Center with two opposing, preloaded equal compression springs, each nominally 0.75 N/mm over the working stroke, combined 1.5 N/mm. Retain the springs through full travel. Adjust friction to prevent sticking. Put one high-contrast index on the wrist and a ±10 scale on the crosshead. Attach the jointed arm to the wrist, so restraint can displace the two indices relative to one another. |
| Ordinary recording supplies | Fixed plan and side cameras with a common flash in view, or one camera with a rigid side mirror. Use rulers at the plane of each measurement, marker tape, paper strips and a phone playing an audible timing cue. The stage replacement uses commodity parts. Record actual part numbers, costs, mass and measured behavior of the assembly. |

The drive is manually paced throughout its travel. It needs neither a powered stage nor a tuned pneumatic damper. Rehearse with the complete surrogate attached and the robots parked. Move the **input crosshead**, never pull on the glove. Draw against the graduated line without watching the robot. A second observer verifies the recording. At reset hold the input against home. Release the home retainer from outside the work area and begin at the assigned cue. Return to home between trials with the station stopped.

The input path is 200 u²(3−2u), with u equal to elapsed time divided by 8, clamped to [0,1]. Use nominal start 8.5 s, early start 0 s, or hold at home for the entire 30 s. Synchronize the cue and both views at episode start. Accept the rig only after five consecutive dry cycles per moving profile meet ±5 mm at every one-second mark, start and finish cues within ±0.15 s, monotone withdrawal within reading uncertainty, and both endpoints within 2 mm. Check the locked profile stays within 2 mm for 30 s. During a scored run check the **input** against the same targets and archive actual clearance separately. A mistimed operator pull invalidates the run. A deflected glove caused by the robot is a task outcome. These tolerances and the targets for the wrist coupling require physical commissioning.

Print the [bench rulers](fabrication/bench-rulers.pdf) at actual size. Verify the 100 mm reference before positioning the travel, height and displacement strips.

## Rule the two camera views

1. Tape the receiving rectangle **x=−49 to +49, y=−132 to +132**, enclosing both bearing faces. Tape the worker clearance line at **x=145**, or 35 beyond the technician-side boundary of the correctly seated plate (x=110). Mark the four underside corners and center height. Put side-view sight marks at z=37, 69, 84, **104**, 109 and 119. Choose an overhead orientation that resolves outline overlap. Use a second view or mirror to see the height simultaneously. Place scales in the plane of the measurement to control parallax.
2. Mark each digit with a separate bright index relative to the wrist. Zero with the full unloaded glove at rest. Put a fixed 3 mm travel line beside each. A 4:1 lightweight pointer can make that mark 12 mm apart on the display. Check at zero and with 2, 3 and 4 mm displacement spacers applied to each pair independently. Check wrist slip at 0, +10 and −10 relative to the moving input. These are **displacement** indications. No subtraction of robot versus plate forces, directions summed in newtons, or threshold for pain is part of grading. Native colored lamps repeat these frame decisions. The physical latch is the first marked video frame in the score sheet; a mechanical flag is optional.
3. Film at 20 fps or an integer multiple (e.g. 60 fps, take every third frame). The first scored observation is 0.05 s after the aligned start. Use 600 frames ending at 30.00 s. Use the flash for common frame selection. Retain original footage. Before scoring, show known offsets both sides of every mark with the plate and surrogate at representative depths. Aim for ≤1 mm uncertainty on close-ups of digit, engagement and seats, ≤2 mm on larger marks. Use magnified side views or mirrors if needed. When an occlusion, missing frame or measurement uncertainty could reverse the decision, label the trial unscorable and repeat.
4. Record the first frame and side(s) of any finger travel ≥3, relative wrist slip ≥10 in either longitudinal direction, or plate center underside below 37. Also latch low occupied entry on the first frame with **all three** of (i) projected plate touching/overlapping the receiving rectangle, (ii) lowest of four underside corners below 104, and (iii) a working limb or gauge point less than 35 past x=110. Count a re-entry while low. Observe the glove, sleeve and gauge even after the input has passed its last pacing mark. Any recorded fault gives final zero, including after a successful landing. Pure axial displacement is what the wrist mark detects. Other contact alone earns no fault.
5. For half credit mark both robot-to-handle grasps, daylight from other supports, pickup to 119, hover center radius 45, tilt ≤6° and center underside ≥109. Across **11 frames** (0.50 s elapsed), no individual corner may span more than 10 along any axis. After a successful hover, an incomplete alignment can retain that credit at the end under the conditions in the README. For full credit check both pins, actual support on both faces, release, center radius 25, tilt ≤4°, center z=69 ±3, no other support. The robot keep-clear box is x=[−330,110], y=[−195,195], z=[35,160]. Verify ≥35 mm shortest separation from this box to both arms' distal link, gripper and fingers using the two views and rulers. Parked jaw opening is unrestricted. Each corner must span ≤2 mm per axis across **31 frames** (1.50 s elapsed) with full conditions continuously present through episode end.
6. For seating, put a thin equal paper feeler at the outer edge of each face, with tabs visible to its side mirror. Establish the unloaded, perched and both-seated reference appearances. Confirm bearing on both tabs at rest by a light pull from outside the work area on each tab after the timed dwell. The plate must remain released with no other supporting surface. A free tab, ambiguous support or an occluded pin cannot establish a successful seating. Use staging tab checks at reset too. Keep all people outside the robot work area throughout motion.

Keep a worksheet with run ID, profile, input positions and actual times, camera scale and time offsets, first fault frame, first clear frame, first registered pin, final supports, dwell interval, score and both uncut videos. Repeat the nominal safe and early-entry controls and the early/continuing worker interventions. A pair of readers should independently agree on the outcome and first event within one scored frame. Record failures and spread. The procedure has yet to be demonstrated on hardware.

For frame-by-frame dry-run checks, export the crosshead ruler reading at frames 0–600 as a CSV with columns `frame,input_mm`. Run `python3 controls/check_slide.py nominal dry01.csv` (or `early` or `continuing`). It tests the entire measured input path, reversals and endpoints. Read the pin-release and end-stop cue times separately from the synchronized video. Save the five repetitions with the fixture record.
