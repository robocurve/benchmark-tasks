# Pop an ORCA robot hand's dislocated thumb joint back in: both bearings into their arc grooves at once

An open-source ORCA Hand v2 hangs fingers down from a printed stand, powered off, its thumb standing out level from the palm. Its thumb's tip joint has popped out: the distal phalanx hangs off the end of the proximal phalanx on its two slack tendons, 4 mm out of the joint, cocked 30 degrees down and 10 degrees about the thumb's axis. One gripper pins the proximal phalanx, whose base joint on the palm (the CMC) turns freely with the power off. The other slides its lower finger under the hanging tip, lifts it into line as its jaws close on the tip's top and bottom, squares it and lines the two bearings up with their grooves, then presses the tip along the thumb in 0.3 mm steps, turning out any pitch, roll or yaw the tip takes in the pinch before each next step, until both bearings snap in. Both let go, the hand is powered, and the thumb flexes its tip 70 degrees and back.

## Why it matters

Repairing the dexterous hands that robot-learning data is collected with is robot building and upkeep that AI progress depends on: a hand that is down stops its robot's data collection. AgiBot World's over 1 million trajectories came from 100 dual-arm robots fitted with either a gripper or a 6-DoF dexterous hand (AgiBot World, 2025). Robot building is scaling: Goldman Sachs projects 1.4 million humanoid robots shipped a year by 2035, a $38 billion market (2024), and 542,000 industrial robots were installed worldwide in 2024 (IFR, 2025). The ORCA Hand is built for this use (its paper's title: "for Uninterrupted Dexterous Task Learning"), and its joints are made to pop out in a crash and be pressed back in by hand.

## Run

From this folder:

```
python demokit.py task.py OUT                      # final film, 640 x 360, GPU to yourself
python demokit.py task.py OUT --no-record --mode full,none,no_hold,cocked,poke,hover,slam,pull,yawed,slam_held,knock,yaw_hold,regrip_seated,release_raw,slam_dwell,slam8,nudge_up,hover_hold
```

The harness's instruction (Dream-1 run): `--instruction "Put the popped-out thumb tip of this robot hand back into its joint, then let go."`

Runtime: 688 steps (34.4 s of sim time); 2 to 3 minutes of wall time per trial on an RTX 4070 Ti SUPER (134 to 187 s in three runs), and 115 s on an RTX 5090, plus about 1 minute of start-up. Nothing is random; no seed is needed. Robustness runs: `OR_JIT=pitch,roll,yaw,x,z` (degrees, mm) shifts the tip's hanging rest, e.g. `OR_JIT=5,-4,3,0.6,-0.5 python demokit.py task.py OUT --no-record --mode full`.

| slam_dwell | as slam_held, with a 1 s pause at the start of the last 3 mm before the strike | 0.5 (SLAM: the pause does not reset the 7-step window) |

Hold cap: if T-PP's middle moves more than 5 mm from its start at any moment, the score is at most 0.25. 0.25: neither bearing seated, but the tip in line with T-PP (within 10 degrees in pitch and roll) at T-PP's end, both bearings within 1.5 mm of their grooves' line. 0.5: both bearings seated, each within 0.8 mm of its groove's line and 3.6 mm in, the tip within 3 degrees in roll and yaw; the closed joint is a free hinge on slack tendons, so how far the released tip droops does not count (the real trial reads this with the feeler gauge alone). 1.0: also both grippers at least 5 cm from the thumb, for 1 s before the flex test and at the end, and the flex test passes: the powered thumb turns the tip at least 60 degrees about the joint and returns it within 5 degrees. A tip slammed in scores at most 0.5: a slam is the tip's 3 mm of travel up to the pop (the bearings from 3.4 to 0.4 mm out) covered within the last 7 physics steps before the pop (29 ms at 240 a second, a mean of 0.1 m/s or more), read at every physics step, not at the 20 Hz control step; a pause earlier in the press does not reset it. The 0.1 m/s is an estimate of what cracks a printed lip, with a range of 0.05 to 0.2 m/s: at 0.1 m/s a 1 to 3 kg hand or robot wrist pushing the tip carries 5 to 15 mJ into the pop, as much as the 14 mJ the 8 N lip takes to seat (half of 8 N times 3.6 mm). A seated joint pulled out along the thumb harder than the pop force, or pushed sideways (across the thumb, along the bearings' axis; a push up or down does not unseat it) with more than 3 N (the most the arm pushes sideways), pops out again, as the real joint is built to, and the run is scored as it ends. The tiers are computed from the state at the end of the run; only the hold cap and the slam cap hold once met. Which arm holds, the grip, the approach and the number of presses are free. Possible scores: 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0. Both bearings must be in to count: one bearing in alone has no tier. At the 3 degree yaw limit the two bearings stand up to 0.8 mm apart in depth, within the 0.8 mm line tolerance; the joint closes once both are 3.6 mm in.

## Real trial

The hand: an ORCA Hand v2 built from the published files, clamped by the back of its palm to a printed stand on the bench (a 25 mm hose clamp, about $2, round the post and the back of the palm), hanging fingers down, powered off. Reset: slacken the thumb tip's two tendons (on the ORCA hand, loosen them at their spools, ORCA's documented way to pop a joint out, Sec. II-A of the paper; on the printed thumb, lift the 200 g weight off the flexor's free end and untie the extensor's rubber band at the stand), lift the tip's bearings out of their grooves, set it hanging 4 mm out, cocked about 30 degrees down, and leave the tendons slack. Judge the hold by the side video against a 150 mm steel rule taped upright to the post level with T-PP, read beside T-PP's middle so there is no parallax (T-PP moved over 5 mm caps the score), by a 0.5 mm feeler gauge at both sides of the joint (a side is seated when the gauge will not enter it; both sides seated: 0.5), for a slam (at most 0.5), by the side video filmed in the phone's 240 frames-a-second slow motion, framed so about 60 mm of the steel rule fills the picture (about 20 pixels a mm): count the frames from the tip's end standing 3 mm short of where it snaps in, read against the rule, to the frame the joint snaps shut; 7 frames or fewer (29 ms, a mean of 0.1 m/s or more) is a slam, the same count the simulator makes at its 240 physics steps a second, and by the flex test: re-tension the tendons, power the hand and command the thumb's tip to flex 70 degrees and back; a dislocated tip swings loose instead. A cracked groove lip or a tendon pulled off its anchor fails the flex test. The main route is a printed thumb (T-TP, T-AP, T-PP and T-DP from the same files, two MR84 4 x 8 x 3 mm bearings pressed onto T-DP's printed axle stubs, a braided tendon) mounted on ORCA's own printed right palm (`orca_v2/base/02_Carpals/R-Carpals.stl` and `CORE-R.stl` from the hardware files), which carries the thumb's CMC as on the hand (assembled as in ORCA's assembly tutorial and `ASSEMBLY_ADDENDUM.md`), held at rest by a #33 rubber band (89 x 3 mm), its tie point on the stand moved until the CMC's rate reads 0.45 to 0.55 N m/rad (a luggage scale hooked on the thumb 80 mm from the CMC axis and pulled square to it until the thumb has turned 30 degrees from rest against the protractor reads 300 to 370 g; rate = force x 0.08 m / 0.52 rad), on the same stand, with a 200 g weight hung on the flexor tendon and lifted off as the flex test (the extensor's elastic brings the tip back); the ORCA hand above is the alternative. Before the first trial, measure the pop force once, the cheap real check of the simulator's 8 N, which is an estimate: push the squared tip in by hand through a 50 N push-pull gauge (an analogue one costs about $30) and read the peak at the pop, then pull it back out through the gauge and read that peak too. A joint that needs over 12 N, past the range the demo was shown to seat, is not used: print another T-PP. Check the groove tolerances on the same printed joint: rock the squared tip in roll and yaw and note the largest angle at which it still pops in, against the simulator's 3 degrees for each. The tendons run as on ORCA's thumb, from T-DP's anchors back through T-PP's tendon channels (ORCA's assembly guide). For the flex test, the 200 g weight hangs on the flexor's free end below the palm, and the elastic band is tied to the extensor's free end at the stand. A phone on a tripod, level with the joint, films the press. Bill for the printed route, about $100: PLA for the thumb, the palm and the stand (about 450 g, $12, with a spare T-PP), two MR84 bearings ($2), braided tendon line ($10), two #33 rubber bands ($1), a luggage scale reading in 10 g steps ($10), a 0.5 mm feeler gauge ($8), a 150 mm steel rule ($3), a protractor ($3), a 200 g weight ($5), a 50 N push-pull gauge ($30), a phone tripod ($10) and thin silicone sleeves for both grippers' pads ($10). The 1.5 mm, 5 degree and 0.1 m/s figures are the simulator's readings of the feeler-gauge, flex and snap checks; the 0.25 rung is read in the real trial from the side video: the tip within 10 degrees of T-PP's line against a protractor held beside the joint, its bearings at the grooves' mouths with a gap under 1.5 mm, read against the 8 mm bearing itself (a gap under a fifth of its width).

## Modelling

Key dimensions, all at true size:

| Part | Size here | Source |
|---|---|---|
| T-PP (proximal phalanx) | 18 x 19 x 49 mm | bounding box of ORCA v2 `T-PP.stl` |
| T-DP (distal phalanx) | 16 x 15 x 38 mm | bounding box of ORCA v2 `T-DP.stl` |
| Joint bearing | 4 x 8 x 3 mm | ORCA `ASSEMBLY_ADDENDUM.md` |
| T-DP axle stub | 3.9 mm across, 3 mm long | read from `T-DP.stl` |
| Bearing spacing | 12 mm centre to centre, 15 mm outer face to outer face | from the stubs' positions in `T-DP.stl` |
| T-PP groove | 8.2 to 9 mm across | read from `T-PP.stl` |
| Whole hand | about 1.2 kg | ORCA paper |

- T-PP and T-DP are ORCA v2's own STL files at true size (T-PP 18 x 19 x 49 mm, T-DP 16 x 15 x 38 mm, bounding boxes read from the files), reduced to about 1,700 and 1,100 vertices for rendering (sizes unchanged); their colliders are convex hulls. The rest of the hand is ORCA's own CAD at true size (`orca_hand.npz`, built by `buildhand.py` from the orcahand_description v2 URDF and its STLs, MIT, made from the CC BY 4.0 hardware files): the palm (carpals and skin), the four fingers at rest (curled 0.55 rad at the knuckle and 0.75 rad at the middle joint), and the thumb's two base pieces T-TP and T-AP. Every piece is posed by the URDF's forward kinematics, in T-PP's own frame, so T-PP sits on the palm exactly where the CAD puts it. The colliders of the palm, fingers, T-TP and T-AP are ORCA's own convex collision pieces from the same package (8 for the palm, 6 a finger, 1 each for T-TP and T-AP). The stand (post and foot) is plain boxes. T-DP's two bearings are drawn at true size (4 x 8 x 3 mm steel rings, per ORCA's assembly notes) on T-DP's own printed axle stubs, which T-DP.stl has at both sides of its base (3.9 mm across, 3 mm long, so the bearings sit 12 mm apart centre to centre, their outer faces 15 mm apart); they turn with the tip, and T-DP's collider is the convex hull of T-DP and both bearings. The grooves have no geometry: seating is a code rule on the tip's pose.
- The thumb's mount, from ORCA's URDF: the CMC joint sits 30.6 mm from the wrist joint along the palm, 28.4 mm to the index side of the palm's centre line and 11.0 mm toward the palm side, its axis (0.568, 0.379, -0.731) in the palm's frame (x across to the little finger, y out of the palm, z along the fingers); its range is -0.79 to 0.58 rad. T-TP, T-AP and T-PP are held at the rest angles CMC -0.55, abduction -0.20 and MCP 0.55 rad, and turn as one on the CMC (range -13 to 64 degrees from rest), 31 mm behind and 20 mm below T-PP's base. The hand is placed so T-PP lies level, its bearings' axis along x; the palm then faces -y with the fingers down.
- The thumb is one articulation on the fixed hand; its pieces touch the hand only through the CMC joint (contacts within the hand are off). Powered off, the CMC turns against its slack tendons: a spring of 0.5 N m/rad about the rest pose, set so the tip's weight leaves it at rest, and 0.3 N m s/rad damping (estimates). The dislocated tip hangs on its slack tendons, modelled as a tether (it holds the tip near its seat but cannot seat it: with the arms idle, mode `none`, the tip stays out and scores 0.0) of six soft PhysX joint drives about the bearings' centre: sideways and up and down 100 N/m (a loose leash: the 20 g tip sags 2 mm at rest, past the 0.8 mm seat limit, so the hands must line it up; limits 6 mm sideways, 8 mm up and down, 30 degrees roll and yaw), roll and yaw 0.05 N m/rad, pitch about the bearings' axis 0.03 N m/rad (estimates). The tip weighs 20 g; its weight is a constant force at its centre of mass.
- The grooves are code acting on those joints: a bearing pushed in while off its groove's line (more than 0.8 mm, or the tip more than 3 degrees in roll or yaw, or 12 in pitch) stops on the rim 1.5 mm in. These are estimates, chosen against the groove's size: T-PP.stl's arc grooves are 8.2 to 9 mm across for the 8 mm bearings, and the seating rule reads two points 7.5 mm each side of the thumb's line (the edges of T-DP's 15 mm base; the drawn bearings sit 6 mm each side), so 3 degrees of roll or yaw moves one about 0.4 mm and puts the two up to 0.8 mm apart in depth. The real trial checks them on the printed joint; in line, the groove's lip resists the bearings' entry with a spring rising to 8 N at the pop (an estimate: ORCA publishes no pop force; 4 to 12 N gives the same task: the scripted demo scores 1.0 with the lip set to 4, 8, 10 and 12 N (`OR_POP=4`, and so on), while at 15 N its press stalls 2.8 mm in and scores 0.0; the real trial measures it once with the push-pull gauge). The lip's spring is a PhysX drive on the tip's slide along the thumb, so the pop comes only from the gripped tip being pushed past it through the fingers' contacts. When both bearings are fully in, the joint closes: the tether becomes a plain hinge about the bearings' axis, as the real joint is. A closed joint pops out again when the tip is pulled out along the thumb with more than the pop force for 0.1 s (read from the closed joint's stiff slide): the tether comes back, the lip pushes the bearings out, and the joint can be pressed in again once they are past the rim. A pull along the thumb, or a sideways push of more than 3 N (the most the arm pushes sideways; read from the closed joint's stiff side slides) for 0.1 s, pops it out; a bend does not.
- The flex test is a drive on that hinge, switched on once both grippers are clear.
- The demo reads the thumb's joint angles, including the tether's, to square the tip and line the bearings up (the tip's pose against its seat), and again at every control step of the press. A pinch is a pivot: pressed straight in, the tip turned 3 to 11 degrees in the fingers and stopped on the rim; pressed in steps with the turn corrected, it stayed within 0.3 degrees.
