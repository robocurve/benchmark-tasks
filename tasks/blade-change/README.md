# Replace a blade in a quick-release holder and check it is retained

A maintenance procedure on an unpowered, bench-held training holder with a twist-release collar:
keep the spring-return collar turned with one gripper while the other withdraws the worn blade,
stows it and seats the replacement; let the collar return so the pin locks the blade; then pull
on the blade to verify it is retained. The final job is a verified mechanical replacement, not
cutting. The release-and-check principle follows twist-collar blade clamps on reciprocating saws
(for example the collar, spring return and tug check described in a Milwaukee Quik-Lok operator
manual); the holder here is an original simplified design, not a replica of that product.

The task is a task-local plugin for the `yam_arena` adapter of
[yam-isaac-lab-arena](https://github.com/kairosiann/yam-isaac-lab-arena) (YAM arms at the calibrated
`station`; Isaac Lab-Arena 0.3.1 / Isaac Lab 3.0 / Isaac Sim 6.1). It adds the embodiment
`yam_blade_change` (the adapter's robot, cameras and 14-D joint action/observation contract,
unchanged, plus the holder mechanism and blades) and the scripted demonstration policy
`blade_change_scripted`. `task.py` lists the fixed rack, tray and rail.

## Instruction

`instruction.txt` is the full text manual used in both recorded evaluations. The
`task.json` instruction is a compact version of the same procedure for the board’s
1,000-character field. The evaluated manual specifies: which blade to install (blue, white mark), how to release (turn the collar lever toward
the arm bases about 35 degrees and keep it turned), the orientation (black edge down, notched tang
first), the seat reference (shoulder against the holder nose), the lock (let the collar return)
and the retention check (pull the blue end block until the carriage moves 6 mm for half a second,
with the other hand open and clear of the mechanism).

## Setup

Coordinates: x forward from the line between the arm bases, y to the left, from their midpoint;
heights above the table.

| Part | Size | Mass | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| holder (grey channel, entry chamfer) on a spring carriage | 2.4 x 6 x 3.2 cm | 0.40 kg | axis along y at x 30 cm, 7 cm high; nose face at y 2 cm |
| collar (orange) with lever tab and cam peg | ring 2 cm radius; tab tip 5 cm from the axis | 0.08 kg | at the nose |
| locking pin (red) with follower arm | 8 x 8 mm pin | 0.04 kg | in the holder, over the tang notch |
| worn blade (grey, red mark), installed | 6 mm thick; body 17 x 3 cm, tang 5 x 1.6 cm with a 10 x 6 mm notch in its top edge; grip block 2 x 3 x 3 cm | 0.06 kg | seated |
| new blade (blue, white mark) | same | 0.06 kg | in a two-fork rack at x 40 cm |
| rest tray (green) | 9 x 22 cm inside, 2 cm walls | fixed | (20, -10) cm |

The intended build uses steel for the holder/carriage/rail, pin and springs, and polymer
for the collar/lever, blunt training blades and moulded grips, rack and tray. Masses and
contact coefficients are authored nominal parameters, not measurements of a commercial saw.
The rail is 5 x 12 x 2 cm, centred at (30, 4) cm and 1 cm above the table. Rack fork
centres are (40, -3) and (40, -12) cm: each has a 2 x 1.2 x 2.5 cm base and two
0.4 x 1.2 x 1.2 cm posts, leaving an 8 mm gap. The tray has a 9.8 x 22.8 x 0.4 cm
floor and 4 mm walls. Exact original geometry is generated by `task.py` and
`blade_change/assets.py`.

## Mechanism (original design; declared springs)

- **Collar:** revolute about the insertion axis, travel 0 to -40 degrees between stops, return spring
  0.0025 N m/deg with 6 degrees of preload against the rest stop.
- **Locking pin:** vertical guide in the holder, spring 300 N/m with 0.9 N preload onto its engaged
  stop; the pin's flat faces stop the tang both ways (no lead-in: a blade cannot push it open).
- **Cam:** a square peg on the collar's side lifts the pin's follower arm by contact; at 35 degrees
  of collar travel the pin rises 14 mm, clear of the 6 mm notch.
- **Carriage (tug gauge):** the holder slides along the axis on a rail, held on its rear stop by a
  150 N/m spring with 0.3 N preload, travel 20 mm. Pulling a retained blade extends it: 6 mm
  corresponds to about 1.2 N of static spring force (150 N/m times 8 mm including preload). The same
  reading can be taken on real hardware with a ruler and a spring gauge.
- Springs and stops are PhysX joint drives and limits. Pin-holder and collar-holder contacts are
  disabled (their bores are not modelled); every other contact is ordinary rigid-body contact.
  Nothing is teleported, welded or driven by the task state.

## Scoring

`blade_change/scoring.py`, reported as `info["score"]` for `--scorer task_score`. Inputs are
measured: blade poses relative to the holder, collar angle, pin lift, carriage extension, part
speeds, gripper openings and TCP distances to the lever, holder, pin and blades.

| Score | Condition (current state unless noted) |
|---|---|
| 0.25 | worn blade out of the holder, its middle inside the tray, at most 4 cm high |
| 0.5 | and new blade seated: shoulder -2.5..+1.0 mm from its seat (it bears at -0.9 mm; the notch gives 1 mm of play), within 1.2 mm of the axis and 8 degrees in roll/yaw/pitch (notch up) |
| 0.75 | and locked: pin within 1 mm of its engaged stop, collar within 3 degrees of rest |
| 1.0, success, trial ends | and the retention check has been passed (carriage extended at least 6 mm for 0.5 s while seated and locked, a gripper 10-50% open with its TCP within 2 cm of the blue end block, and the other gripper at least 80% open with its TCP 6 cm from the holder centre, collar lever and pin follower), then both grippers at least 80 % open and 6 cm from the lever and blades, all parts below 1 cm/s, for 2 s |

Unlocking, unseating or invalid measurements invalidate a prior retention check. The tug grasp is
a geometric proxy from measured finger openings and TCP positions; it does not measure contact force.
Either arm can pull.

The installed start scores 0; an upside-down blade is not seated; a wrist pull without carriage
extension is not a retention check; non-finite states score 0 for that step. CPU checks:
`python -m pytest tests` in this folder.

## Running

Install the pinned adapter, then this package (from the repository root):

```bash
mkdir -p .deps
git clone https://github.com/kairosiann/yam-isaac-lab-arena.git .deps/yam-isaac-lab-arena
git -C .deps/yam-isaac-lab-arena checkout 0059d02a1a91bc6834c1d0846022891df0c78730
ARENA_DIR="$PWD/.deps/IsaacLab-Arena" .deps/yam-isaac-lab-arena/setup.sh
source .deps/IsaacLab-Arena/yam.env
export ISAACSIM_ASSET_ROOT=https://omniverse-content-production.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/Assets/Isaac/6.0
uv pip install --no-deps -e tasks/blade-change
```

Scripted demonstration, two episodes with a reset between (run from `tasks/blade-change`; parts are
generated into `assets/generated/`):

```bash
cd tasks/blade-change
inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_blade_change --policy blade_change_scripted \
  -E control_hz=20 -E cam_width=320 -E cam_height=176 \
  --instruction "$(python -c 'from pathlib import Path; print(Path("instruction.txt").read_text().strip())')" --scorer task_score --max-steps 1200 --epochs 2 \
  --store-frames --log-dir runs/blade-demo
```

- `-E telemetry=<file.jsonl>` writes the per-step mechanism state and score.
- Comparison modes: `-P mode=closed` (pull with the collar untouched), `-P mode=early` (collar
  released while the tang is half out), `-P mode=push` (collar released before inserting).
- Run limit 1,200 steps (60 s at 20 Hz); one fixed layout, no randomisation.

The demonstration is privileged: it reads part poses (including where the replacement sits in the
hand, to aim the tang), and commands ordinary joint targets through the adapter's IK.

## Comparisons (scripted, this model)

- Pulling with the collar closed: the carriage runs to its 20 mm stop and the blade moves only its
  1.9 mm of notch play; it stays locked.
- Pushing the replacement in with the collar closed: the tang stops against the pin, shoulder
  24.7 mm short of the seat.
- Letting the collar return while the tang is 18 mm out: the pin lands on the tang's top edge and
  the blade still slides out. The pin blocks withdrawal only while the notch is under it (the first
  ~5 mm), so the collar must be held through the start of removal and the whole insertion.
- The lever and the blade grip are about 14 cm apart along the axis, so one gripper cannot hold the
  collar and move the blade at once. This does not prove every one-arm strategy impossible.
