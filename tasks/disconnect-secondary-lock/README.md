# Disconnect an automotive harness connector with a secondary lock

Slide the red secondary lock toward the wires. Hold down the rear latch while pulling the plug straight out of the mounted cap. Set the plug down with its leads attached.

## Impact on jobs

Automotive electrical diagnosis and repair routinely involves uncoupling harness connectors to inspect or replace a terminal, seal, sensor, or wiring branch. ASE's automobile A6 task list includes testing and repairing harness connectors and terminals in lighting, instrumentation and body electrical circuits. Its truck guide also lists servicing connector terminals, pins, seals and locks on electronic engine controls. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics counted about 825,800 automotive service technicians and mechanics and 316,000 diesel service technicians and mechanics in 2025. These figures describe the two occupations. The AMPSEAL 16 pair in this task is one representative instance of the service skill. TE illustrates moving the captive connector position assurance slider (CPA) toward the wire end, pressing the rear latch pad, and maintaining that pressure while pulling on the rear housing. Wires exit both halves. A stock reverse-mount cap and its mounting clip reproduce a stationary chassis connection on a de-energized workbench. A narrow, blunt service pick can reach the stock CPA ridge and rear release pad. Automotive service guidance documents a small pick or screwdriver as a hand tool for tight connector-lock access. Crimp the contacts to the wires according to TE 114-13045. TE lists hand tool 2217753-1 for these contacts. Insert sockets into plug cavities 1–4 and pins into matching cap cavities 1–4, through the installed wire seals, then fully seat both TPAs. Confirm all eight leads are retained with the light pull specified in TE 114-13065. Insulate the cut far ends individually and leave them unconnected to any power source. The CPA is the small rear slider used in the trial. [TE tooling and contacts](https://www.te.com/content/dam/te-com/documents/industrial-and-commercial-transportation/global/ict-ampseal-16-cat-a4-7-1773982-8-en-2010.pdf) · [TE assembly steps](https://assets.sourcengine.com/datasheets/e62f6501-8ea1-41b4-bfff-c5f1e04eb069.pdf#page=4)

Slide the 17.65 × 23.60 mm stock clip onto the cap’s underside rail until its lock seats. Mark the two mounting posts from that assembled cap, 12.20 mm apart along the mating axis. Drill and tap the M8 fir-tree hole with at least 6 mm of thread in the plate and its backing, and drill the 5 mm anti-rotation hole. Leave clearance under both holes for the exposed posts. Fasten the plate to the board with countersunk screws at x=−14 and +14 mm, y=+8 and +35.5 mm. The nominal plate center is x=0, y=+21.75 mm. Counterbore or shim the plate seat until the installed connector mates horizontally with the plug supported on the 4 mm reset rest. Verify that the cap does not rotate or move during a light axial pull on the plug body. [TE clip mounting drawing](https://www.te.com/commerce/DocumentDelivery/DDEController?Action=srchrtrv&DocFormat=pdf&DocLang=English&DocNm=1924487&DocType=Customer+Drawing&PartCntxt=1924487-2)

For reset, mate the housings with their stock keying aligned until the primary latch seats. Slide the CPA forward into its fully engaged position. Confirm by hand that the primary latch is blocked at reset and the housings do not come apart under a light axial pull. Leave the rear latch pad and CPA exposed above the board.

### Bench layout

Mark the coupled cap mouth as the planar origin. The cap extends in +y and the plug wire exit points in −y. Mark +x across the housings toward the cable bows. The cap mouth’s centerline is 15.2 mm above the worktop in the simulator. Fix two 4 mm-thick level plastic strips to the tabletop. Both strips are 61 mm wide along x and centered on the connector axis.

| Rest | Center y | Length along y |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Plug reset rest | −11.5 mm | 23 mm |
| Plug release rest | −67 mm | 72 mm |

Secure the four wires of each far end with a 10 mm-clear nylon tie or insulated P-clip, centered on x=0. Put the plug clip at y=−145 mm and the cap clip at y=+154 mm. At both clips the bundle center is 5.1 mm above the worktop. Leave approximately 168 mm of wire exposed from the plug cover to its clip and 152 mm from the cap cover to its clip. Leave enough additional length for contact termination and insulated tails beyond the clips. Bow the plug bundle toward x=+48 mm near y=−106 mm and the cap bundle toward x=+45 mm near y=+124 mm. The slack lets the plug move back along the mating axis and reach its rest while both far ends stay routed. Start with the stock actuators facing up and the plug supported on the reset rest. On the stock YAM station the coupled cap mouth is at x=310 mm, y=0. Local +x and +y in the layout correspond to station −x and −y, so the plug points toward station +y and the wires bow toward station −x. Center the pick handle and its tool rest at station x=215 mm, y=−144 mm. The shaft points 30° from station +x toward +y with the blunt toe pointing down. Use the blunt end on the inboard face of the red ridge and move it toward the wires along the mating axis. After the CPA seats open, use the tool to depress the rear latch without levering against the cap. Keep that pressure on the latch during the initial straight withdrawal. Set the free plug on its rest and move both hands clear. The pick holder is available for staging the tool.

## Scoring and physical checks

Place a ruler beside the seam, parallel to the mating axis, and record a top-oblique view with the CPA, latch pad and seam visible. Put a small washable witness mark on the plug’s rear latch pad and a corresponding mark on the fixed shell. Review the marks when the pad is pressed and as the seam opens. On the physical connector, confirm a closed CPA blocks pad motion and a light axial pull cannot separate the housings. With the CPA open and the pad untouched, repeat the pull to check the primary latch still holds. Pad motion followed by straight withdrawal while depressed demonstrates functional clearance. The internal travel and force values below describe the simulation proxy.

- **0.25** when the CPA has moved rearward into the unlocked position (at least 2.8 mm of travel in simulation).
- **0.50** when pressing the rear pad raises both latch tips above the cap shoulders (at least 1.55 mm pad depression in simulation).
- **0.75** after at least 15 mm axial travel with the latch held down as its tip passes the shoulder. Track the moving plug relative to the cap.
- **1.00** when the plug nose is more than 30 mm clear of the cap mouth, the cap stays in its mount, the plug rests on the release strip, and both hands withdraw at least 10 mm for one second. Use the free plug mouth datum at x=0 and y=−62 mm with a 21 mm planar tolerance. Check that the leads stay attached and the parts remain stable within 2 mm and 2° during the one-second dwell.
- **0.00** if the latch is bypassed or a housing is dropped below the work surface. An untouched reset also scores zero.

## Simulation model

The connector envelopes and stock contact surfaces follow the product drawings. Compound rigid shapes approximate the mating channel, guards, rear CPA ridge, latch pad and shoulders. A native prismatic joint provides the 3.2 mm CPA stroke. Its internal continuous cam bears on a 1.5 N/mm spring follower. A 12° native revolute joint with a 0.064 N·m/rad spring approximates the molded primary latch flexure. Its rear pad moves down and raises its forward tip, with the CPA physically blocking that movement at reset. The simulated plug and cap weigh 12.5 g and 14 g. The cap is fixed at its bracket and retains a static four-wire bundle between that cap and its routing clip. The plug-side distal clip anchors an 11 g passive four-wire bundle with six collision-bearing segments at the stated routing mark. The four visible conductors share a collision envelope along each bundle. Local shell corner radius, wire stiffness, terminal friction, electrical performance and wear are outside the simulation's calibration.

## Run in simulation

Activate the RoboCurve environment and run from this task directory. The recording helper keeps the stock overhead camera and substitutes a fixed oblique close-up for the right wrist stream. The budget is 2,400 controls at 20 Hz, or 120 seconds of simulated time. The submission seed is 0. The scripted demonstration reached 1.0 in 837 controls (41.85 seconds of simulated time).

```bash
mkdir -p .local/revised-demo
INTERLOCK_DIAGNOSTICS=.local/revised-demo/diagnostics.jsonl \
INTERLOCK_CONTACTS=.local/revised-demo/contacts.jsonl \
python capture_detail.py run --embodiment yam_arena --policy yam_scripted \
  -E task=task.py -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 \
  -E cam_width=640 -E cam_height=360 \
  --instruction 'Slide the red secondary lock toward the wires. Hold down the rear latch while pulling the plug straight out of the mounted cap. Set the plug down with its leads attached.' \
  --scorer task_score --seed 0 --max-steps 2400 --store-frames --no-prompt --log-dir .local/revised-demo
python -m unittest test_checks
```

For matched controls, set `INTERLOCK_DEMO=locked` to try the press and pull with the CPA still engaged. Set `INTERLOCK_DEMO=unpressed` to open the CPA and attempt the same pull with the pick held above the latch. Use a fresh log directory for each trial.

For the Dream-1 evaluation, use `inspect-robots` in place of `python capture_detail.py`, select `--policy dream`, and use a separate log directory. This uses the original policy cameras.

The locked control scored 0.0 in 755 actions, with 0.54 mm peak withdrawal during the loaded pull and the CPA remaining closed. The open-CPA control without rear-pad pressure scored 0.25 in 894 actions and remained coupled, with 0.68 mm peak withdrawal during the pull. Both were run at seed 0.
