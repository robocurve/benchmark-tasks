# Real-trial setup: build a motorised stage with a two-button jog box from this list, about 314 USD

Why a build list and not a stand: commercial motorised test stands with a jog function start near USD 2,500 (Shimpo FGS-100E 2,497.50-3,050; PCE-VTS 50 2,755; Imada MX-110 3,336-4,083; Mark-10 TSFM500-DC 3,752-4,095), and the commercial small wedge grip (Mark-10 G1062) is USD 270-335 each. The official review allows "a parts list and build files". Prices are page prices checked 2026-11-17; estimates and budgets are labelled.

## What the real trial claims, and what it does not
It supports the three actions of this task: mounting the specimen, the spring-closed grips, and the jog of the crosshead (hold a button, the stage moves, release, it stops: the controller manual describes a mode in which the motor "rotates when keep press buttons and stop if release button"). It does NOT claim a 500 N pull: the stage lies horizontal, where the published rating is 30 kg (15 kg vertical, about 147 N), enough to carry the crosshead and the grip; a tensile load is not part of the task or its scoring.

## How it is put together
- Stage on its side along y; grip B on the stage carriage through a printed plate on the four M4 workpiece holes (draw the plate from the hole pattern in the vendor page's images). Grip A on a printed clamp block bolted to the extrusion at the fixed end. Each grip has a 10 x 10 x 25 mm shank; each block has a 10.2 mm square pocket 20 mm deep and two M4 set screws (`build_files/stl/grip_block.stl`, two off).
- Jog box (the scene's third key, STOP, is a fixed visual; only the two jog buttons are wired): the two PBS111 buttons in a printed box at the fixed end, wired to the controller's CW and CCW terminals; speed pot set to about 100 RPM (5 mm lead: 8.3 mm/s = 500 mm/min, the scene's value).
- The scene's 460 mm frame, 76 mm loading axis and 35 mm back-off are carried over from a commercial stand's data sheet: the build list's stage travels 200 mm and its axis height is set by the frame.

## What the checks cover
`build_files/check_assembly.py` runs on the emitted geometry: scalar constants against `qc_met10.py`; a 45-position sweep of the lever with the sloped rail faces under every crossbar solid, every pair of parts tested, tolerance 0.001 mm; lift 0 at rest and 6.2 mm at 22 mm; the jaw spring and return spring lengths and forces computed from the solids' faces; fit of stem, hole and slot; the spring bodies' clearance over the travel; print-bed size. It is a geometry and spring-arithmetic check; the task ends at mounting, before any pull.

## Install check procedure
1. Stage: with the controller powered, hold the CW button 10 s and the CCW button 10 s: PASS if the carriage moves only while a button is held and stops on release; record the speed at the pot setting (mm/s).
2. Grips on the stage and the frame: lay a 165 mm dogbone across both open slots: PASS if it lies flat, and the jaw faces can be set 150 mm apart by ruler.
3. Jog to 115 mm and back: PASS if the printed parts clear each other over the 35 mm travel and the lever still moves its full 22 mm.
4. Press the lever back 22 mm: PASS if the jaw opens above 3.2 mm (feeler) and closes by itself on release; load a dogbone tab 25 mm deep and release: PASS if it hangs held by the jaw spring.
The result of each step is recorded against its pass line.

Reset between trials: dogbone flat on its tray against the back stops, redraw the 17 mm pen mark on both tab ends, jog the crosshead back 35 mm until the jaw faces are 150 mm apart (ruler), both levers forward.
