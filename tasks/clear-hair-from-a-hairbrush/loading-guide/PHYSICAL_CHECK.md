# Hairbrush loading: physical verification required

A reported manual loading/removal check is documented in [PHYSICAL_CHECK_REPORT.md](PHYSICAL_CHECK_REPORT.md), including its limitations. The procedure below defines the measured evaluation reset; the report does not claim that every specified measurement was verified in the household check.

## Materials

Use the specified four-column, eleven-row brush and loose synthetic wig fibres, cut to 75 mm. Keep the brush and one donor wig/product, colour and fibre batch fixed across trials; record the donor source or package. Use dry, straight fibres without adhesive or attached wig backing. The reported manual check used synthetic fibres from an old wig on a similar household brush, not a verified exact match to the specified dimensions. The diagram and measurements below define the evaluation reset.

Also use a ruler, scissors, a 0.01 g scale for preparing small locks, a blunt pin-tail comb, ten numbered storage cards and a stopwatch. The original 0.1 g judging scale remains sufficient for weighing the total removed hair; it is not sufficiently precise for preparing each 0.2 g lock.

## Prepare once per batch

1. Separate dry, untreated fibres; do not use conditioner, heat setting, oil, adhesive or knots.
2. Cut ten loose parallel locks to 75 ± 2 mm. Adjust each to 0.20 ± 0.02 g; record individual and total mass. Keep both ends loose. Hold a lock only while handling it; remove temporary clips before loading.
3. Store the locks on cards 1–10. Confirm the brush has all 44 intact pins. Label columns A–D left to right, and rows 01–11 from the end away from the handle toward the handle.

Report the one-off preparation time separately from reset time.

## Load each trial

Place the brush pins up with its handle toward you. Use [numbered-loading.svg](numbered-loading.svg) and [close-route.svg](close-route.svg). Lock k uses row k, for k=1…10. Row 11 stays empty.

For each lock, lay its midpoint beside column B, then use the blunt comb tail to route the loose bundle alternately around the four pins: **A on the handle side, B on the tip side, C on the handle side, D on the tip side**. Work under the ball tips. Do not wind a complete turn around a pin. Leave approximately 11 mm of each end beyond the sides of the brush head; use the endpoint coordinates in `routes.json` as reference positions, not a requirement to measure individual fine fibres to millimetre precision. Lay the remaining locks in increasing row order.

For visibility during loading, place a contrasting card behind the loose dark hair and use diffuse task lighting. Support the bundle with the blunt comb tail while releasing it from the fingers; let go of one end at a time. Remove the card before the trial. These handling aids are recommendations prompted by the reported difficulty seeing and releasing the fibres; they were not part of the reported check.

The route is a shallow weave between existing pins, not a tied loop. Do not tighten it by pulling both ends. Fine fibres can spread within the lock; neither a bonded ribbon nor a single thick cord is an acceptable substitute. Use no hand-applied snag force: the real pin/hair contact is what must retain the lock.

## Minimum physical check: one timed load and removal

Start the stopwatch with prepared locks on their cards and an empty brush in its starting place. Stop only after all ten locks pass the visual checklist below. Inspect directly above and close from the side, with a ruler. Save photographs when available; otherwise record the observations in writing and state that no photographs were retained. Then use the comb tail to remove the hair in the normal way. Record any snagging, dropped locks or damaged pins. One actual timed load and removal is the minimum evidence proposed here; a repeat is useful if time permits, but three repetitions were our suggestion, not a reviewer requirement.

- Ten loose locks, one per specified row; row 11 empty.
- Every lock follows A+/B−/C+/D−, where + means handleward.
- Free ends are visible, not tied or trapped under the handle; both ends remain exposed by approximately 10 mm as in the diagram.
- Each lock lies below the pin tips and stays caught when the brush is moved gently from loading position to its photographed trial position. No shaking or artificial retention test is required.
- The comb tail can enter under the hair; all pins remain intact.

A usable protocol should load and verify within a few minutes. Record the actual elapsed time; do not round it down or substitute an estimate. The reviewer did not specify a numerical time limit, so five minutes is only our proposed practical target. If a lock falls free, cannot be routed without knotting, or the hair cannot be prepared consistently, record that and revise the method rather than reporting a pass.
