# Inert fixture build and calibration

The following is a bench design for a second lab to evaluate. Use a dummy glove and a captive cover. The industrial counterpart is the loose cover section after removal of its fasteners. No powered conveyor, person, or production machine is part of this setup.

## Before collecting robot trials

1. Measure each moving mass, guide travel, tip position, cover edge and end stops. The longest finger starts 22 mm beyond the origin and the far edge is at +15 mm. Check a visible horizontal 10 mm separation at 47.1 mm of slide travel. Mark the 78 and 80 mm carriage positions. Mark both lift homes and their 3 mm home bands.
2. Support the cover independently. Tare each digit channel. Apply the calibration masses vertically to one carrier at a time, then to both. At standard gravity, 100 and 200 g apply approximately 0.981 and 1.961 N. At 0.655 N/mm, these correspond to 1.50 and 2.99 mm of spring deflection on a single carrier. Repeat loading and unloading three times. Record the force, displacement and red-tip position at every step. Check for stiction, hysteresis and cross-talk.
3. Measure apparent pointer motion from its calibrated image plane. Ideal small-motion gain is 6.5:1. The red tip follows an arc. Use the actual scale calibration for larger deflections. The early-unload video comparison requires at least two 5 mm divisions of same-side pre-clearance pointer increase. Preserve images and uncertainty. A pointer event supports the reload classification; use force/displacement measurements for the exact scorer thresholds. If measurement uncertainty straddles a decision boundary, record an indeterminate physical result.
4. Weigh and balance the cover. Place a gauge under each fixed rest with the glove clear and saddles home. With symmetric loading the resting reactions should be near 2.35 N each. With the glove at the start, both finger reactions should exceed 0.20 N, and neither saddle should read 0.10 N or more. A hand that drifts over 3 mm in a 60 s idle trial invalidates reset.
5. Measure static slip and dynamic drag on a grip-sleeve coupon with the actual jaw material, normal preload, and lift speed. Measure force versus carriage speed at two or more speeds with and without the return engaged. Fit the specified slopes. At full withdrawal, release from measured lift, confirm both grips leave the swept envelopes, measure the remaining spring-driven stroke and film each saddle reaching home. A change of grip material, guide friction or damping calls for rerunning the controls.
6. Synchronize both pointer scales, a carriage mark and both landing reactions to the overhead image and wrist recordings. Run left, right and bilateral lowering from the same bilateral-relief state, then repeat with support maintained. The outcome of interest is a same-side load and pointer rise before the fingertips clear. Keep every trial, including marginal and failed repeats.

The design has simulated mass/friction sensitivity tests. Fabricated-fit tolerances, grip friction, physiologic response and metrology repeatability still require physical measurement.
