# Load four tapes into a magazine in barcode order

Four LTO tape cartridges stand on edge in a tote on the table, and a five-slot tape magazine stands behind them,
its slots facing the arms. The instruction is: **"Load the four tapes into the magazine in barcode order, starting at
slot 1."**

To do it, the robot has to:
- read the barcode labels: the cartridges look the same, and only their labels (AI0001L9 to AI0004L9) tell them
  apart;
- find the one it cannot read: AI0002L9 stands turned round in the tote, its label facing away from the arms;
- load AI0001L9 into slot 1, AI0002L9 into slot 2, AI0003L9 into slot 3 and AI0004L9 into slot 4, each label out and
  the right way up (the cartridge turned round has to be turned back);
- push each cartridge home, flush with the magazine's front, and let go.

The tote holds them in the order AI0003L9, AI0001L9, AI0002L9, AI0004L9 from the robot's left, so loading them as they
stand scores 0.

Tape libraries hold the archive and backup copies of training data and models, and loading cartridges into a
library's magazine by their barcodes is routine data-centre work. Label out is how a magazine is loaded, so the
library's scanner can read the barcodes. Loading a set in barcode order keeps it in sequence, for example a backup set
going off site, so it can be checked against its list at a glance. The task tests three things:
- **Reading small labels.** The barcode labels are 8 cm long on a 2.15 cm face, printed up the face of a cartridge
  standing on edge, with characters about 5 mm tall. They are too small to read in the top camera; the wrist cameras
  have to read them.
- **Order and orientation together.** The cartridges must go in by barcode, not by position, and one has to be
  turned round before it can be read or loaded. A YAM wrist turns 120 degrees either way, so turning a cartridge
  round in one hand is not possible; the demo hands it from one arm to the other.
- **Seating.** A cartridge slid in part way is not loaded. It has to be pushed until it is flush with the
  magazine's front.

No task on the board uses tapes. The closest is #422, which moves four look-alike drives into the bays with the same
numbers in a replacement chassis. #251 and #612 load four objects into slots or a rack in a set order, and #744 turns
look-alike cartons to read labels that face away. Here the cartridges can be told apart only by their printed
labels, one has to be turned end for end before it can be read or loaded, and each has to be pushed flush.

## Files

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `task.py` | The yam_arena task file: the tote, the magazine and the cartridges, the monitor, `success()` and `score()` |
| `geometry.py` | Dimensions and layout: the cartridges, the tote's bays, the magazine's slots, the scoring tolerances |
| `build_assets.py` | Writes the textures and `assets/*.usda` (needs `pxr`, PIL and the DejaVu fonts) |
| `assets/` | The four cartridges (with their barcode labels), the magazine and the tote |
| `scenekit.py` | USD bodies and reset hooks for yam_arena task files |
| `demokit.py` | Helpers for the scripted demo |
| `demo.py` | The scripted full-score demo, and variants that load in the tote's order, skip the turn, or keep a hand, closed or open, on the last cartridge |

`scenekit.py` hooks `yam_arena.scene` and the embodiment's step and reset without modifying yam_arena, so the task
runs with the standard `yam_arena` embodiment and `task_score` scorer. Tested with yam-isaac-lab-arena at `bd62771`
(Isaac Lab-Arena 0.3.1).

All the assets are generated by `build_assets.py` from `geometry.py`; there are no third-party models. The labels
are drawn with the DejaVu fonts (Bitstream Vera licence), and the barcodes are Code 39.

## Run a trial

From this folder, in a shell with the yam_arena environment active. New shells on the walkthrough's Brev instance
have it; elsewhere, `source <ARENA_DIR>/yam.env`.

```bash
inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_arena --policy dream \
  -E task=task.py -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 -E cam_width=320 -E cam_height=176 \
  --instruction "Load the four tapes into the magazine in barcode order, starting at slot 1." \
  --scorer task_score --max-steps 2400 --store-frames --log-dir ~/yam-eval/tape-dream
```

`task_score` prints the trial's score: 1.0 on success, otherwise `score()` at the end of the episode.

- **Seeds:** there is one fixed layout and no randomisation (`object_jitter=0`), so the seed has no effect.
- **Runtime:** a full 2,400-step Dream-1 trial took 5 min 46 s on an RTX 5090 (mean inference latency 0.66 s),
  plus about a minute to start Isaac Sim. A successful trial ends earlier: the scripted demo stops after 1,413
  steps.

Dream-1 scored 0. Within the first 10 s both arms went to the tote, the left one over its left end and the right one
beside its right end, and they stayed near there for the rest of the trial. The left gripper came down on AI0003L9,
the leftmost cartridge, and nudged it 2 mm at about 69 s. No cartridge was lifted, and the magazine stayed empty. The
run used one continuous Dream-1 session, with no errors or retries.

## Scripted demo

```bash
python demo.py task.py demo_out                               # full score: demo_*_cam.mp4, setup.png, result.json
python demo.py task.py tote_out --order 3,1,2,4 --no-turn     # the tote's order, no reading or turning: 0
python demo.py task.py noturn_out --no-turn                   # barcode order, but AI0002L9 not turned: 0.25
python demo.py task.py hold_out --keep-hold                   # all four, but a hand stays on the last one: 0.75
python demo.py task.py open_out --keep-hold --open-hand       # the same, with that hand's fingers opened wide: 0.75
```

The slots are filled in order, 1 to 4.
1. **AI0001L9 into slot 1** (left arm).
2. **AI0002L9 into slot 2, turned round first.**
   - The right arm takes it from above by the middle of its top edge, lifts it, turns it a quarter turn, and stands
     it on the table between the tote and the magazine.
   - The left arm takes it the same way, turns it the other quarter turn so its label faces the arms, and loads it.
3. **AI0003L9 into slot 3** (left arm), then **AI0004L9 into slot 4** (right arm).

Each load:
- From the cartridge's measured pose, the arm squares it to the slot, lines it up across and sets it 4 mm above the
  slot's floor.
- It slides the cartridge about 2 cm in, until the gripper's housing nears the magazine, then lets go.
- It pushes the upper half of the label face with its closed fingertips, measuring and pushing again until the
  cartridge is home.

The demo succeeds (1.0) in 1,413 steps (71 s): all four in order, label out and home, with both arms clear for 1 s.
Loading them in the tote's order without reading or turning (slot 1 gets AI0003L9) scores 0. Skipping the turn
(`--no-turn`) leaves slot 2 without AI0002L9 label out, and the run scores 0.25. Keeping the fingertips on the last
cartridge (`--keep-hold`) scores 0.75, and so does opening that hand wide where it stands (`--open-hand`).

## Scoring

Judged at the end of the trial: a photo of the magazine's front for the barcodes and which way up they are, a ruler
or a photo from above for home, and the trial's video for letting go.

| Score | When |
|---|---|
| 1.0 (success) | Slots 1 to 4 hold AI0001L9 to AI0004L9, each label out and pushed home, and no part of the robot has touched them for the last 1 s. This ends the trial |
| 0.75 / 0.5 / 0.25 | Counting from slot 1, the first 3 / 2 / 1 slots are right. Counting stops at the first slot that is empty, holds the wrong cartridge, holds one turned round or upside down, or holds one not pushed home. All four right but still touched in the last 1 s scores 0.75 |
| 0 | Slot 1 is not AI0001L9 label out and home, including doing nothing |

- **Label out:** the cartridge stands the way up it stood in the tote (its write-protect tab at the bottom, the
  barcode text reading from the bottom up), with its label on the face toward the arms, readable through the slot's
  opening.
- **Home:** the label face stands at most 3 mm in front of the slot walls' front ends. It cannot go deeper: a stop at
  the back holds it flush. Measure it with a ruler, or in a photo from above, which sees the cartridge's top in the
  1.5 cm gap the magazine's top leaves at the front.
- **Let go:** in the video, no part of either arm touches a loaded cartridge in the last 1 s.
- **Chance:** loading the four in a random order scores about 0.10 on average.
- **In simulation:**
  - **Label out.** The label face points within 26 degrees of the arms, and the cartridge stands within 18 degrees
    of upright.
  - **In a slot.** Its middle is within 6 mm of the slot's middle, across, standing on the slot's floor.
  - **Home.** Its label face is within 3 mm of the magazine's front.
  - **Let go.** For the last 20 control steps, both arms stay at least 1 cm from every loaded cartridge. The check
    uses yam_arena's collision hulls of every link, the gripper body and both fingers, at their simulated poses.

## Real-world build

All positions are in cm from the midpoint between the arm bases: x forward, y to the robot's left. Heights are above
the table top.

- **Cartridges.** Four LTO-9 cartridges, 10.2 x 10.54 x 2.15 cm, about 200 g each, with plastic shells. Use blank
  or retired ones, since the robot may drop them. LTO barcode labels AI0001L9, AI0002L9, AI0003L9 and AI0004L9 are on
  their label faces, the end with the write-protect tab.
- **Tote.** Blue acrylic, glued, taped to the table. It is 11.4 x 28 cm inside, from x 11.4 to 22.8, centred on y 0,
  on a 4 mm floor, with 3 mm walls 3 cm tall.
  - Four bays at y +10.5, +3.5, -3.5 and -10.5, separated by dividers 2 mm thick and 2.4 cm tall (2 cm above the
    floor).
  - In each bay two rails, 4 mm thick and 1 cm tall, 2 mm clear of the cartridge, hold it upright.
  - The cartridges stand on edge with their near faces at x 12. From the robot's left: AI0003L9, AI0001L9,
    AI0002L9 turned round (its label at x 22.2, facing the magazine), and AI0004L9.
- **Magazine.** Dark grey PLA, 3D-printed, clamped to the table. It is 11.4 cm deep, 15.7 cm wide and 12.6 cm tall,
  with five slots that open toward the arms at x 36.
  - Each slot is 2.6 cm wide and 11.6 cm tall inside, 3 cm apart, on a 6 mm floor under a 4 mm top. The walls are
    4 mm thick between the slots and 5.5 mm at the sides.
  - Slot 1 is at y +6 (the robot's left), through slot 5 at y -6.
  - A stop 10.2 cm behind the front holds a cartridge pushed home with its label face flush with the front.
  - The walls taper over their front 1.6 cm to a 0.5 mm edge, and an 8 mm ramp leads up to the floor.
  - The top stops 1.5 cm behind the front, leaving room for fingers. The slot numbers 1 to 5 are printed along its
    front edge.
- **Arms** start at the ready pose with open grippers.
- **Safety.** The cartridges weigh 200 g and the magazine is clamped. Keep hands away from the slots while the arms
  push.

## Simulation fidelity

- Rigid bodies, 240 Hz physics, 20 Hz control.
- **The tote and the magazine:** fixed.
  - The magazine's slots are built from walls, a floor, a top and a back stop.
  - Friction: the magazine 0.3 static and 0.25 dynamic, the tote 0.8 and 0.7.
- **Cartridges:** rigid bodies, 200 g each, friction 0.5 static and 0.4 dynamic. PhysX averages the two surfaces, so a
  cartridge slides in a slot at 0.4 static and 0.325 dynamic.
  - The magazine has no keying, so a cartridge can go in turned round or upside down; it then scores as wrong.
  - Real magazines often refuse a cartridge the wrong way round.
- **Monitor:** after each control step, task code:
  - reads each cartridge's pose;
  - finds the slot it stands in, whether its label is out and whether it is home;
  - checks how close the collision hulls of either arm, fingers included, come to the loaded cartridges.
- **Resets:** the reset puts the cartridges back in the tote. The tote and the magazine are fixed.

## Reset between trials

Take the cartridges out of the magazine and stand them back in the tote, from the robot's left: AI0003L9, AI0001L9,
AI0002L9 (turned round, label away from the arms) and AI0004L9.
