# Take one chip out of an open packet

Take one loose chip from an already open snack packet and put it on the plate. Leave the other two inside and release the objects. Either chip may be chosen.

This familiar household action tests reaching through a confined mouth and extracting one item while retaining the other contents in a lightweight movable container. It does not require opening a seal or choosing a particular chip. The category is Societal relatability.

## Station and reset

Use the official two YAM arms with parallel grippers in Isaac Lab Arena through Inspect Robots. Coordinates are metres forward and left of the midpoint between the arm bases. The table surface is at z = 0.7475 m. The layout is fixed with seed 0 and no object jitter.

The packet root starts at (0.285, 0.015, 0.7495) m and settles onto the table. It represents an open metallized polypropylene pouch using a rigid 90 by 70 by 1 mm lower gusset and 28 mm lower walls. Two 90 by 30 by 0.8 mm upper panels rotate passively about X at local (0, ±35, 28) mm. They start 12 degrees outward with ±35 degree limits. Their weak passive springs are 0.001 N m/rad, with damping 0.0001 N m s/rad. No task action or hidden controller opens the mouth. The lower shell weighs 12 g and each panel 2 g. A 30 by 50 by 1 mm upright side seam is centered at local (-60, 15, 28) mm. Stabilizing the packet is optional.

Each potato-chip surrogate weighs 1.2 g and spans approximately 32 by 40 mm. Seven 2 mm thick convex strips approximate a curved surface with up to 4 mm rise. Initial local origins are (8, -6, 28), (0, 0, 18) and (-8, 6, 8) mm. Gravity settles them before the grasp. They are separate dynamic rigid bodies with physical collisions.

The fixed polymer plate is 100 by 100 mm with a 4 mm floor and 12 mm rim, centered at (0.370, -0.145, 0.7475) m. Geometry is generated by design.py and task.py. No external custom assets are required. Reset restores all three chips, the packet and its hinge angles. No object is consumed.

## Fidelity

The lower pouch is rigid and the upper panels are articulated rigid sheets. This approximates mouth motion but does not model arbitrary film crumpling, tearing or a sealed package. The chip models cannot fracture. The task does not measure chip integrity, crushing avoidance, hygiene or food safety. These are material limitations of the simulation.

The demo parks the left arm clear and uses the right arm to retrieve a chip. Both arms are available to Dream. All motion is executed through robot joint commands and simulated contact. The task does not attach a chip to the gripper or reposition objects during the trial.

## Primary score

All three chips must start inside the packet. Scoring uses the transformed corners of every physical chip strip and the measured packet-root pose.

* 0.5 after a chip wholly clears the maximum mouth envelope by 5 mm, meaning every corner is at least 65 mm above the packet root, while the other two remain inside.
* 1.0 when that chip rests wholly within the plate's inner X and Y limits of ±47 mm. Its lowest point must be 3 to 6 mm above the table, corresponding to resting on the plate floor. Translational speed must be at most 5 mm/s and angular speed at most 0.1 rad/s. Both gripper-tip midpoints must be at least 60 mm from every chip and the packet for one second.
* Otherwise 0 or the applicable 0.5 partial credit. Removing the chip from the plate removes full credit.

Packet displacement greater than 30 mm, tilt greater than 15 degrees, a chip falling more than 2 mm below the table, invalid telemetry or more than one chip leaving the packet permanently scores zero. Returning an extra removed chip does not restore credit. Packet containment requires every corner within local X ±44 mm, Y ±34 mm and Z from -2 to 65 mm.

The physical outcome can be checked with video and ruler marks: one chip resting on the plate, two remaining inside, an upright packet near its start and both hands clear. No force sensors are used for scoring.

## Run

The evaluated environment uses Isaac Lab Arena 0.3.1 and Inspect Robots 1.0.0 on the official YAM launchable. Use Python 3.12 or later and the launchable's installed robot assets.

    cd tasks/remove-chip-from-open-packet
    source /data/IsaacLab-Arena/yam.env
    uv pip install --python /data/IsaacLab-Arena/.venv/bin/python --no-deps -e runtime -e policy
    python validate.py --yam-source /data/yam-isaac-lab-arena
    bash run.sh pilot
    bash run.sh demo

The pilot uses 320 by 180 cameras and up to 900 control steps. Submission demo and Dream modes use native 1280 by 720 cameras and a 2,400-step limit at 20 Hz, equivalent to 120 simulated seconds. The full-score demo ends at 531 steps, or 26.55 simulated seconds. Wall-clock runtime depends on rendering load; concurrent runs on an L40S take longer than simulated time.

Configure Dream credentials privately using the provider's normal setup. With the Dream SDK available, start the loopback bridge in another terminal:

    source /data/IsaacLab-Arena/yam.env
    python policy/current_api_bridge.py

Then run bash run.sh dream. The policy is Dream-1 through Inspect Robots. DREAM_BRIDGE_PORT selects another bridge port and YAM_ENV selects another environment file. The bridge logs provider session recovery explicitly and never substitutes scripted actions for model actions. After the simulator exits, run the following before shutting down the bridge. It preserves the actual session summary if the worker skipped its exit handler and otherwise does nothing.

    python tools/close_dream_session.py runs/dream/TRIAL

## Evidence

Each run saves a source hash snapshot, continuous 20 Hz measured telemetry and an Inspect Robots trial log. Export a completed submission recording with:

    python evidence.py export runs/demo/TRIAL/adhoc_ID.json
    python evidence.py export runs/dream/TRIAL/adhoc_ID.json

The exporter checks source identity, the actual scorer result, step count, frequency and recording frame count. It emits all three H.264 1280 by 720 camera videos, the starting screenshot, logs and a checksum manifest. The 21 scorer checks include extra-chip removal followed by return, incomplete extraction, a held chip, unstable placement, an overhanging chip, a moved or tipped packet and invalid telemetry. These checks are distinct from native robot demonstration results.

Recorded results: scripted demo 1.0 in 531 steps; Dream-1 beta 0.0 in 2,400 steps. The model completed using one provider session with no session resets. The simulator worker skipped its exit summary, so that summary was captured explicitly from the still-running bridge before it was closed. The session-close utility is an evidence helper and does not change the simulated task or recorded actions.
