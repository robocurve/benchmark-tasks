# Reproduce the bedside comparison with an inert leg

The measurable question is whether the heel remains unloaded once the voluntary lift ends. Use a weighted mannequin for all trials. The following describes an unbuilt fixture and how to commission it. Hardware cost, fit on a shared rig, repeatability and scoring agreement have yet to be measured.

## Register the bed and both robots

Use the working right robot’s URDF `base` origin as the survey datum. Dimensions below are in millimetres. Both bases face +X into the bed. +Y points along the bed toward the head and the parked left arm. +Z is up. Locate the actual URDF base frame from the installed mount drawing or a surveyed fiducial. The edge of a pedestal can have a different offset. In the native station’s world coordinates the right base is at (0, −305, 750), the left base at (0, +305, 750), and the fixture origin at (185, −305, 725). Both bases and the fixture have identity rotation.

|---|---|---|
| Working right base and parked left base | Right (0, 0, 0), left (0, 610, 0) | Survey both centers and yaw directions. Both robots are present. The scripted trial commands motion only on the right. |
| Bed top | X 135 to 955, Y −500 to 1320, Z −25 | Mark the four corners of the level 820 × 1820 mm surface. The simulated slab is 70 mm thick. |
| Fixed hip hinge | (605, 575, 115), hinge axis parallel to X | At the nominal settled reset the knee hinge is at (605, 200.6, 90.2), and the heel center at (605, −182.1, 23.8). Use a plumb line and side-view ruler. The heel center is an internal landmark. Measure the separate heel underside to establish contact. |
| Cushion sole center at reset | (350, 20, −13), long axis parallel to Y | Center the loose cushion on this mark. Its 164 mm dimension runs across the bed. For longitudinal-offset trials move the resident and hip mounting datum together by +50, +80 or +200 mm in Y. The bed, bases and initial cushion stay in place. |

The registration is taken from the frozen [scene dimensions](../task/station.py), [station origin](../isaac/readout.py), [camera setup](../isaac/camera.py), and native reference reset. The [native scene bridge](../isaac/bridge.py) removes the default tabletop. A pre-existing table can occupy the same volume as the proposed bed. Survey it before assuming the bed can fit. Rigidly translating the bed and both robot mounts together preserves their relative geometry. Check floor, mounts, stand, sling and clearances again in the new location.

The overview stand’s 240 × 220 mm foot is centered at (1250, −715) in plan. The bed, both base origins and this foot alone span X 0 through 1370 and Y −825 through 1320 mm. This **1370 × 2145 mm lower bound** excludes the parts of baseplates or moving arms outside those extrema, the fixture support, sling frame, operators and access space. The necessary room and mount capacity depend on the installation. Record its actual surveyed drawing before commissioning.

**Reach check.** Across the reset and 280 action samples of the scripted reference, the right tool-center point ranged from 228.0 to 530.1 mm in X, 0.0 to 22.6 mm in Y and −1.1 to 266.0 mm in Z. Its greatest straight-line distance from the right base was 546.9 mm. The tool point lies 120 mm along the gripper’s local +Z axis. The left tool point stayed at approximately (311.9, 610.0, 266.0). These values describe one simulated trajectory. Check the actual end effector, all links, wrist cables, parked arm, bed edge, instruments and sling through the complete motion. Start with a scaled plan and a powered-down placement check. Use the robot manufacturer’s commissioning procedure for any inert-fixture dry run. A TCP endpoint alone cannot establish joint feasibility or collision clearance on a shared rig.

### Cushion interface and physical transfer

The submitted cushion has a **200 g rigid sole, 164 × 258 × 24 mm**, beneath six 30 g compliant strips. This construction affects what the robot can push. A native reference moved the cushion center 280.5 mm forward. During the 40 action intervals with forward movement above 0.5 mm, right-arm geometry was within 1 mm of a foam strip in 37 observations and within 1 mm of the sole in 24. These counts can overlap. Reconstruct each post-action pose with the [station readout](../isaac/readout.py), then query the minimum convex geometry distance from all right-arm link meshes to each surface using the [witness](../task/witness.py) search limit of 0.25 m. The saved poses establish proximity. The share of contact load carried by the foam and sole is unknown. In a separate matched-command CPU PhysX experiment, excluding direct robot-to-sole collisions kept the reference at 1.0 with 280.46 mm cushion travel. The sole still carried the foam and slid on the bed. [Six-run contact comparison](../validation/cushion/README.md)

For a bench reproduction of the surrogate, document the base mass, thickness, foam profile, and cover material. Mark its unmodified side face. Measure the force at which the unloaded cushion starts sliding on the selected bed cover using a spring scale and a slow horizontal pull, then repeat for moving drag. Record actual values and dispersion. Use the same materials and marked starting orientation after each reset. During the robot trial remove the measuring scale and leave the cushion free. A rigid edge, added handle, taped-down corner or guiding rail can change the manipulation.

A separate regular-cushion check can test whether the demonstrated push transfers to ordinary bedside equipment. Record the model, dimensions, cover and mass of an unmodified cushion. Repeat the nominal, heel-on-foam and idle controls on the registered inert leg and retain all outcomes. If the cushion folds, slips or leaves the heel loaded, record the result as a transfer failure on that cushion. The benchmark currently establishes performance with its simulated surrogate. Physical transfer to an ordinary clinical pillow remains open.

## Manual voluntary lift

Use a wide soft ankle sling with free tails joined to a low-stretch cord. Route the line over an overhead pulley to an operator outside the robot workspace. Use an anchored support and cord with a supplier-rated working load for the measured peak tension. Guide the cord by hand throughout the raise and return. The line provides upward assistance only. Park the loop visibly slack, with clearance from the leg and cushion, after the lift. Keep the line away from the heel sensor. Mark the line for home, peak and return. The simulation uses an independent, upward-only knee moment. A sling supplies an external force with a different moment arm, so match the observed heel path and release timing by measurement.

1. Settle the mannequin on the bare mattress. With the robot parked, film five complete lift cycles. Use the supplied [idle-arm heel-height curves](idle_lift_profiles.csv) as the target for commissioning. Record when the heel clears, peak height and when the line is entirely slack. The nominal assisting moment begins after 0.30 s. In the native station’s idle run the heel underside was approximately 68.4 mm above the bed at 1.00 s, 193.5 mm at 1.35 s and 222.2 mm at 2.00 s. At 5.50 s it was back down to 150.4 mm and by 6.20 s it had settled on the bed. The supplied standalone PhysX curve agrees with the recorded idle station curve within 0.04 mm at all 280 samples. The separate MuJoCo development curve peaks at about 211 mm. Later profiles shift the nominal cues by 3.50 or 4.50 s.
2. Mark the pulley stroke based on that specific mannequin. Measure cord travel separately from vertical heel rise. For initial commissioning aim for peak height within 15 mm, release within 0.15 s and heel-to-bed height within 15 mm at each 0.25 s sample of the free-lift trace; keep all five recordings. These are proposed tolerances. For a PhysX reproduction use the separately labeled PhysX curve for that engine. Never pull a loaded heel to a commanded position.
3. On scored trials give the operator the clock cue independently of robot behavior. Let the leg settle naturally on the bed or cushion during return. Verify slack through the final two seconds. Record the actual heel trajectory, including differences caused by support. An independently mistimed operator cue, operator-held terminal support or missing decisive measurements invalidate a bench reproduction. Preserve the recording and reason. A robot-induced disturbance, an incorrectly placed cushion, a robot-tensioned sling or residual heel loading after a correctly delivered lift counts against performance. If intervention interrupts an episode, record the cause and interruption. A completed 280-action episode is required to report its numeric grade.

## Calibrate the single heel channel

Tare the installed heel cup free of contact. Apply known loads at 0, approximately 1, 2, 5, 10, 18 and 30 N using weighed masses or a checked scale. Repeat loading and unloading at least three times and repeat at representative contact angles. Test for cable pull, drift over the full 14 s, edge bypass and foam hysteresis. Calibrate the film and readout together. Validate the initial relaxed baseline, the 2 N comparator, 50% relief, and 150% over-baseline indications at the episode baseline. Record uncertainty at each boundary. If the fitted channel cannot discriminate either side of a threshold, classify the affected trial as unscorable. A switch or free paper tab can corroborate a gap. Partial relief and excess loading require the **analog** reading. A reading below 2 N is reported as below the fixture threshold.

In the native +80 mm trial, the reset median was 17.0645 N, so its half-load cutoff was 8.5322 N. The maximum terminal reading was 9.2354 N, exceeding the cutoff by 0.7032 N. If the baseline and terminal reading each have an absolute error bounded by `u`, the difference from half-baseline has worst-case uncertainty `1.5 × u`. To distinguish those quantities on this trial, the common bound must be below about 0.469 N (0.7032 divided by 1.5). Use the actual calibrated bounds and readings for every other trial. An unresolved boundary yields an unscorable result.

## Bench score sheet

| Condition | Bench reading at the 20 Hz scoring clock |
|---|---|
| Reset | The first five frames show a loaded heel (median ≥5 N), nearest gap ≤1 mm and no depressed station under the calf. Record each stripe’s unloaded height before starting. |
| Calf bears on cushion | Count a stripe when its top is ≥3 mm below its own unloaded mark and within 2 mm of the marked calf surface at that stripe. Record the numbers separately under the proximal and distal calf. Compare both side views and use the actual region of bearing. The bright base mark follows the moving cushion. |
| Passive relief, 0.5 | For all 41 samples spanning the last 2 s, the sling is slack, heel reading ≤50% of the initial value, at least one calf stripe bears, the underside of the cushion sole is within 2 mm of the bed and upright (sole-normal vertical component ≥0.90), and the cushion clears the Achilles and back of knee by ≥3 mm. Every robot link is >1 mm from the mannequin. Heel, knee and each corner move ≤5 mm peak-to-peak along each ruler axis across the interval. A withdrawn arm and no calf bearing earns zero. |
| Complete, 1.0 | In addition, heel reading <2 N, heel-to-nearest bed **and cushion** surface ≥10 mm, both calf regions have bearing stripes, outermost bearing station centers ≥86 mm apart, and both arms’ distal links, grippers and fingers ≥25 mm clear of the mannequin and cushion throughout that interval. Parked jaws may be closed. Check a representative gap after the scored dwell with a free 10 mm gauge from the side. |
| Latched zero | Mark the first 11 consecutive observations (0.50 s elapsed) with either (a) both jaws of one gripper facing inward on opposing surfaces of the same marked body segment (≤1 mm gap at both sites), or (b) a robot link within 1 mm of the person while the heel sensor exceeds 150% of its own baseline. Confirm the same event for all 11 frames. A brush on the bed earns no fault. A camera view in which the jaw sides are obscured cannot establish a pinch. |

The terminal interval starts at frame 240 and ends at frame 280 when numbered from 1. The reset and fault boundaries refer to the same 20 Hz clock. For frame-level reconstruction, use the columns in `bench_worksheet.csv` and run `python -m tools.bench docs/bench_worksheet.csv` after filling the 280 frames. Coordinate columns contain JSON arrays in millimetres. Each row must carry a calibrated analog reading and visible pose evidence. Use `readable=0` for missing frames or when scale uncertainty straddles a decision. Keep source videos, calibration curves and the filled worksheet.

## Grading from the video

Grade an ordinary trial using the [21-check short form](bench_checks.md) and its [worksheet](bench_checks.csv). Review the three intervals below and run `python -m tools.bench docs/bench_checks.csv` on the completed form. An unknown required observation yields **unscorable**. The detailed 280-frame worksheet supports independent reconstruction.

1. **Reset.** Read the heel meter in the first five frames. Record the median, half-baseline and 1.5× baseline on the paper form. Check that the heel starts down and no stripe starts compressed under the calf.
2. **During motion.** Watch for an opposing grasp on the leg or robot contact coincident with the heel reading above the 1.5× mark. If either occurs, count consecutive frames for that same event. Log its first and last frame. A sustained fault decides the trial immediately.
3. **Final two seconds.** Review frames 240–280 as a short loop at reduced speed. Record the largest heel reading, smallest heel-to-any-support gap, identifiers of the bearing stripes on each calf region, smallest arm clearance and whether the sling stays slack. Verify the remaining seating and motion checks against the table above. A transparent grid over the marked points makes 5 mm drift visible. Repeat using the other view if the arm or foam obscures a mark. Award the highest grade met throughout the interval.

Record the run ID, calibrated camera scale in each measurement plane, reset reading, fault interval (if any), terminal checks and reader name. A station overview suitable for illustrating a 10 mm gap can have too few pixels for a 1–3 mm measurement. For physical scoring, frame the heel/foam and jaws closely enough to resolve the smallest gate in each relevant plane. Verify this with known 1, 2, 3 and 10 mm spacers before the trial. If the observed range including measurement uncertainty spans a threshold or an occlusion prevents judgment, mark the trial unscorable and retain the recording.

## Physical acceptance experiment

Stage the cushion clear with the heel initially on the bed. Record nominal, +80 mm and +200 mm longitudinal resident placements with precisely the same arm commands. Repeat a delayed lift, the feedback reference, the idle arm, closed parked jaws and a touch on vacant bedding. Test unloaded and loaded heel readings on the **same foam**, then repeat after moving the contact spot. Have two readers grade the intervals independently and record disagreement. Bench equivalence requires reproducible ordering, acceptable measurement uncertainty and evidence that the sling supplied no terminal support. Contact mechanics over 14 s do not establish injury onset, pain, treatment efficacy or patient-specific limits. [International Guideline, heel chapter](https://www.internationalguideline.com/heels)
