# Reproduce the bedside comparison with an inert leg

The measurable question is whether the heel remains unloaded once the voluntary lift ends. Use a weighted mannequin for all trials. The following describes a proposed low-cost fixture, its commissioning tests and its limits. Physical repeatability and equivalence have yet to be measured.

## Parts and registration

| Part | Specification and setup |
|---|---|
| Bed and limb | Rigid level 820 × 1820 mm support surface. Fixed hip, sagittal hip and knee hinges. Hip-to-knee distance 375 mm. Upper segment 4.8 kg plus a 150 g knee shell. Working shin and foot together 3.00 kg, center of mass about 238 mm distal to knee. Reference heel-center position in the shin frame is 387 mm distal and 33 mm below the knee axis. Match the resting posture and approximately 18 N initial heel reading. Record actual mass, inertia estimate and hinge friction. |
| Cushion | Nominal footprint 164 × 258 mm, height about 77–79 mm at rest, mass 380 g. Mark six longitudinal stations numbered 0–5 at 43 mm pitch along the 258 mm dimension. Use soft open-cell foam with a thin removable cover on a free sliding base. Record dimensions, unloaded height, friction and compression. At each station, photograph displacement under repeated known loads on a 40 mm-wide pad. The nominal local simulation stiffness is 850 N/m. Continuous foam may compress and bulge differently from the six modeled strips. |
| One heel channel | Put a calibrated single-channel compression sensor on the mannequin’s posterior heel so it travels with the heel over bed **and** foam. A prototype can use a commercial analog FSR, a rigid backing and a thin floating heel cup that transfers the contact patch through a central puck onto the film. Keep a rim gap around the cup, retain it with a slack fabric sleeve and route the cable along the leg. Test the assembled cup at center and edges on both surfaces. A cup that bridges the gap, binds or misses an oblique contact fails qualification. Factory force ranges establish neither installed resolution nor accuracy. [Example FSR specifications](https://www.ohmite.com/res-fsr-rev-4.23/) |
| Visible marks | Two colors on the calf, contrasting marks on the heel and knee, four cushion corners, and a side tick at the center of each foam station referenced to its own base tick. Put a second set on the opposite cushion side or use a mirror. Add separate tendon and back-of-knee outlines. The modeled calf contact regions are centered about 176 and 292 mm proximal to the heel center. Use the actual mannequin landmarks. A universal 90–250 mm mattress stripe misses one center and moves relative to the leg during the offset intervention. |
| Recording | Synchronized plan and bedside views with rulers, or a bedside camera that sees both planes using a mirror. Include the heel meter display and the operator-side cord marks. Record at 20 Hz or at a known integer multiple. Put a 10 mm gauge and a 25 mm scale in the relevant plane during setup. Remove the gauge from the space beneath the heel before motion. The working gap includes the nearest edge of the cushion. |

## Manual voluntary lift

Use a wide soft ankle sling with free tails joined to a low-stretch cord. Route the line over an overhead pulley to an operator outside the robot workspace. Use an anchored support and cord with a supplier-rated working load for the measured peak tension. Guide the cord by hand throughout the raise and return. The line provides upward assistance only. Park the loop visibly slack, with clearance from the leg and cushion, after the lift. Keep the line away from the heel sensor. Mark the line for home, peak and return. The simulation uses an independent, upward-only knee moment. A sling supplies an external force with a different moment arm, so match the observed heel path and release timing by measurement.

1. Settle the mannequin on the bare mattress. With the robot parked, film five complete lift cycles. Use the supplied [idle-arm heel-height curves](idle_lift_profiles.csv) as the target for commissioning. Record when the heel clears, peak height and when the line is entirely slack. The nominal simulation starts its lift at 0.30 s, is near 182 mm heel-to-bed height by 1.35 s and plateaus at about 211 mm in MuJoCo. The return begins around 5.05 s and assistance ends by 6.20 s. Later profiles add 3.50 or 4.50 s to those cues. A 4 s drop would shorten the tested opportunity to position the cushion.
2. Mark the pulley stroke based on that specific mannequin. A 211 mm heel rise is **not** a 211 mm instruction for cord travel. For initial commissioning aim for peak height within 15 mm, release within 0.15 s and heel-to-bed height within 15 mm at each 0.25 s sample of the free-lift trace; keep all five recordings. These are proposed tolerances. For a PhysX reproduction use the separately labeled PhysX curve for that engine. Never pull a loaded heel to a commanded position.
3. On scored trials give the operator the clock cue independently of robot behavior. Let the leg settle naturally on the bed or cushion during return. Verify slack through the final two seconds. Record the actual heel trajectory, including differences caused by support. An obstructed or mistimed operator motion invalidates the trial. A misplaced cushion is a scored result.

## Calibrate the single heel channel

Tare the installed heel cup free of contact. Apply known loads at 0, approximately 1, 2, 5, 10, 18 and 30 N using weighed masses or a checked scale. Repeat loading and unloading at least three times and repeat at representative contact angles. Test for cable pull, drift over the full 14 s, edge bypass and foam hysteresis. Calibrate the film and readout together. Validate the initial relaxed baseline, the 2 N comparator, 50% relief, and 150% over-baseline indications at the episode baseline. Record uncertainty at each boundary. If the fitted channel cannot discriminate either side of a threshold, classify the affected trial as unscorable. A switch or free paper tab can corroborate a gap. Partial relief and excess loading require the **analog** reading. A reading below 2 N is reported as below the fixture threshold.

## Bench score sheet

| Condition | Bench reading at the 20 Hz scoring clock |
|---|---|
| Reset | The first five frames show a loaded heel (median ≥5 N), nearest gap ≤1 mm and no depressed station under the calf. Record each stripe’s unloaded height before starting. |
| Calf bears on cushion | Count a stripe when its top is ≥3 mm below its own unloaded mark and within 2 mm of the marked calf surface at that stripe. Record the numbers separately under the proximal and distal calf. Compare both side views and use the actual region of bearing. The bright base mark follows the moving cushion. |
| Passive relief, 0.5 | For all 41 samples spanning the last 2 s, the sling is slack, heel reading ≤50% of the initial value, at least one calf stripe bears, the sole is within 2 mm of the bed and upright (sole-normal vertical component ≥0.90), and the cushion clears the Achilles and back of knee by ≥3 mm. Every robot link is >1 mm from the mannequin. Heel, knee and each corner move ≤5 mm peak-to-peak along each ruler axis across the interval. A withdrawn arm and no calf bearing earns zero. |
| Complete, 1.0 | In addition, heel reading <2 N, heel-to-nearest bed **and cushion** surface ≥10 mm, both calf regions have bearing stripes, outermost bearing station centers ≥86 mm apart, and both arms’ distal links, grippers and fingers ≥25 mm clear of the mannequin and cushion throughout that interval. Parked jaws may be closed. Check a representative gap after the scored dwell with a free 10 mm gauge from the side. |
| Latched zero | Mark the first 11 consecutive observations (0.50 s elapsed) with either (a) both jaws of one gripper contacting opposite sides of the same marked body segment (≤1 mm jaw gap at each side), or (b) a robot link within 1 mm of the person while the heel sensor exceeds 150% of its own baseline. Confirm the same event for all 11 frames. A brush on the bed earns no fault. A camera view in which the jaw sides are obscured cannot establish a pinch. |

The interval starts at frame 240 and ends at frame 280 when numbered from 1. The reset and fault boundaries refer to the same 20 Hz clock. For frame-level reconstruction, use the columns in `bench_worksheet.csv` and run `python -m tools.bench docs/bench_worksheet.csv` after filling the 280 frames. Coordinate columns contain JSON arrays in millimetres. Each row must carry a calibrated analog reading and visible pose evidence. Use `readable=0` for missing frames or when scale uncertainty straddles a decision. Keep source videos, calibration curves and the filled worksheet.

## Grading from the video

A reader can score an ordinary trial by reviewing three intervals. The full CSV supports subsequent frame-by-frame reconstruction when needed.

1. **Reset.** Read the heel meter in the first five frames. Record the median, half-baseline and 1.5× baseline on the paper form. Check that the heel starts down and no stripe starts compressed under the calf.
2. **During motion.** Watch for an opposing grasp on the leg or robot contact coincident with the heel reading above the 1.5× mark. If either occurs, count consecutive frames for that same event. Log its first and last frame. A sustained fault decides the trial immediately.
3. **Final two seconds.** Review frames 240–280 as a short loop at reduced speed. Record the largest heel reading, smallest heel-to-any-support gap, identifiers of the bearing stripes on each calf region, smallest arm clearance and whether the sling stays slack. Verify the remaining seating and motion checks against the table above. A transparent grid over the marked points makes 5 mm drift visible. Repeat using the other view if the arm or foam obscures a mark. Award the highest grade met throughout the interval.

Record the run ID, calibrated camera scale in each measurement plane, reset reading, fault interval (if any), terminal checks and reader name. A station overview suitable for illustrating a 10 mm gap can have too few pixels for a 1–3 mm measurement. For physical scoring, frame the heel/foam and jaws closely enough to resolve the smallest gate in each relevant plane. Verify this with known 1, 2, 3 and 10 mm spacers before the trial. If the observed range including measurement uncertainty spans a threshold or an occlusion prevents judgment, mark the trial unscorable and retain the recording.

## Physical acceptance experiment

Stage the cushion clear with the heel initially on the bed. Record nominal, +80 mm and +200 mm longitudinal resident placements with precisely the same arm commands. Repeat a delayed lift, the feedback reference, the idle arm, closed parked jaws and a touch on vacant bedding. Test unloaded and loaded heel readings on the **same foam**, then repeat after moving the contact spot. Have two readers grade the intervals independently and record disagreement. Bench equivalence requires reproducible ordering, acceptable measurement uncertainty and evidence that the sling supplied no terminal support. Contact mechanics over 14 s do not establish injury onset, pain, treatment efficacy or patient-specific limits. [International Guideline, heel chapter](https://www.internationalguideline.com/heels)
