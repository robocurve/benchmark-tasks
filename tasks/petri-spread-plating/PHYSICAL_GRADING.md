# Physical specimen and grading

## Reference plate

Make a batch of plain water gel for this nonliving colour-transfer trial.
Use NOW Real Food Agar Powder, SKU 6410, from one recorded lot. The finished
mixture contains **1.50% agar by mass**. For 200.00 g, disperse 3.00 g of
powder in 197.00 g of distilled water. Simmer while stirring until dissolved,
then restore the total to 200.00 g with hot distilled water to account for
evaporation. Use the manufacturer's single-ingredient powder.
[Product identification](https://www.nowfoods.com/products/natural-foods/agar-powder)

Cool the dissolved mixture to 55 ± 3 °C, then pour into the specified
nominal 90 mm dishes on a level surface.
An 86.8 mm diameter disk 5.0 mm deep takes about 29.6 mL. Check the set
depth through the side against a scale (5.0 ± 0.3 mm). Cover and condition
plates on a level bench at 22 ± 1 °C. Use each plate 120 ± 10 min after
pouring, at the same temperature as the tracer. Stagger pours if needed.
Use four small ink marks on the **dry rim** at the ends of perpendicular
diameters. Reject a plate with visible surface water, pre-existing scratches,
bubbles in the grading area or condensate likely to drip onto it. Leave the
gel surface untouched. Record lot, pour time, depth and bench temperature.

## Tracer and reset

Use Brilliant Blue FCF (E133, CAS 3844-45-9, Supelco 80717), **2.00 mg
of supplied powder per mL** of final distilled-water solution. For 25.00 mL
weigh 50.0 mg, dissolve and bring to the final volume. Use a single capped,
labelled batch on the day of preparation. Record the dye lot. Take a clean
aliquot per run and discard the remainder of that aliquot.
[Tracer identification](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/BE/en/product/sial/80717)

Use a fresh plate for every run and a clean, rinsed, fully dry T-spreader.
Set the open dish level in its starting pose. Take a reference photo of the
undisturbed surface and rim ticks before dispensing. Using a calibrated pipette and
fresh tip, deliver **100 µL as one drop** at the gel centre from about
2 mm above it. Keep the tip off the gel and the lid interior dry. Start
timing when dispensing ends, close the lid immediately, and begin robot
control **10 ± 2 s after dispensing**. Keep unused plates covered. Replace
the plate if the drop runs to the rim, the tip touches the surface, or the
start window is missed. Discard the used gel after imaging.

## Endpoint evidence

Use the trial video to check closure, upright support, tool return, release
and the final one-second hold. Then lift the lid vertically without tipping
or contacting the gel. Take the coverage photo within **10 s of the end of
the hold**. Use the same camera position, scale, diffuse light, white backing
and locked exposure across trials. Record the time. Use the four rim marks
and scale to register the central 80 mm circle on the supplied
[`1 mm grading grid`](fabrication/grading-overlay.svg). Count the area of
visible blue tracer in the circle and in each quadrant, including fractional
boundary cells. Trace the visible boundary on the original-resolution photo
and divide the coloured area by the area of that same circle or quadrant.
For a close call, record the traced areas and compare the unrounded ratios
to the thresholds before displaying percentages. Keep the photo and traced
overlay together. Take a second image under
raking light with a 1 mm reference to identify physical gouges anywhere on
the gel, including outside the central circle. Compare against a pre-load
photo under the same light if a mark is ambiguous.

The formulation and timing define the physical reference. Wetting, absorption,
optical contrast and damage of this specimen have not been calibrated against
the simulated 5 µm film or 0.3 mm indentation thresholds.
