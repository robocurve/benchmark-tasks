# Manual feed and crank protocol

Use a commercial enclosed, manually fed crank sharpener mounted to the guided plate. Start from a fresh blunt round pencil, 140 mm long and 7 mm in diameter. Set the carriage to its centre, with travel of 5 mm either way on an eight-second cycle. Set the crank knob below the shaft and both arms in READY with open grippers. The included CAD defines the simulated fixture and sharpener envelope. The reference describes the simultaneous manual feed and crank operation. [Hand-crank sharpener patent, background paragraphs 0003–0005](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/72/28/61/9f0984daeaf3a9/US20110226383A1.pdf)

## Physical-trial score

Before the trial, mark a narrow circumferential witness band 40 mm from each blunt end. Hold the crank still and gently insert one end until it first contacts the cutter. Set the zero of a millimetre ruler attached to the housing parallel to the pencil at that witness-band position. Mark the crank with a high-contrast index. Return the pencil to the two rests and start the trial. Use a side camera square to the ruler and a second view that shows the crank, opening and rests. Take a close-up of the final tip alongside a ruler. Use a sharpener and stock that can yield a taper of at least 18 mm in an ordinary manual qualification trial.

Read the witness band against the housing ruler so carriage motion cancels. For feed credit, the crank must turn clockwise during inward travel. A pencil that is simply pre-inserted and held at one depth earns zero. Check that depth increases at successively deeper positions over the clockwise sequence. The taper length is the axial distance from the start of wood exposure at the shoulder to the intact tip. Judge the same sharpened end in the video and close-up. Apply the highest tier whose criteria are all met.

| Score | Visible measurement |
| --- | --- |
| 0.25 | At least 6 mm of axial advance and at least 6 mm of taper. |
| 0.50 | At least 12 mm of axial advance and at least 12 mm of taper. |
| 0.75 | At least 18 mm of axial advance and at least 18 mm of taper, with an intact exposed graphite point. |
| 1.00 | Meets the 0.75 tier and is laid across both rests, released and still for 1 s with both hands more than 100 mm away. |
| 0.00 | Any other outcome, a broken pencil or a pencil that drops to the floor. |

Use the two fixed rests as the return gauge. The numerical return gate permits ±15 mm in X, ±12 mm in Y, a low point 29–34 mm above the tabletop and up to 10° from the rest axis. The simulator uses speeds below 10 mm/s and 0.5 rad/s during the hold. For video judging, use the projected scale at the rests and verify the final pose stays constant within 10 mm and 10° for one second. Reset with a fresh pencil and clear the shavings after the trial.

## Simulated cutting response

A compliant native cutter face starts at the opening's effective first-contact depth. Only an aligned pencil in physical contact can cut. The taper grows during clockwise cutting, within an envelope of 2.4 mm of axial removal per net loaded turn. Measured pencil insertion past initial contact, less the 1.4 mm minimum spring compression, limits the actual taper depth. The resulting effective face retreats inside the housing, so continuing the cut requires advancing the pencil to maintain pressure. Reverse motion cancels loaded angular progress before any further material can be removed. Each end has separate angular progress and taper length.

The 780 N/m spring gives about 1.1–3.9 N over the admitted 1.4–5 mm compression range. The crank uses a zero-speed viscous drive, capped at 0.08 N m. Its authored gains are 0.006 N m s/degree unloaded and 0.026 N m s/degree when cutting. At the demonstration speed the cap sets a nominal tangential load of 2.3 N on the 35 mm crank. These are nominal bench-tool surrogate loads. They have not been measured on the commercial sharpener. Force and internal face motion never enter real-trial scoring. The visible taper grows with cut depth. The full pencil collider remains for contact stability, and the simulation omits teeth, shavings and fracture.

## Recorded comparison

The progressive-feed demo scored 1.00 in 59.30 s. The pencil moved 17.90 mm axially during productive cutting after its initial preload. The native cutter face retreated 17.90 mm and the released pencil finished with an 18 mm taper. The fixed-depth control used the same cutter and scoring with a fixed left-hand axial target. It completed 10.00 clockwise turns while producing a 0.23 mm taper and scored 0.00. An independent replay reproduced the scoring in both recordings from native pose traces.
