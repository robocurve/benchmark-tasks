# Open a flat shopping tote, pack two groceries and lift it by both handles

Put the box of crackers and the pack of toilet rolls into the bag so that both rest on the bag's base and the bag stands upright on its own. Then lift the bag by its two handles, set it back down, let go and move both arms away.

At a grocery checkout a reusable tote can arrive folded flat. Open its walls, pack the small carton and the bulky bundle, and lift the loaded bag by both handles. O*NET lists bagging merchandise among cashier duties (41-2011.00).

## Build the tote

Use the [dimensioned cutting pattern](docs/tote-pattern.svg). All dimensions are in mm. The finished targets are a 310 × 160 base board, a rim 180 above the tabletop when straightened, and an empty mass of 109 g including both handles. Allow about 25–30 minutes for assembly with the pieces cut and a sewing machine threaded. Cutting, making the groceries and checking the build take additional time.

| Material for one tote | Typical source | Estimated USD for material used |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Green 80 g/m² spunbond non-woven polypropylene (PP), one 700 × 550 piece (save scraps for four 32 × 32 root pockets) | Fabric supplier or reusable-bag stock | $1–3 |
| 2–3 mm corrugated PP sign board, one 310 × 160 piece | Sign-shop offcut | $0–2 |
| Cream 16 mm (5/8 in) PP webbing, 0.5 m for four legs and hinge tabs | Fabric or outdoor sewing shop | $0.50–2 |
| Craft wood strips, 12 × 4 section for four 86 mm legs and four 25 mm root blocks, plus two 148 × 14 × 2 grip inserts | Hobby shop or wood offcuts | $1–3 |
| Soft closed-cell craft foam, two 148 × 16 × 8 blanks, and thin cream fabric, two 165 × 65 sleeve blanks | Craft shop or packing scraps | $0.50–2 |
| Polyester thread, cloth tape and plain paper for adjusting mass | Sewing and office supplies | $0.50–2 |

Budget about $4–14 per tote using offcuts and shared supplies. A ruler, scissors, craft knife, cutting mat and sewing machine suffice. The board and webbing are standard sign-making and sewing materials. [2 mm board stock](https://coroplastsheets.com/2mmCorrugatedPlasticSheets.html) · [Polypropylene webbing by the yard](https://www.seattlefabrics.com/Standard-Polypropylene-Webbing_c_347.html)

1. **Cut and mark.** Cut the green shell as one cross. Mark the 310 × 160 floor, the four creases 90 up from the floor folds, the 10 mm rim turnbacks, and the four 10 mm corner flanges on the end panels. The drawing includes all allowances. Cut the board square to size, ease its sharp tips, and cut the handle parts in the table. Keep the mating ends of the wood at the hinge square.
2. **Sew the shell.** Turn the four rim hems 10 to the outside and stitch. Bring each end flange around the outside of the adjoining long wall. Align the flange fold with the long wall's raw side edge and stitch 5 from that edge through both layers, floor to rim. Use a 3 mm straight stitch and backstitch at the ends. Finger-press one horizontal crease per wall. Push the end creases outward and the long creases inward, bringing the rim back above the board edges. Ease the cloth at the four corners into soft tucks. Keep the four walls flexible between floor and rim.
3. **Make the handles.** On the outside of each long wall, mark leg centrelines 88 and 222 from the same corner. Cut four 115 mm lengths of webbing. Each has a 25 mm tail below the rim and 90 above. Centre each tail on a 32 × 32 scrap of shell fabric, flush at the rim end, and sew the tail to the scrap. With webbing facing out, place a 25 mm root block between scrap and wall, its square end flush with the rim. Stitch the scrap to the wall snugly around both sides and the foot of the block. Catch the two upper corners of the pocket with thread to retain the block. Put an 86 mm leg stiffener directly above each block, also on the wall-facing side of the webbing. Butt the square ends together at the rim and secure the moving stiffener to its strap with tight cloth-tape bands at 8 and 65 above the rim. The continuous webbing across the **outside** faces is the hinge tab. When swung outward the square ends part. When pushed inward the ends bear against each other. Keep the hinge line clear of tape and stitches.
4. **Pad the grips.** Centre one 148 mm crosspiece across each pair of legs behind their tips, its upper edge 89 above the rim. Bind the overlaps firmly with thread through the webbing and around the wood. Slit the foam lengthwise around the crosspiece, relieving the foam where it overlaps the legs. Wrap the cream sleeve around the foam, turn the ends under and stitch the sleeve closed. Trim foam or add thin foam shims before closing to finish at **150 long × 18 wide × 10 thick**, measured uncompressed. The grip spans the full loop width. Its lower edge is 72 above the rim and its far edge is 90 above it. Check both loops swing freely out to the reset position.
5. **Fit the board and weigh.** Lay the board flat inside the shell with its smooth top exposed to the groceries. Weigh the finished tote. If light, tape flat sheets of paper evenly across the underside of the board until the assembly weighs 109 g (one board-sized sheet of 80 g/m² paper adds about 4 g). Include the tape in the final weighing. If heavy, remove packing paper or use lighter stock within the specified board thickness. Keep the board level and the walls free of ballast.

### Physical acceptance check

Run this check **after building and before each session**, using the same tote and two groceries the robot will handle. Use a mm ruler, a gram scale, a 0–5 N spring scale, a straightedge and a timer. Record the measurements and date for that set of objects.

1. **Size and mass.** Measure and record the board, straightened floor-to-rim height, crease height, both loops and grips, and the closed groceries against the dimensions above. Weigh each article separately. Accept masses of **99–119 g** for the assembled empty tote, **270–290 g** for the carton and **350–370 g** for the wrapped pack.
2. **Collapse on the marks.** On the level tabletop centre the board at (360, 70), long side along x. Fold the two ends outward, then the right (−y) and left (+y) large walls inward, doubling each wall back at its crease. Lay both handles on the tabletop, grip centres at (360, −85) and (360, 227). The simulated folded body spans x 119–601 and y −9–149, with a nominal height of 26. Release the empty bag. Sight across the entire bag, including the handles, with a straightedge supported on two 30 mm spacers outside its footprint. It passes if the highest point is **≤30 mm** above the table without compression.
3. **Stand under load.** Open the walls and seat both articles directly on the board. Put the upright carton across the near end, 60 side along x, with the pack beside it towards the far end, 200 side along x. Release walls, handles and groceries. With rulers or 144 mm reference marks beside the bag, observe the lowest point of each of the four top edges throughout **10 continuous seconds**. The board must stay on its marks and all four edges must stay **≥144 mm above the tabletop**, with only the bag and its contents supporting them. Record the minimum height for each wall.
4. **Handle stops.** Remove the groceries. Hold one large wall vertical immediately below its two fixed blocks, leaving the handle free. Viewed end-on, the inner face of that wall at the rim defines the vertical mouth line. Raise the grip from outside. Loop the spring scale around its midpoint and pull horizontally towards the opposite wall to **2 N**. Sight along the rim. The grip's innermost edge must remain on or outside the line at 2 N. Release and swing it back outward. Repeat on the other wall and record both results.

If a check fails, correct the build and repeat the check before using it. For a trial, remove measuring tools, collapse the bag again and return the groceries to the centres and orientations in task.json. The lab's readings document the physical build. The run scores below are from Isaac.

## Real-trial grading

Scored on the state at the end of the trial (the run limit, or as soon as the 1.0 state has lasted 2 seconds). Nothing is scored for the order in which the items go in or for how they are held. Definitions. An item rests on the base: its lowest point is within 3 mm of the bag's base board, it stands on one of its faces (that face within 10 degrees of the board) and, seen from above, it lies wholly within the board's outline; an item lying on a folded-in wall is higher than that and does not count. The bag stands upright: its base board is on the table (lowest point within 5 mm of it), level within 10 degrees, its middle within 50 mm of its tape marks, and the top edge of each of its four walls is at least 144 mm above the table (80 % of 180). The lift is done once, for 2 seconds without a break, the base board's lowest point is at least 50 mm above the table, both handle grips are pulled at least 30 mm above the bag's top edge and both items remain in the bag. In the lift, each item’s centre stays over the base outline and its lower edge stays below the top of the bag. Check that neither exits on the video. Check the 3 mm base gap only after set-down. 1.0: both items rest on the base, the bag stands upright, the lift is done, and for the last 2 seconds no part of the robot touches the bag or an item and both grippers' centre points are at least 50 mm from the bag (its outline up to its top edge, and its handle grips). 0.75: both items rest on the base and the bag stands upright, but the lift is not done or the robot has not let go. 0.5: the pack of toilet rolls rests on the base and the bag stands upright (the box is anywhere). 0.25: only the box of crackers rests on the base and the bag stands upright. 0: anything else, including a bag that at the end is held up, lying over, slumped or more than 50 mm off its marks. Also 0, whatever happens later, once an item has fallen off the worktable, an item has come out of the bag while the bag was off the table, or a corner seam of the bag has been pulled more than 30 mm apart (torn). On a real trial: film from the side; at the end hold a ruler against each wall, photograph the bag from above and from the side, and try a 3 mm card under each item inside the bag. Possible scores: 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0.

Film the lift from the side with a 50 mm height reference. Use the end view or an overhead view to confirm the two groceries remain in the bag. Check their seating only after set-down. [Cashier tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/41-2011.00).

## Run one trial

Use Python 3.12, Isaac Sim 6.1, Isaac Lab-Arena 0.3.1, Inspect Robots 1.0.0,
inspect-robots-yam 0.38.0 and yam-arena 0.1.0 on Linux with an NVIDIA GPU.
The stock YAM station, arm meshes and cameras are runtime dependencies; they are not bundled.
Prepare the [pinned YAM Lab-Arena runtime](https://github.com/kairosiann/yam-isaac-lab-arena/tree/bd62771e60320433893bd1d2df0fc79b5ddcd6d1) and install its Dream client adapter at commit b5e936a201cec66c54c297e522c920d98445e339. Install this folder in that environment:

```sh
uv pip install --no-deps .
python -m rdb_bag_load.run scripted --out out/scripted
python -m rdb_bag_load.run dream --out out/dream
```

The Dream run requires a Dream Labs credential configured through its client. No credential is included.
Both runs use Inspect Robots, the same scene and scorer, seed 0 and zero object jitter.
Run limit: 2,400 steps (2 minutes at 20 Hz). Use the default layout; the controls in the recorded qualification summary are not the submitted trial.

| Recorded run | Score | Steps | Wall time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scripted physical-feasibility reference | 1.0 | 1,620 | 540 s |
| Dream-1 | 0.0 | 2,400 | 686 s |

Dream-1 receives only 320 x 176 RGB from the overhead and two wrist cameras, plus joint and gripper positions.
The scripted reference uses simulator object state for feedback; it is not an observation-matched model baseline
or a demonstration of perception from RGB. It commands robot joints and grippers without writing object poses.
The review-only side camera is never a model input.

Each run writes `RESULT.json`, `media/setup.png`, `media/top_cam.mp4`, `media/side_cam.mp4`
and `media/grader-trace.jsonl`. Read `score` in `RESULT.json` for the final score.
The scripted command exits successfully only at full score. Startup and model latency add wall time.
