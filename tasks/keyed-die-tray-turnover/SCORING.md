# Grading a tray prepared for backside inspection

Use the marked, clipped corner as upper right in each tray's own face-up view. Rows below run from the far edge (top) to the near edge. A dot means empty. The reflection is a consequence of mating the indexed trays face to face. Photograph the actual four-coupon source at reset and keep that photo with the trial.

**Check the last second.** Film the hands and jig for at least a second after release. The receiving tray must rest level in its receiving holder, and neither arm may touch the tray, receiving carrier or its rotating handle. All nine mouths must be clear of the source tray and its carrier from above. A converging or ambiguous camera angle calls for a second view. A bench scorer can take photographs after the arms have stopped. Use a camera close enough to resolve the coupon edges. Put a ruler or fixed grid beside the jig to register the views. The jig may finish on either side of the pivot.

**Award 0.25 for each expected pocket** occupied throughout that second by the same single released, supported coupon, entirely seated with its plain face up. A pocket gets zero for a missing, upside-down, perched, doubled or misplaced coupon. A coupon in a spare pocket earns zero. Four correct pockets with four supplied coupons account for the entire stock. Swapping two indistinguishable coupons before the final hold preserves the visible pocket map. Score zero for the whole tray if the handoff conditions above fail. A photograph alone cannot show release over time or distinguish every perched edge from a seated one.

**Physical grading.** Photograph the tray normal to its key and take an oblique close-up with diffuse light. A loose flat reference coupon in an identical pocket has its plain back 0.50 mm below the rim. For credit, all four corners of the visible back must be at least 0.40 mm below the surrounding rim. This allows 0.10 mm above nominal seating. A coupon jammed between the walls can be entirely below the rim and still fail. Check face polarity against a striped reference. Use the video and the rim against a fixed grid to verify the supported, level, released one-second hold.

**Checking a disputed corner without moving the die.** Mount a fixed-focus inspection microscope or USB microscope on a graduated vertical fine-focus stand. Lock the optics and magnification. Keeping the optical axis normal to the tray, focus on the adjacent rim, zero the vertical travel and focus on the edge of the plain back at each corner. Read the travel, approaching focus from the same direction. Keep the rim local to each reading or level the tray before the trial. Do the check on the stationary ending, before touching the tray. Verify the arrangement first on a flat reference and on the same coupon raised by a measured 0.20 mm shim in a spare pocket. Their nominal recesses are 0.50 and 0.30 mm respectively, on opposite sides of the 0.40 mm limit. If glare, focus repeatability or uncertainty straddles the limit, repeat with better magnification and preserve both images; an inconclusive measurement does not establish credit. No probe bears on the coupon. Preserve the images and hold video before lifting any suspect double by its edges to count it. Score the four pockets separately.

**Simulator implementation.** Use the last 21 poses spanning one second at 20 Hz. The receiving tray touches its holder at every sample, its normal is within 10° of up, and no rim corner moves more than 2 mm from its final position. Check exposure against the source tray and carrier above all nine mouths. The release check includes all 18 reporting robot links. For a credited pocket all eight corners fit the 10.9 mm mouth with 0.05 mm numerical edge allowance. The topmost corner is at least 0.40 mm below the rim, the lowest corner is no more than 0.10 mm below the nominal floor (solver tolerance), and the back normal points up (dot product above 0.8). The same free die touches the receiver and stays released at every sample. Another die overlapping the mouth at floor height or above disqualifies that pocket.
