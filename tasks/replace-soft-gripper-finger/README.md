# Replace a worn soft gripper finger

A spare parallel gripper with soft fin-ray fingers lies flat in a fixture between two YAM arms. One finger is worn. The arms must lift off its fork plate, take the worn finger out and put it in the red bin, seat the
new finger from the tray rather than the used spare beside it and refit the plate. After the swap, the same fine placement is repeated for three M3 screws, each picked from a block and dropped into its hole. Moving the good finger out of place, or knocking any finger, plate or screw off the table, scores 0, and so does shoving the worn finger out without lifting it.

## Requirements

- `yam-isaac-lab-arena` with Isaac Lab-Arena 0.3.1 installed (`source <ARENA_DIR>/yam.env`), as on the Brev launchable.
- `fingerkit.py`, `usdkit.py`, `umi_parts/` and `textures/` are in this folder. They build the gripper, fingers, fork plates, tray and bin: importing `fingerkit` hooks `yam_arena.scene` without modifying it, so the parts, declared
  as subclasses of `Box`, spawn from USD files the kit writes at start-up.

## Run a trial

From this folder:

```bash
inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_arena -E task=task.py -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 \
    -E cam_width=320 -E cam_height=176 --policy dream -P rows_per_request=20 --scorer task_score --max-steps 2400 \
    --instruction "One soft finger on this spare gripper is worn. Its three screws are already out, in the white block. Replace the finger with a new one, put the worn finger in the red bin, put the fork plate back on the new finger and drop the three screws into their holes." \
    --store-frames --log-dir out
```

## Scripted demo and failure

`bash run_scripted.sh new OUT_DIR` runs the demo (score 1.0): the right arm lifts the fork plate and holds it aside, the left arm takes the worn finger by its root, drops it in the bin and seats the new one, the right arm refits
the plate, then the left arm picks each screw from the block and drops it into its hole. `HOLE_OFFSETS` in `task.py` corrects the arm's position error at the holes, from a logged run. `bash run_scripted.sh used OUT_DIR` runs the same moves with the used spare and scores 0.5. The plans are open loop, so run them with `-E object_jitter=0`.

`inspect-robots video <log>.json` writes one MP4 per camera, and `python make_video.py <frames dir> out.mp4 --setup setup.png` joins them and writes the setup screenshot.

## Scoring

`--scorer task_score` prints the total. Per-step `info` is not saved, so the kit also writes the state to `finger_scores/score_*.json` (or `$LIFT_SCORE_DIR`) every 10 control steps and when the score changes.
`fingerkit.task_score` applies the scoring text in `task.json`, and `_Tracker` latches the good finger's move and any part leaving the table, which do not recover, and the worn finger's lift by a gripper.

## Runtime and seeds

The scene has no random parts with `-E object_jitter=0`, and the seed does not change it. All runs used seed 0.

A Dream-1 trial of 2,400 steps (120 s simulated) took 381 s of wall time on an RTX 5090, with `-P rows_per_request=20` and cameras at 320 x 176. The scripted demo of 1,552 steps (77.6 s simulated) took 242 s at 640 x 360.

The slowest 30 steps took 5.05 s at 320 x 176 with nothing else on the GPU, and 9.15 s during the Dream-1 run. At 20 steps per call, a session stays under the gateway's limit of about 8 s without a request. `bash run_dream.sh OUT_DIR "<instruction>" 20 2400` runs the trial and stops it if the session is lost.

## Asset sources

- Gripper body, finger holders and soft fingers: the real parts of the published UMI gripper, credited to its authors (Chi et al., Universal Manipulation Interface, 2024, https://umi-gripper.github.io/), taken from its print files `CE3S1_0.6_finger.3mf`, `_gripper_covers.3mf` and `_gripper_internals.3mf`, linked from its hardware guide, by `import_umi.py`. Licence: its code is MIT; its CAD states no licence, so the meshes in `umi_parts/` stay under their authors' terms and this task's licence does not cover them.
- Our own, generated by `fingerkit.py` with `usdkit.py`: the M3 screws and nuts at the parts' real holes, the linear rail, the marker tag, the cradle, fixture, tray and bin, and the wear on the worn finger. We placed the parts ourselves from their holes and the rack over its gear, moving the body 2 cm further from the fingers so that a hand on the fork clears its mirror wings.
- Textures, from ambientCG (CC0): Metal032 (`steel_*`), Plastic012A (`plastic_*`), Rubber004 (`rubber_*`, and `worn_rubber`, a pale scuffed version). Our own, generated by `make_textures.py`: `print_white`, `tpu_orange`, `tpu_worn`, `pla_normal`.
- Arms, table, lights and cameras: the harness's station, unchanged.
