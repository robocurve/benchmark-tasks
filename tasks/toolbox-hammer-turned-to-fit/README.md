# Close a toolbox propped open by a hammer, latch it and padlock it through its eye

Locking up a toolbox before leaving a job. A claw hammer has been tossed into a 19 in plastic toolbox: its head lies on
the tote tray and its handle sticks straight out over the front rim, so the lid, let down, rests on the handle and stands
2.7 cm open at the front. The robot has to hold the lid up, swing the hammer round flat into the tray, shut the lid,
fasten both latches, and then padlock the box: carry the open padlock in under the padlock eye so the shackle's free leg
stands over the eye's holes, and press the shackle down through them until the lock snaps shut.

Instruction: `Close the toolbox, fasten both latches and padlock it, then let go.`

## What makes it hard

- **The hammer.** It is 29 cm long and the box is 23.4 cm deep inside, so pointing front to back it can never get below
  the rim; it fits only turned toward the box's long side. The box stands 5.4 cm in front of a wall, which stops the lid
  at about 50 deg, short of upright, so the lid falls back onto the hammer if it is let go. One hand holds the lid up
  while the other lifts the handle off the rim and swings the hammer about its head until it lies flat in the tray.
- **The padlock.** The padlock eye is a tab on the lid's front edge lying just over a matching tab on the body; each
  sticks out 34 mm and has a 13 mm square hole, and the shackle is 9.5 mm (3/8 in) thick (about 1.75 mm of play each
  side). Open, the
  shackle's free leg stands 22 mm above the lock body; the lock has to be brought in so the 9 mm stack of tabs slides
  into that gap, the leg ending up over the holes, then the shackle pushed down through both holes while the other hand
  holds the lock. Out of line, the shackle won't go down.

### Padlock eye fixture

Stanley does not publish the size of the box's moulded padlock eye, so the trial uses a bolt-on eye of known size
that fits the lock above. Cut two 54 mm pieces of 40 x 3 mm aluminium flat bar, bend the last 20 mm of each to 90 deg as a
screw flange (leaving a tab 40 mm wide sticking out 34 mm), and in each make a 13 mm square hole (drill 12 mm, file square) centred 22 mm from the box face. Screw the body tab to
the box front at the middle, its top face 2 mm below the lid seam, and the lid tab to the lid's front edge so that,
shut, it lies 3 mm above the body tab with the holes in line (check by passing a 12 mm rod through both). The YAM's stock finger pads handle the hammer by its wooden handle only and push the latches and lock; no pad
protection is fitted. Deburr every cut edge, hole and corner of both tabs with a file and fine sandpaper (break the edges to about 0.5 mm) so they can't nick the gripper pads. The lock's
3/8 in shackle passes the 13 mm holes with about 1.75 mm of play each side, with the free leg in the holes (22 mm out) its 24 mm inside width puts the
long leg's inner side about 51 mm out, past the tabs' 34 mm tips, and its 25 mm inside height clears the 9 mm stack.

Wall stop check (once, after taping the box down with its back 5.4 cm from the wall, nothing in it): swing the lid
up until it rests against the wall and read its angle from the box top with a phone inclinometer app; it must stop
at 45-55 deg (sim about 50 deg), and let go there it must fall back shut by itself. If it stays up, move the box 1 cm
closer to the wall and check again; if it stops below 45 deg, move it 1 cm away.

Trial lock spec (checked once when the lock is chosen): popped open, its free leg stands 20-24 mm above the body
(calipers; the sim uses 22 mm, leaving the 9 mm tab stack about 13 mm of room), and pushed straight down it clicks shut
under 2-6 N (press it onto a kitchen scale; the sim needs about 3 N). A No. 5 outside this range is swapped for another.

## Scoring (real trial)

End photo, the trial video and a 2 mm feeler gauge; after the photo the referee unlocks the padlock with its key
and opens the lid to check the hammer. Score 0 if the box moves more than 2 cm or any part of the hammer
is outside the box or above its rim. Latched = latch flat over the lid's lip and the 2 mm feeler not entering under
the lid beside it. Padlocked = the shackle's free leg passes down through both tabs' holes and the shackle is snapped
shut (a tug on the lock body doesn't open it). A hammer left above the rim keeps the lid from closing flush (the
feeler then enters under it), but a hammer knocked out onto the bench would not show from the shut lid, so the box is
opened after the photo; the sim scorer checks the hammer's position directly (below).

| score | end state |
|---|---|
| 1.0 | both latches latched and the box padlocked; both grippers 5 cm or more from the box and the lock |
| 0.5 | both latches latched, not padlocked; same release rule |
| 0 | otherwise |

The sim scorer checks the same: every corner of the hammer's handle and head inside the walls (3 mm tolerance) and no
more than 2 mm above the rim; a latch within 3 deg of shut with the lid's front edge within 2 mm of shut; the shackle
within 2 mm of home and in line (within 6 deg) with its free leg inside the holes' 13 mm square and reaching from below
the body tab into the lid tab; every gripper point 5 cm or more from the box, its open latches and the hanging lock.

## Who does this / why it matters

- Padlock: Master Lock's page for the No. 5 lists a 2 in (51 mm) body, 1 in (25 mm) shackle length, 15/16 in (24 mm)
  shackle width and 3/8 in (10 mm) shackle diameter (https://www.masterlock.com/products/product/5); McMaster-Carr's
  listing gives its body as 1 5/8 in tall and 1 1/4 in thick.
- Locking a toolbox is the last step of packing up on a job site, and a tool left lying across the tray is the usual
  reason the lid won't shut. The box: Stanley's page for the 19-in ESSENTIAL Tool Box lists "Dimensions :
  48.2 x 25.4 x 25cm." (https://www.stanleytools.co.uk/product/stst1-75520/stanley-19-essential-tool-box).

## How this differs from close tasks

- **Holding a part down while the other hand turns or fastens something on it**: here the lid is held *up*, out of the
  way, while the other hand repositions a loose rigid object inside the box.
- **Snap-fit closing**: the latches snap, but they are one step of three; the padlock step is a two-hand threading of
  a rigid U through two stacked holes with about 1.75 mm of play each side, the lock carried in one hand and pressed by the other.
- **Padlocking a lockout hasp or tag point**: closing a padlock through holes is only the last of three steps here.
  The skill the task turns on is first clearing the obstruction: seeing that the hammer, not the latches, keeps the
  lid up, and turning it to fit under the lid while the other hand holds the lid open against the wall stop.

## Scripted demo

The right hand lifts the lid by its front grip and holds it against the wall stop while the left lifts the hammer's
handle off the rim and swings it round flat into the tray; the lid is let down, both latches pushed shut, then the left
hand carries the open padlock in under the eye and holds it while the right presses the shackle down through both
holes until it catches. Both hands let go and park. It scores 1.0 at step 997 of 1,100; the end frames show the lock
hanging shut from the eye.

## Fidelity

Rigid box, fixed (walls and tray as boxes; moulded ribs and curves left out); the lid is one solid rigid body on the back hinge, stopped at about 50 deg by the wall. Each latch is one
rigid colliding body on a hinge with its hook at the tip; a spring pulls it shut once within 15 deg of shut (open stop
30 deg). The hammer is a free rigid body of boxes (a rectangular handle and a box head with no claw curve) at the 29 cm
hammer's length, colliding with the tray, rim and lid. Padlock eye: the body
tab is fixed to the box, the lid tab welded to the lid, at the fixture's size. Padlock: a rigid body at the No. 5's
size; the shackle (three welded square-section bars standing in for the round 3/8 in bar) sits on a
slide-and-turn joint. Open, a spring holds it about 2 cm up (about 3 N to push it home); it turns only when pulled fully up,
can't be pushed down unless in line with the body and can't turn once partly down (both enforced by stiff joint
springs); pushed within 2.5 mm of home it catches and
stays locked (there is no key in the task). The lock's rise (22 mm) and push to close (about 3 N) sit inside the trial lock spec in the fixture section; its catch point is modelled.

## Reset

Unlock and remove the padlock and stand it open at its mark. Swing both latches open, open the lid, set the hammer head
against its stop block with the handle straight out over the rim mark, and let the lid down onto the handle. The box
stays taped at its marks, 5.4 cm from the wall.
