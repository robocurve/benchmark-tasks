# Physical model and validation boundaries

Coordinates use the unchanged YAM station: x forward from the arm bases, y to the left, z above the 747.5 mm worktop. Robot bases are at (0, ±305, 750) mm. The toaster's tray origin is at (730, 170, 20) mm, with the appliance oriented so its ordinary front pull handle points toward the robots. The housing is 1.6 kg on four native rubber feet, 300 × 170 × 190 mm overall. Unpublished dimensions, inertia and friction are reconstruction estimates. Straight rails and a rear stop reproduce withdrawal and seating; no particular OEM click-lock is modeled or credited.

The tray is a free compound rigid body with a 260 × 140 × 0.4 mm steel floor, four 6 mm flanges and a connected ABS pull handle. Its authored component volumes determine mass, center of mass and diagonal inertia approximation. The forty aerated dry-bread pieces have individual mass and inertia from their actual analytic-box dimensions and an estimated density of 180 kg/m³. Their collision and visual bodies use the same box geometry; no convex-hull approximation can silently shrink them. Surface friction estimates are steel 0.35, bread 0.55, ABS 0.60 and rubber feet 0.90, with zero restitution. Contact offset is 0.1 mm and rest offset zero.

Task actors use 64 position and four velocity solver iterations. Native CPU PhysX sweep CCD is enabled on the scene and the task bodies, with speculative CCD for angular motion; RTX still renders the cameras on the selected GPU. The task integrates native physics at 960 Hz while the unchanged stock robot accepts targets at 20 Hz. At 240 Hz a small falling chunk can traverse a 4 mm bin floor in one discrete step, and a rapidly tumbling chunk can rotate a corner through that floor despite sweep CCD. A smaller step resolves the actual millimeter-scale contact. Continuous collision detection preserves the actual thin metal tray and plastic bin dimensions rather than changing collision thickness or tolerances. The [Isaac Sim physics API](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/latest/py/source/extensions/isaacsim.core.simulation_manager/docs/api.html) confirms that sweep CCD and GPU dynamics cannot be enabled together. Sixteen velocity iterations, inherited from an earlier mechanical apparatus, produced numerical energy growth in an untouched initial scene. A one-parameter contrast with four velocity iterations kept the actual appliance stationary and all forty chunks in the installed tray. The robot's stock actuators, gains and effort limits were not changed. Failed development runs and reset histories are retained in the separate source-repository history; they are excluded from success evidence.

Score after the action or at the trial limit. The tray counts as withdrawn once its rear edge clears the opening by 5 mm. With the tray withdrawn at least once, award 0.25 when all 40 pieces can be accounted for in the tray or bin, or 0.6 when the tray is empty and all 40 are inside the bin below the rim. An unaccounted-for piece (including a spill on the worktop) earns 0 at that observation. Award 1.0 when all 40 remain in the bin and the upright tray stays home in its guides, hands clear, for 1 s. Compare both ends of the external pull against the initial home photos: within 5 mm in each direction, with no more than 5 mm of metal floor protruding. Direct handling of pieces, a fallen object, housing displacement over 20 mm or tilt over 10°, or invalid input voids the trial. Otherwise score 0.

Simulation uses piece centres to count the contents of each receptacle. Tray contents take precedence when the two receptacles overlap. The seating check compares both ends of the 44 mm pull with their housing-fixed home positions along all three axes, checks the upper face points up, and checks the front edge of the metal floor against the fascia. The first qualifying sample starts the timer; one second spans at least 961 samples at 960 Hz.

The scripted reference reads the actual tray pose and corrects ordinary stock joint targets for motor deflection and grip rotation. During reinsertion it first levels and aligns the tray, settles both leading floor corners above the actual rails, and follows the actual free housing frame. Its integral TCP target stays within 25 mm per coordinate of measured TCP, with a 30 mm minimum commanded height to prevent pushing into the worktop when rail contact constrains the held tray. These are reference-controller safeguards, not scorer inputs. The native guides and stop, actual final poses, debris containment and released stability determine completion.

The task begins with a cooled and unplugged appliance; no unperformed electrical operation is inferred. It ends after dry crumb emptying and reinsertion. Washing, electrical behavior, thermal hazards, adhesive burnt residue, bread deformation and fine dust are not modeled. The reference results are fixed-start simulator results and require later hardware calibration. The later Dream-1 evaluation is described below.

## Separate completed model evidence

The stock `dreamlabs/dream-1-beta` baseline completed one seed-0 episode at score **0.0**, using all 1,100 declared actions and 52,800 native grading samples. Both original policies used the same recording-time source, scene, scorer, physics rate and action budget. The model receives only the three stock RGB cameras and measured 14-D joint/jaw state; it has no access to object poses, contacts, grades, the reference controller or the review camera. Every native grade and the full model-input/action provenance were audited separately.

See the [model result and audits](evidence/dream/RESULT-SUMMARY.json) and the [evaluation protocol](EVALUATION.md). The current package validation binds the earlier scripted evidence and later model outcome without changing either historical recording. The original candidate validation snapshots remain preserved. Complete model videos and later audits are kept separately from those original archives. A single fixed-start model outcome is not a success-rate or hardware-robustness estimate.
