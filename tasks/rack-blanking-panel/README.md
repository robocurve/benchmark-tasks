# Fit a blanking panel at U6

A 19-inch two-post rack stands on the table, its front facing the arms. A server, a switch and a patch panel are
installed in it, and U4 to U7 are empty. A 1U blanking panel stands in a slotted stand in front of the rack. The
instruction is: **"Fit the blanking panel at U6."**

To do it, the robot has to:
- find U6: units are counted from the bottom, and the posts carry the unit numbers and a line at each unit boundary;
- pick the panel out of its stand, carry it to the rack and line the clips at its two ends up with U6's middle holes;
- push both ends home against the posts, then let go.

Each unit has three square holes. They are 15.9 mm apart within a unit and 12.7 mm apart across a unit boundary. A
panel whose clips go one hole too high or too low straddles two units: it leaves a third of U6 open and covers part
of U5 or U7. This is a common mistake when fitting rack equipment by hand.

Closing every empty unit with a blanking panel is standard data-centre practice. Without the panels, hot exhaust air
flows back through the gaps into the cold aisle. Fitting equipment and panels at the units a rack elevation names is
a basic step in building out a data centre. The task tests three things:
- **Read a unit number off a dense, repeating pattern.** The holes repeat at 15.9, 15.9 and 12.7 mm, and the unit
  lines every 44.45 mm, so a robot is easily one hole off and has to see it.
- **Line up two points 46.5 cm apart.** The panel is 48 cm wide, so both clips have to end up in their holes, one at
  each end. The demo uses both arms. One arm can also do it, because the panel weighs 120 g.
- **Finish the job.** The panel has to stay in place once the robot lets go.

Peg-in-hole benchmarks give the robot one hole to aim at, for example the Factory and IndustReal assembly sets and
the NIST task board. Here every hole looks the same, and the right one is named only by a unit number counted on the
rack. The other data-centre tasks on the board work with servers, drives, fans and cables, not rack units.

## Files

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `task.py` | The yam_arena task file: the rack, the stand and the panel, the monitor, `success()` and `score()` |
| `geometry.py` | Dimensions and layout: the rack's hole pattern, the panel and its clips, the stand, the scoring tolerances |
| `build_assets.py` | Writes the textures and `assets/*.usda` (needs `pxr`, PIL and the DejaVu fonts) |
| `assets/` | The rack (with its installed units), the stand and the panel |
| `scenekit.py` | USD bodies and reset hooks for yam_arena task files |
| `demokit.py` | Helpers for the scripted demo |
| `demo.py` | The scripted full-score demo, and variants that fit the panel at another hole or never let go |

`scenekit.py` hooks `yam_arena.scene` and the embodiment's step and reset without modifying yam_arena, so the task
runs with the standard `yam_arena` embodiment and `task_score` scorer. Tested with yam-isaac-lab-arena at `bd62771`
(Isaac Lab-Arena 0.3.1).

All the assets are generated by `build_assets.py` from `geometry.py`; there are no third-party models. The labels
are drawn with the DejaVu Sans font (Bitstream Vera licence).

## Run a trial

From this folder, in a shell with the yam_arena environment active. New shells on the walkthrough's Brev instance
have it; elsewhere, `source <ARENA_DIR>/yam.env`.

```bash
inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_arena --policy dream \
  -E task=task.py -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 -E cam_width=320 -E cam_height=176 \
  --instruction "Fit the blanking panel at U6." \
  --scorer task_score --max-steps 2400 --store-frames --log-dir ~/yam-eval/rack-dream
```

`task_score` prints the trial's score: 1.0 on success, otherwise `score()` at the end of the episode.

- **Seeds:** there is one fixed layout and no randomisation (`object_jitter=0`), so the seed has no effect.
- **Runtime:** a full 2,400-step Dream-1 trial took 3 min 36 s on an RTX 5090 (mean inference latency 0.59 s),
  plus about a minute to start Isaac Sim. A successful trial ends earlier: the scripted demo stops after 288
  steps.

Dream-1 scored 0. In the first half minute both arms reached toward the stand, coming within about 1 cm of the
panel, then they went back near the ready pose. The panel never moved from its stand. The run
used one continuous Dream-1 session, with no errors or retries.

## Scripted demo

```bash
python demo.py task.py demo_out                  # full score: writes demo_*_cam.mp4, setup.png, result.json
python demo.py task.py hole18_out --hole 18      # clips in U6's top hole: straddles U6 and U7, 0
python demo.py task.py hold_out --keep-hold      # fitted at U6 but never let go: 0.5
```

1. **Pick it up.** Both arms come in from the front, 10 cm either side of the panel's middle, with the fingers open
   above and below it. They pinch its top and bottom edges and lift it out of the stand.
2. **Line it up.** They carry it to 3 cm in front of the posts with its clips level with hole 17, U6's middle hole.
   From the panel's measured pose they make up to four small corrections to its height, side and tilt.
3. **Fit it.** Both arms push it home together, open their fingers, back off and return to the ready pose.

The demo succeeds (1.0) in 288 steps (14.4 s): both clips in hole 17, both ends against the posts, and
the arms clear of the panel for 1 s. With `--hole 18` the panel ends up fitted one hole too high, straddling U6 and
U7, and scores 0. `--hole 14` fits it at U5 and scores 0. `--keep-hold` fits it at U6 but never lets go and scores 0.5.

## Scoring

Judged on the panel at the end of the trial, from a photo of the rack's front, a check of each end against its post,
and the video. A robot that drops the panel may pick it up and try again.

| Score | When |
|---|---|
| 1.0 (success) | The panel is at U6 with both ends pushed home, and no part of the robot has touched it for the last 1 s. This ends the trial |
| 0.5 | The panel is at U6 with both ends home, but the robot still touches it in the last 1 s; or one end is home and the other end is lined up with its hole, level with U6 and no more than 15 mm out from the post |
| 0 | Anything else, including doing nothing, the panel at another unit, one hole off (straddling two units), the two ends in different holes, and the panel off the rack |

- **Units** are counted from the bottom, U1 to U8. Each has three holes, so U6's are holes 16, 17 and 18 counted
  from the bottom. Its middle hole, 17, is where the clips go.
- **In the real world:**
  - **At U6.** Photograph the rack's front square on. A panel at U6 covers U6's three holes on each post and
    leaves hole 15 (U5's top hole) and hole 19 (U7's bottom hole) fully visible. Its top and bottom edges are within
    3 mm of U6's unit lines at both ends. A panel one hole off covers hole 15 or hole 19.
  - **Home.** At each end the gap between the panel's back and the post is at most 2 mm: a 2 mm shim does not go
    in.
  - **Let go.** The video shows no part of the robot touching the panel in the last 1 s.
- **In simulation:**
  - **At U6.** Both clips are within 3 mm of hole 17's height.
  - **In a hole.** A clip is in a hole when it is within 1.75 mm of the hole's centre, across and up. A clip hanging
    in its hole rests 0.75 mm low.
  - **Home.** An end is home when its clip is in its hole and the panel's back is no more than 2 mm in front of the
    post and no more than 1 mm behind its front face.
  - **Let go.** For the last 20 control steps, both arms stay at least 1 cm from the panel. The check uses
    yam_arena's collision hulls of every link, the gripper body and both fingers, at their simulated poses.

## Real-world build

All positions are in cm from the midpoint between the arm bases: x forward, y to the robot's left. Heights are above
the table top.

- **Rack.** A 19-inch two-post open-frame rack, 8U, dark grey steel, with EIA-310 square holes (9.5 mm).
  - Clamp it to the table with the front of its posts at x 42, centred on y 0.
  - Each post's face is 3.4 cm wide and the post is 4.6 cm deep. The holes are 1.6 cm deep with solid post behind
    them.
  - The posts' hole centres are 46.5 cm apart, the opening between the posts is 44.5 cm, and a 2.5 cm bar joins
    the posts at the top.
  - Each post stands on a 30 x 4 x 2 cm foot that runs from 10 cm in front of the post to 20 cm behind it. U1's
    bottom edge is 3 cm above the table.
  - Each post's face carries the unit numbers 1 to 8 beside its holes, in white about 1 cm tall, centred in each
    unit, with a white line at each unit boundary. If the rack has no numbers, add them with labels.
- **Installed units.** Each is screwed through its ears into the middle hole of each of its units. U4 to U7 are
  empty.
  - A 2U server at U1-U2, 28 cm deep.
  - A 1U switch at U3, 20 cm deep.
  - A 1U patch panel at U8, 6 cm deep.
  - Their fronts are 48.3 cm wide. Empty chassis or dummy fronts will do.
- **Blanking panel.** A 1U panel in black plastic, 48.3 x 4.3 cm and 6 mm thick, about 120 g.
  - A clip at each end: an 8 mm square peg 12 mm long at the panel's mid-height, chamfered to 5 mm at its tip.
  - The two pegs are 46.5 cm apart. They are a slip fit in the rack's 9.5 mm holes, so they go in a unit's middle
    hole and the panel hangs on them. There is no snap.
  - Cut it from 6 mm sheet with 3D-printed pegs glued on, or fit the pegs to a standard 1U plastic panel trimmed to
    43 mm.
- **Stand.** Two wooden uprights, 4 cm wide (y), 3.2 cm deep (x) and 16 cm tall, on a 41 x 5.2 x 1.2 cm base board.
  - Their centres are at y ±17.
  - Each has an 8 mm slot 2.65 cm deep.
  - The panel stands in the slots, face to the arms, its back at x 30.
- **Arms** start at the ready pose with open grippers.
- **Safety.** The rack is clamped and the panel weighs 120 g. Keep hands away from the posts while the arms push.

## Simulation fidelity

- Rigid bodies, 240 Hz physics, 20 Hz control.
- **Rack:** fixed.
  - Each post's front has real square holes: strips beside the holes and bridges between them, with gaps where the
    holes are and solid post behind.
  - A clip goes in only at a hole, and only 16 mm deep.
  - The installed units are solid.
- **Panel:** one rigid body: 120 g, friction 0.5 static and 0.4 dynamic.
  - Its clips are rigid pegs with 0.75 mm clearance each side in the holes.
  - A panel pushed home hangs on them, as the real one does.
- **Stand:** fixed, with the slots as gaps between blocks.
- **Monitor:** after each control step, task code:
  - reads the panel's pose;
  - finds the hole each clip is level with and whether it is in it;
  - checks whether each end is home;
  - checks how far the nearest part of either arm is from the panel.
- **Resets:** the reset puts the panel back in its stand. The rack and the stand are fixed.

## Reset between trials

Lift the panel off its clips and stand it back in the stand, face to the arms.
